The Tegelberghaus was built in the mid-19th century (usually given as 1852)
as a hunting lodge for King Maximilian II. His son Ludwig II took it over and
in 1866 wrote to Richard Wagner from the Tegelberg. During the First World War
it housed soldiers on patrol, and afterwards the German Alpine Club took it
over. Today it is run privately, together with the Tegelbergbahn, which has
reached the mountain station right next to the hut since 1968.

From the terrace you look down on Neuschwanstein, the Forggensee and the
Alpine foothills. Paragliders and hang gliders launch just below, and the
Branderschrofen (1879 m), the highest point of the Tegelberg, is about 45
minutes further along a partly secured ridge.

Most routes up gain about 900 m: the Gelbe-Wand-Steig (helmet and via ferrata
set required) or the Schutzengelweg in under three hours from the valley
station, the path past the Rohrkopfhütte, or the long Ahornreitweg from
Neuschwanstein through the Bleckenau. The Tegelbergsteig is the hard option,
a grade C via ferrata through the cliffs below the hut.

- **Open:** most of the year, including winter (access may be limited in
  heavy snow); usually closed from November to mid-December.
- **Hours:** from 9:00 to the last cable car, longer on some evenings; a hut
  evening runs on Fridays until 21:00.
- **Beds:** about 28 in four dormitories, renovated over winter 2025/26 and
  bookable through huetten-holiday.com.
- **Website:** [tegelberghaus.com](https://www.tegelberghaus.com/)
