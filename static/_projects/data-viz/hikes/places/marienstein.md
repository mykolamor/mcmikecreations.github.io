In 1835 Paul Deuringer, who owned the nearby Oberkammerloh estate, found cement
marl here. He built kilns and a mill in 1850/51 and named the quarry
Marienstein, after a girl in his family called Maria.

The tunnels dug for the marl cut into a seam of pitch coal (Pechkohle).
Deuringer got the right to mine it in 1852, and from 1902 the Marienstein mine
was run commercially, with shafts eventually reaching 564 m. From 1891 a
4.5 km freight line to Schaftlach carried the coal and cement out. The mine
closed on 31 March 1962 in the coal crisis, with about 300 miners still
working. Over 4 million tonnes of coal had come out since 1852. The railway
followed in 1965 and the cement works in 1998. The village grew out of the
companies' worker housing. Its church, Mariä Himmelfahrt, was built in 1927,
and a memorial recalls the mining.

![Kohlebergbaudenkmal memorial](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/places/marienstein/2026-10-04-00.jpg)

The Festenbach runs through the village on its way from the Nesselscheibe
ridge, past the Nickel-Alm, to the Mangfall. Bogs nearby are protected as part
of the EU habitat site "Attenloher Filzen und Mariensteiner Moore". Most of the old
railway bed can still be walked. In the village you can spot bridge abutments
where it crossed the Festenbach, and streets named Paul-Deuringer-Weg and
Karl-Lechner-Weg.
