The Kühzagl-Alm is the summer pasture of the Hans-Anderl-Hof, a farm run by
the Berghammer family in the hamlet of Kühzagl (820 m), part of Rottach-Egern
about 4 to 5 km south of the Tegernsee. The alm has been here a long time, but
the present building went up from 1999 and was finished in 2002, when the
owners also modernised how it is run. It has toilets, water and solar power.

The cows are driven up at the start of June and spend the summer here in the
care of a Sennerin. Only dairy cows are kept, so someone has to be on the alm
every day of the season. The milk goes down to the valley daily, to the
Naturkäserei TegernseerLand in Kreuth, and comes back as cheese, such as
Tegernseer Bergkäse and Wallberger Kräuterkäse, served on the alm's Brotzeit
boards.

From the Marienkapelle in Kühzagl a forest path follows small streams up to
the alm in about 40 minutes, and it rides well on a mountain bike. Above the
alm the gravel road climbs on to the ridge of the Rainerkopf (1463 m), the
Wasserspitz (1552 m) and the Bodenschneid (1668 m), and the Wallberg can be
seen from the terrace.

- **Open:** daily from the cattle drive in early June to about the end of
  September. Visitor reports differ on a rest day, so call ahead.
- **Food:** coffee, fresh milk, homemade cake, and Brotzeit with cheese or
  bacon and fresh bread.
- **Parking:** spaces in Kühzagl are few; most hikers use the large car park
  at the toll station.
