With my tooth feeling a bit better after the surgery, and having the
[Walchensee round trip](/hikes/2026-09-13-fischberg/) under my belt, I was
ready for a light hike to test my health. David was also up for a challenge
with his usual limitation of 800 m of ascent (on the map measurements), so
I offered a set of routes to him and he chose the Dürnbachwand (1260 m)
ridge, the last remaining ridge between Schliersee and Spitzingsee that I
had not yet hiked.

I've been on the neighboring ridges before: once on [Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/hikes/2025-08-31-brecherspitz/)
with a fever and several times on the opposing ridge, largely doing one
mountain at a time. [Rainerkopf (1463 m) with Serhii](/hikes/2025-12-23-rainerkopf/),
[Wasserspitz (1552 m) and Stümpfling (1506 m) with David](/hikes/2025-09-07-wasserspitz/),
and [Rinnerspitz (1611 m) and Bodenschneid (1669 m) with DAAD FK](/hikes/2025-11-01-bodenschneid/).

After a chill train ride to Neuhaus, we left the station and immediately
started the ascent. It's crazy how close the hill is from the settlement.
I have to note though, that the trail marking the start of the route is a bit
hidden, stemming off of the [Bockerlbahn-Weg trail](/hikes/2024-10-03-bockerlbahnweg/)
I checked out in 2024.

![View from Anklspitz (1112 m) towards Wendelstein (1838 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-00.jpg)

Since the settlement at the station is at roughly 800 meters from sea lavel,
the ascent to the ridge and the first peak Anklspitz (1112 m) is quite
steep. The trail is not that well maintained, but still much better than my
recent hikes, like the one to [Großer Laber (1466 m)](/hikes/2026-09-06-grosser_laber/)
I took with David at the start of the month. The last bit of the ascent to
the cross itself is a bit steep, but nothing too challenging. The view from
the top is nice though, with [Wendelstein (1838 m)](/hikes/2025-06-10-wendelstein/)
in the distance.

![View towards Dürnbachwand (1260 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-01.jpg)

From this point onwards, the actual ridge trail starts. It's quite steep and
narrow, going through a lot of wonky sections with gravel, dust, and wobbly
rocks. In dry weather it's an amazing feeling to ascend so quickly while
looking at the views ahead through the narrow ridge. In other conditions, I
would recommend staying away from it. The next peak on the way,
Dürnbachwand (1260 m), is not that far away, so we ended up there in no time.

![View from Dürnbachwand (1260 m) towards Schliersee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-03.jpg)
![View at Schlierseespitz (1279 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-02.jpg)

Schlierseespitz (1279 m), the peak after Dürnbachwand (1260 m), is just a few
minutes away, offering the same views and no markings. We walked past it and
continued towards an unnamed 1445-meter peak on the "difficult alpine hiking"
trail.

![Climbing tree roots on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-04.jpg)
![Ascending the unnamed cliff to the 1445 meter peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-05.jpg)

The road really does get difficult there. On occasions, you have to climb up
steep (but walkable) sections with tree roots and rocks, making for an
interesting traverse. Other times, you have to climb up cliffs with no cables,
handholds, or other aids. It's not that difficult though, and the whole time
you walk on the south-eastern side looking at Brecherspitz (1685 m) and
other mountains in the distance.

![Mykola mid-climbing one of the cliffs](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-06.jpg)
![Mykola on the unnamed 1445-meter cliff peak near Schliersee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-07.jpg)

Once we got over the hurdle and reached the peak, the road became much easier.
We still had to climb upwards, but the trail was much more walkable and we
could enjoy the views while catching up on our lives. The next peak, an
unnamed 1524-meter flat summit, was still quite some distance away, so we got
deep into the conversation and stopped paying close attention to the trail.

![View from the ridge towards Brecherspitz (1685 m) and Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-08.jpg)
![Muchroom on near a barbed fence stepover](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-09.jpg)

In-between our discussions, I was trying to give David some information about
the surrounding mountains, especially the ones I visited, like [Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/hikes/2024-08-31-aiplspitz/).
Unfortunately, this info fell on deaf ears, as he wasn't trying to soak in any
of the things I was saying. Around that point we came to a barbed fence that we
had to step over using a very delicate unstable stepover. I was worried to tear
my trousers for the third time, but I mostly got over it without issues.

![Ascent from Ankel-Alm (1311 m) towards Freudenreichkapelle (1587.2 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-10.jpg)
![View towards Freudenreichkapelle (1587.2 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-11.jpg)

From there we descended a bit to a saddle leading to Ankel-Alm (1311 m) and
joined the normal flow of hikers going up to Freudenreichkapelle (1587.2 m).
The trail was a bit crowded, with people slipping while descending, but we
stayed to the side and let them pass.

![View from Freudenreichkapelle (1587.2 m) towards Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-12.jpg)
![View from Birkenstein towards Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-13.jpg)

From there, the trail splits into two equally easy sections. One of them goes
down to Freudenreich-Alm (1262 m), while the other goes up to
Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m). I gave David the choice and warned him that
I visited that peak already. He was interested in trying out
Brecherspitz (1685 m) itself and descending to Obere Firstalm (1369 m), so
we chose that route, going past the Birkenstein shrine on the way.

![View from Birkenstein back at the Dürnbachwand ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-14.jpg)
![The shrine of Birkenstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-15.jpg)

From there the route was easy. We quickly walked past Birkenstein, let a bunch
of hikers pass us, talked about being hungry for a while, and eventually
reached Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m). It was overcrowded, with people
laying on the grass, on the antenna platform, and on the rocks. Others were
passing by on the trail, going both directions.

![View from Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m) towards Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-16.jpg)
![View from Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m) onto Dürnbachwand (1260 m) and its ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-17.jpg)

Since the ridge, the road down is very comfortable if you don't have a fever.
The route is well-kept given the traffic, the trail is wide and has steps on
slightly difficult or steep sections, there are people walking dogs there.
At this point, we got back into our conversations and stopped observing the
terrain again, although I did point out the huts and the Stümpfling (1506 m)
summit to David, since he visited it in the past.

![View from the trail towards Stümpfling (1506 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-18.jpg)

From there the trips was the same old. We went to Obere Firstalm (1369 m),
checked out the menu and the prices, and listened to local musicians playing
there. David wanted a different dish not offered on the menu, so he asked us
to descend to Untere Firstalm and check the menu there.

![The local musicians at Obere Firstalm (1369 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-20.jpg)
![The road between Obere Firstalm (1369 m) and Untere Firstalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-21.jpg)

The menu at the bottom hut was also not to his liking, so we made the decision
to head down to Spitzingsee and look for food there. I was really unlucky with
booking tables there in the past and warned David that the story might repeat.
Thankfully, when we came down to the lake, most of the restaurants had empty
tables and David was able to get exactly the meal he wanted.

![The view from Spitzingsee towards Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/duernbachwand/2026-09-20-22.jpg)

To summarize the hike, I would highly suggest it to experienced hikers not
afraid of open ridges, steep ascents, and heights in general. The views are
amazing, and arguably better than the neighboring ridges. For everyone else, I
would suggest taking the simplified version from Ankel-Alm (1311 m) across
Freudenreichkapelle (1587.2 m) and over to Obere Firstalm (1369 m). It's
much more accessible to hikers of all skill levels, and offers the majority
of the views you get on the ridge. You miss out on the cliffs though!
