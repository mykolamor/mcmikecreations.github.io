For my reunion with Serhii and Pavlo from Ukraine (and Sviatoslav who didn't
join us on this hike), I was designated as the hike planner. I wanted to find
a peak that would challenge us a bit, but not be too difficult. After looking
at various options around Trento, where we stayed for 2 nights, I settled on
the Molveno area, which has several peaks accessible from the town itself.

![Trip up the cable car](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-00.jpg)

I spend several evenings exploring neighboring ridges on Komoot, and finally
settled on Cima Sophia (2360 m) and Croz dell'Altissimo (2339 m) from
[this page](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/41225363). Since the others didn't
have that much recent hiking experience, I decided to skip the walk from
Molveno upwards, and suggested taking the cable cars.

![An owl carved out of wood](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-01.jpg)

We set off early in the cold winter morning, with everything covered in frost,
just like during my previous hike to [Rainerkopf (1463 m)](/hikes/2025-12-23-rainerkopf/).
We paid for the two-way trip on both cable cars, and ascended to
Rifugio La Montanara (1575 m).

![View towards Cima Molveno (2831 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-02.jpg)

A small surprise for us was that nobody except the person at the ticket booth
could speak any English. Luckily, we managed with hand gestures like true
Italians, and found the way up without issues.

There, we took some group photos for other tourists and took some ourselves,
and headed to the start of the hike near the giant bear statue
[Orso del Pradel](https://www.lecosmopolite.it/orso-del-pradel-di-marco-martalar-a-molveno/).

As soon as we started climbing up, we got hot under the direct sun and had to
take off our underpants, jackets, mittens, and just about anything that
was not strictly necessary. The trail didn't have any snow at that point,
there was no wind, and the views were great, so we just enjoyed the walk.

![View towards Piz Galin (2442 m) from the crossroads](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-03.jpg)
![View towards Campanile Basso (2883 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-04.jpg)

When we hit the first signpost at a [crossroads](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/4315978385),
we put on our gaiters and prepared for the snow and ice ahead. The trail
quickly got worse, with the ground being covered in a layer of smooth ice. We
started slipping and sliding, but kept going up, and soon reached
Palon de Tovre (1913 m).

![Palon de Tovre (1913 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-05.jpg)
![View towards Lake Molveno](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-06.jpg)

I didn't know it at that point, but this was the most scenic spot of the entire
hike. The views of the Dolomites were great, the lake in the distance was
fantastic, and the sun was shining bright. We took a lot of photos there,
and enjoyed the moment.

![A split between the summits](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-07.jpg)

As we kept going, I started noticing that our pace was way behind schedule,
despite all the precautions we took by waking up early. The terrain was getting
steeper, and the snow was deeper. I was going first, breaking the trail, and
it was quite exhausting. The others were following me, but I could see that
they were struggling, especially without waterproof clothing.

![Pavlo and Serhii on the lower plateau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-08.jpg)

After a long climb up surrounded by treetops covered in snow, we finally
reached a plateau near the crossroads leading to Pizzo Gallino (2442 m), and
took a break. We noticed deep holes in the snow. If you fall into one, it's
very easy to get stuck and get injured, so we had to be very careful.

![View towards Piz Galin (2442 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-09.jpg)

From there, we started heading up, but the trail became even worse. We came
across a lone trail runner going down from Cima dei Lasteri (2457 m), who
didn't manage to reach the summit, and warned us to be careful and consider
turning back. The snow was very deep, and had tons of gaps and holes between
rocks, so it was quite risky.

![The second plateau with a view towards Croz dell'Altissimo (2339 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-10.jpg)

We took notice of his advice and continued our ascend carefully. After some
steep sections, where we had to use our hands to climb up, we finally reached
another small plateau, where Serhii and Pavlo decided to stop. They stayed
there for around 10 minutes, took some photos, and then headed back down.

As I was leaving them, I saw them completely drenched in cold snow water,
freezing and being tired. Going back was definitely the right choice for them.
They stopped around [46.173356, 10.944032](https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?lat=46.173356&lon=10.944032&zoom=19#map=19/46.173356/10.944034).

![Climb towards Cima Sophia (2360 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-11.jpg)

I continued my way up alone, breaking the trail through deep snow. The wind
picked up, and I had to put on my jacket and mittens again. The summit was
still quite far, and I was getting tired, but I kept going.

Several times along the way, I considered turning back as well, especially when
slipped and fell between the rocks a couple of times. My fractured finger from
the [Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m) hike](/hikes/2025-11-23-vordere_kesselschneid/)
hasn't healed yet, so I had to be extra careful not to hurt it again.

The deep snow covering any and all marks and signposts didn't help either. I had to
constantly check the terrain to look for a reasonable path up. What made it worse,
there was no official path to the summit on the map, so I had to improvise.

![The peak of Cima Sophia (2360 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-12.jpg)

Finally, after what felt like an eternity, I reached the summit of Cima Sophia
(2360 m). The views were breathtaking, with the Dolomites all around me,
but the lake was hidden behind Croz dell'Altissimo (2339 m). I took a bunch of
photos, signed the summit book, and started heading back down. There was no time
to waste, as the sun was already starting to set and I started to freeze too.

![View from Cima Sophia (2360 m) towards Croz dell'Altissimo (2339 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-13.jpg)

It took me around 30 minutes from the place I left Serhii and Pavlo to reach
the summit, but the descent was much faster. I managed to backtrack my steps,
avoiding the deep snow holes, and reached the same spot in around
10-15 minutes.

At that point, I didn't know the others decided to leave, but it was clear from
the footsteps they left in the snow. I felt like a true mountaineer, being able to
reach the summit and come back safely; and also like a detective, looking at the
snow patterns and footsteps to figure out what happened.

![Sunset near Molveno](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-14.jpg)

I didn't know when the others left, so I shouted quite a few times on the top of
my lungs, but there was no response. Considering my friends are not dumb, I
correctly assumed they already went down, so I kept going.

When I finally descended back to the lower plateau, I finally saw them standing
on the summit of Palon de Tovre (1913 m), looking around for me, but I was too
tired to shout again. I just got happy that they are safe and sound, so I
hurried to catch up with them.

![A look back at the peaks](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-15.jpg)

The rest of the descent was getting more and more easy, as the snow was getting
shallower, and the trail was already broken by our footsteps. I kept pushing,
constantly being surprised by how far ahead the others were. Although I made a
few stops to take photos of the sunset, I was running practically the entire
way down.

![A glimpse at the lake from the hillside](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-16.jpg)

Despite that, I finally caught up with them near the bear statue, right before
the cable car statue. According to Google Maps, the cable car operation closes
at 16:15, and the time we reached it was 16:03. To our surprise, the cable car
was already shut down for the day, and there was nobody around.

![The famous bear statue](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-17.jpg)

I was really salty for the missed opportunity and wasted money, and I clearly
saw others, especially Pavlo, being exhausted. We had to walk down the entire
way to Molveno, with no transport we could take. The only saving grace was that
the trail down was clear of snow apart from the ski route, so we could walk
down without issues and slipping.

![Going down along the cable car](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-18.jpg)

We reached the intermediate cable car station soon after, with the squirrel
statue and the hotel there. I was disappointed to find out the hotel doesn't
have a souvenir shop, only post cards, so I took one for each of us, and
returned outside to see a snow plow truck clearing the ski route.

![Snow plow truck](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-19.jpg)

There was no road and no cars going down, so we had to keep going. There was
a trail leading down along the cable car poles, which I saw on the way up,
so we chose that one. A few hikers going up met us near the top, telling us
it took them around 20 minutes from the bottom to reach the station, so we
were hopeful and motivated to reach Molveno before the last bus back at 18:40. 
As a side note, I want to give my regards to the public transport system of
the area. The bus operates 3 times per day, so if you miss it, you're stuck.

![Molveno in the evening](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/cima_sophia/2025-12-29-20.jpg)

We walked down slowly, as the guys were exhausted and couldn't walk fast
without hurting their knees. When we reached the town, we were happy to make
it to safety on time, but it was clear that we would get sick after such
an adventure. As a few days have passed since the hike, I can confirm that
indeed happened, with all of us catching a cold and having to rest for a week.

In the end, with enough time and a car, this hike would be great even in
winter. I'm sure it's even better in summer, although I'd recommend going to
Croz dell'Altissimo (2339 m) instead, as the views of the lake are better from
there. Otherwise, the hike is definitely manageable, and the cable cars make it
accessible to almost anyone.
