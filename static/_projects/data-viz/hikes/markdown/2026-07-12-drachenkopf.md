During the [outing to Drachensee](/hikes/2026-06-21-drachensee/)
I've been to recently, the original plan was to gather a small group with
hikers from Nuremberg and explore the lakes on a chill social hike. That time
the trip fell through so Stanislav joined my by himself. The original
participants reached out to him again to organize another trip there, and he
reluctantly agreed.

![The slope near the beginning of the Hoher Gang via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-00.jpg)

Stanislav warned me that the hike will start very late due to transport issues.
I didn't see the point in repeating the same route trail-for-trail, so I looked
in the area to expand my route. Surprisingly, most of the via ferrata routes
had alternatives that required no equipment, so I chose Drachenkopf (2303 m) as
the easies peak. Then, I could meet the other hikers on the way down and
descend together with them.

The first section of the hike was the same as last time, with me running across
Hoher Gang. I did the whole section in around an hour, way faster than before,
and without resting at the nice picnic area. This time I recorded the via
ferrata section, but even back then I already knew blurring all the faces would
take ages.

![Looking at Schneefernerkopf (2874 m) from the top of the Hoher Gang via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-01.jpg)
![The trail towards Seebensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-02.jpg)

Once I got to the valley near Seebensee, I started feeling quite tired. Knowing
I still have a lot of time left, I got a stupid idea to visit multiple summits
on this hike, so I rushed further. Soon after, passing the first lake and
reaching the slope towards the next, I got my first cramp. I stopped in time
and avoided the biggest harm from it, but where one comes, others follow.

![Seebensee and Westliche Griesspitze (2741 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-03.jpg)
![Ducks at Seebensee with Östliche Wetterspitze (2709 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-04.jpg)

The route to Drachensee wasn't that big of a deal. It takes longer than you'd
assume, but the route is nice and easy, and there's tons of people around at
any time of the day. The lake itself looked nice as usual, but I had no time
to rest, and I didn't rest for long.

![Cows at Seebensee with Drachenkopf (2303 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-05.jpg)
![Looking down from Drachensee to Seebensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-06.jpg)

It took me a bit to figure out where to go from the hut towards
Drachenkopf (2303 m), but eventually I found the right trail and set sail.
The start of this section is a nice dusty trail uphill onto the valley
between Drachenkopf (2303 m) and Sonnenspitze (2416 m). I've met a group of
young fellas hiking up and was tagging behind them, trying to take it easy
because of the cramp. They had a really weird pace, running up and taking
long breaks, so I just overtook them and kept going, slow and steady.

![The dusty trail towards Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-07.jpg)

Around half-way up the valley I looked back and saw the last glimpse of
Seebensee, with numerous hiking groups passing by it. It was a nice vista,
especially with Schneefernerkopf (2874 m) and Zugspitze (2962 m) in the
background. Soon after, they disappeared behind the rocky slopes and
neighboring peaks, and I started the real ascent.

![Looking at Sonnenspitze (2416 m) from the trail towards Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-08.jpg)

I also got a good look at Vorderer Tajakopf (2450 m) and
Hinterer Tajakopf (2407 m), hoping to visit one of them after my current goal.
The rest of the mountains in the area, like Schartenkopf (2332 m) or
Hinterer Drachenkopf (2410 m) all require rock climbing, preferably with a
partner and a rope, so they were a no-go for me.

![The cliffside climb to the top of Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-09.jpg)

When I reached the bottom of Drachenkopf (2303 m), I was mortified. I was told
by Stanislav a while back that the route to the top is a T4 hike, but I wasn't
expecting to see an actual cliff without any safety cables, especially in such
a popular spot. The other guys hiking up with me decided to turn back and not
pursue the summit. I, on the other hand, looked closely and saw a lot of people
at the top, all without any safety equipment. This reassured me and I went on
climbing.

![Mountain flowers on the way to Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-10.jpg)

The first section was quite easy, with a visible trail leading up. I went
through it quite fast, knowing I'll be able to do the same on the way down.
I also saw a bunch of flowers growing in cracks between the rocks, making for
a nicely decorated trail to follow. Then, the sections got steeper, the rocks
got more loose. Even the better-kept sections with an actual road were steep
and covered in tiny rocks, making everyone slip on the way down. I knew I'd
take a long time to go down from there. Together with my cramp issues, I made
a decision to skip other peaks for this day and focus on one and only
Drachenkopf (2303 m).

![The cross on the top of Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-11.jpg)

The climb lasted around 30 minutes, and eventually I saw the cross and the
hikers taking photos there. Despite the sun blazing from above, it was cool
and windy enough there to enjoy our stay. I stayed in the queue to take a
photo with the cross for a bit, texted some people (the summit was the only
place with internet connectivity in the area), and started going down.

![View from Drachenkopf (2303 m) towards Seebensee, Schneefernerkopf (2874 m) and Zugspitze (2962 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-12.jpg)

The people hanging around on the top decided to descend roughly at the same
time, so I took my time taking photos of the surroundings. Once I started
descending, my worries came to life. The route down was taking a long time,
I kept taking wrongs paths down, climbing back up, slipping on wobbly rocks.

![The way down from Drachenkopf (2303 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-13.jpg)

A few people passed by, a few were struggling like me, but eventually we all
arrived at the bottom. There, I met a fresh group of hikers trying to go up.
An older guy climbing down behind me tried giving them advice, but the
youngsters were visibly scared of the climb, so both of us advised them to
turn back. Some of them did, the others continued to the top.

![The gravel-covered trail down from the Drachenkopf (2303 m) slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-14.jpg)

Meanwhile, I kept going down and very soon after got my second cramp. I sat
down and started applying pressure to my leg, looking at others slowly catching
up with me. Eventually I felt good enough to continue, and started walking
down the trail back to the hut. On the way there, I met a group of mixed
russian-Ukrainian hikers from "Horod" (garden in English). The
Ukrainian-speaking people told me they knew Stanislav, but haven't seen him
since the train station, so I said my goodbyes and kept going down.

![The view at Drachensee from the hut](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-15.jpg)

I reached the hut at 14:00 and checked my phone. I estimated the rest of the
group should reach it by 15:00, so I got a non-alcoholic drink and enjoyed the
view. After I was done, I started going down at a slow pace.

![Drinking my Schiwasser at Drachensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-16.jpg)

When I was about to reach Seebensee, on one of the numerous winding trails
between the lakes, I came face to face with Stanislav. After a quick greeting,
he asked me to go back up to Drachensee with them for some group photos, so
I did, squeezing the last drops of power from my body.

We got there, took our photos and started heading down immediately after, as
the last bus from the route was at 17:45. Coming down later was not an option,
since the others needed to catch a connection all the way to Nürnberg. As we
were practically running down, I was told that there was a mother with her
12-year-old son waiting for us at Seebensee. They took the same Hoher Gang via
ferrata route that I did and were too tired to run to the upper lake and back.

![The Seebensee panorama](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-17.jpg)

Once we reached the lake, though, they were missing. We had no cellular
connectivity and were tired ourselves, so everyone started worrying. I
suggested walking around the area and looking for them myself, while others
would walk down. As we departed, we caught some internet and called them.
Turns out, they were worried about missing the bus and headed down themselves
without any map or equipment.

![Mykola and Stanislav going down from Seebensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/drachenkopf/2026-07-12-18.jpg)

I ran forward in front of our group to find them and direct them. At that
point, it was already quite late, and we had to take the Immensteig via ferrata
to make it in time for the last bus. Although I didn't know them personally, I
followed the description others gave me and found them. After a small greeting,
I suggested to them that we should start heading down, as they walked a bit
slower than others.

When we reached the Immensteig via ferrata, we got another surprise. This time,
by the quality of the route. The cable was barely fixed or too long in places,
some of the poles were not cemented into the rock and would fall out when we
grabbed onto the attached rope. Some steep sections were not secured at all,
while others were blocked by fallen trees that nobody cleaned up. The woman
was angry and upset the whole time, worried about her son and getting more and
more exhausted as time went on. Still, both of them reached the bottom of the
cliff, just as the rest of the group.

At this point we still had plenty of time before the bus, so we just took our
time, came down, washed up, bought some drinks (my water ran out all the way
back at Drachensee), and traveled home.

![The 360° climb of Hohen Gang](/404/)

All in all, it's an amazing hike, despite some rough sections on the via
ferratas and near the peaks. I would highly recommend it to everyone in the
area, but make sure to reserve plenty of time for the route, as it can be very
unpredictable.

P.S. I was eating Moser Roth 90% dark chocolate (not sponsored) while writing
this post and I can highly recommend it.
