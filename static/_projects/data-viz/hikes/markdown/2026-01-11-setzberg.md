Hi! My name is Mykola and I like getting myself into situations that normal
people would avoid. This time I decided to hike in the mountains after heavy
snowfall for the last few days. The destination was Setzberg (1706 m) - quite a
popular peak in the Tegernsee area. Since I've explored [Wallberg (1722 m)](/404/)
on the same ridge and had Risserkogel (1826 m) on my list for a while, I
decided to continue covering the ridge and hike from Kreuth to Rottach-Egern
via Setzberg (1706 m). Although I gathered some people to join me, in the end,
they all bailed out, so I had to do it solo.

![The Weißach river](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-00.jpg)

I took one of the earlier trains to Tegernsee and started the hike from Kreuth
around 8:30. It was a bit concerning that I was the only passenger in the bus,
but with many people going skiing in the area, I already presumed that I won't
see as many hikers as usual.

![The view towards Blauberge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-01.jpg)

The trail started with a gentle ascent through the forest. The snow was quite
deep, up to my knees in most places, but the trail was well-marked and easy to
follow. I got a glimpse of the [Blauberge ridge](/hikes/2025-06-01-blauberge/),
which I had hiked a few months ago.

![The gravel road up the slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-02.jpg)
![The view towards Hirschberg (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-03.jpg)

At some point, I reached a wider road leading up, marked as Setzbergweg on the
map. The snow was fully cleared here, making the section much easier to hike.
Although the area is heavily forested, there were occasional gaps in the trees
that allowed me to see Leonhardstein (1452 m) and [Buchstein (1701 m)](/hikes/2025-10-05-buchstein_only/),
which I visited before. Little did I know that it would be the last time I
would see any views that day. I also saw [Hirschberg (1670 m)](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/),
which I had visited with DAAD in the past.

![The view towards Leonhardstein (1452 m) and Buchstein (1701 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-04.jpg)

After reaching some huts along the way, the road stopped being cleared, and I
had to trudge through deep snow again. It was knee- to waist-deep and quite
tiring. Thankfully, I was still following the gravel road, so navigation was
not an issue.

![The way up without footsteps](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-05.jpg)

After a while, the road ended, and I had to continue on a narrow trail that led
up to the summit. I'm saying the trail was narrow, but in reality, I had no way
of knowing where it was, as the snow completely covered it. I just kept going
upwards, trying to follow my GPS route. At some points, the slope was quite
steep and filled with small rocky drops, which made the ascent tricky. I could
use my hands, of course, but that would make me freeze faster in the -10°C
weather.

Still, I realized that my goal was to reach the saddle, the highest elevation
point going up from where I was. Thus, I kept pushing myself upwards, trying
to remain roughly on the right path. At one point, I noticed my phone missing
from my pocket. I had to stop and use the "Find my device" feature from my
smartwatch to locate it. Thankfully, it was just a few meters back where I had
taken off my gloves to check the GPS route. I quickly retrieved it and put it
back in my pocket, hoping it wouldn't have suffered any damage from the cold
and moist snow. You can see the dip in speed on my GPS track where the phone
was missing on my [Strava activity](https://www.strava.com/activities/17013207776).

![The hut at the saddle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-06.jpg)
![The view from the hut towards Risserkogel (1826 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-07.jpg)

After some more climbing, I finally reached the saddle and saw the tiny shelter
with a map of the local area. I was extremely happy to be there, as it meant
that the hardest part of the hike was over. From there, it was just a short
climb to the summit of Setzberg (1706 m) and a descent to Rottach-Egern.
Unfortunately, I severely underestimated this climb.

![The view along the Alpenlehrpfad](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-08.jpg)
![The ridge section](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-09.jpg)

The ridge path, referred to as the Alpine Nature Trail ([Alpenlehrpfad](https://www.baysf.de/index.php/alpenlehrpfad/))
on the map, was covered in waist- to chest-deep snow because of the wind and
the exposed sections. Ever since Kreuth I had not seen a single footprint on
the trail, so I had to break the trail myself. Each step was a struggle, and
I had to take frequent breaks to catch my breath. The wind was also quite
strong, making the conditions even more challenging.

![The opening near Setzberg (1706 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-10.jpg)
![The view from Setzberg (1706 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-11.jpg)

After what felt like an eternity, I finally reached the opening near the summit
of Setzberg (1706 m). It felt like a small victory, the last push before the
peak. As I kept going, I saw ski tracks in the snow, indicating that I wasn't
the only one who had braved the conditions that day.

![Cross-country ski enjoyers on Setzberg (1706 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-12.jpg)

Finally, I saw 3 new skiiers making their way down a few meters ahead of me,
which made me feel relieved. I wasn't alone in this adventure! I quickly made 
my way to the top, seing 4 more people and a dog resting there. I asked them
about the conditions on the way down to Wallberghaus, and they confirmed that
in some places, the snow was still quite deep, but it was manageable. They
also advised me to follow the ski tracks, as they would lead me down more
easily.

![The way down from the summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-13.jpg)

I thanked them, took some photos of the nearby peaks covered in clouds, and
started my descent. They were right - the snow was waist-deep, but following
the ski tracks made it much easier. I was able to stand on the packed snow in
some spots, which gave me a much-needed break from the deep snow. Tons of
cross-country skiers went by me on their way to the top, which was quite
refreshing after being alone for the whole hike so far.

![Setzberg (1706 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-14.jpg)

Eventually, I reached the open side of the mountain, exposed to the strong
winter winds. Although it wasn't pleasant, the wind helped to blow away most of
the snow, making the descent a piece of cake. I quickly made my way down to
Wallberghaus, considering to stop for a hot drink, but I was eager to finish
the hike.

![Rottach-Egern panorama](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-15.jpg)
![Going through Rottach-Egern](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-16.jpg)

I joined a random group of hikers with dogs going down from Wallberg (1722 m)
back to Tegernsee, following the well-trodden trail. The snow was much
shallower and packed by tons of boots, skis and sledges. After an uneventful
descent, I reached the bottom station of the cable car and walked towards a
nice cafe in Rottach-Egern to warm up and have some food.

![Much deserved snack after the hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/setzberg/2026-01-11-17.jpg)

Overall, I can see this hike being really popular and enjoyable in normal
conditions, with great views from the summit and along the ridge. It's listed
as T2 on [hoehenrausch.de](https://www.hoehenrausch.de/berge/setzberg/).
However, the heavy snowfall made it quite challenging, and I had to rely on my
navigation skills and physical endurance to complete the hike. If you plan to
do this hike in winter conditions, make sure to check the weather forecast,
bring appropriate gear, and be prepared for deep snow and strong winds.
