After my [adventure on Kasprowy Wierch (1987 m)](/hikes/2026-11-14-kasprowy_wierch/)
the day before, I was excited to do another hike on the next day. The initial
plan of taking a light route first, then a difficult route afterwards fell apart
due to our carelessness going from summit to summit. We were quite tired,
Sviatoslav left our vacation early to prepare for the next working week, the
weather was cold and windy, so we had to downscale.

Meet Morskie Oko (1395 m), the most popular location around Zakopane, and one of
the most crowded mountain vacation spots in Poland. Pavlo, who had visited it
before, warned me about the sheer size of the crowds taking the mostly horizontal
asphalt road. After a bit of thought, we decided to instead take a scenic route
up Dolina Roztoki, then walk around the ridges and peaks of nearby mountains to
descend towards the lake.

When we arrived at the parking lot at the entrance to the Tatra National Park,
we found out that the last bus back left before 18:00 at this time of the year.
It was already past 09:00 and the planned route should have taken around
8–9 hours, leaving us no wiggle room to rest, have a snack in the hut, or enjoy
the scenery.

![The Roztoka river at the entrance to Dolina Roztoki](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-01.jpg)

We contemplated our route for quite a bit while walking the asphalt road
section, but ultimately decided to go through with the plan. We got through the
asphalt part quite fast, took the steeper walk up a forest road into the valley,
and soon after we were well ahead of schedule. The scenery looked quite nice,
but the biggest mountains were still hidden behind trees and hills.

![View from the valley towards Buczynowa Strażnica (2242 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-02.jpg)
![Siklawa waterfall from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-03.jpg)

In the valley, we followed the trail south-west and arrived at the Siklawa
waterfall. Pavlo told me that in summer it has much more water, so I guess I'll
have to revisit this place again to check it out for myself. He also said that
the shallow incline of the first few sections was now over, and we had two big
climbs ahead of us. One near the waterfall, and one across the ridge.

![Iced-over road near Siklawa](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-04.jpg)
![View from Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m) back at the valley](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-06.jpg)

From the waterfall, we started ascending the steep trail to the
Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m). The first few meters were fine, but after that came
a long iced-over section of the trail which was impossible to pass without
proper crampons. The splashing water and breeze from the waterfall made a thick
slippery layer. I started going up, but then I heard a terrible screech, looked
below me, and saw the spikes slowly sliding down the ice. My only choice was to
hurry up and climb ASAP until I reached the top.

![View of the Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-07.jpg)
![Wielki Wołoszyn (2155 m) from Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-08.jpg)

Once I got over the dangerous segment, the rest of the way was relatively easy.
We walked past some other hikers and came up to the first lake of the day. It
was beautiful, and Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m) (as per the name) was
surprisingly big. There was already a group of tourists there taking photos,
including an Australian hiker asking about directions. We told him which trail
to take, he took some more time to enjoy the view, then took the opposite route
deeper into the mountains. We managed to stop and redirect him though.

![Pine bushes on the beach of Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-09.jpg)
![A small hut near Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-11.jpg)

At that moment, we were also approached by a Slovakian lady asking for
directions. We started speaking to her in English while she responded in her
native language. To our confusion, she said: "you're Slavic, I'm Slavic, we
understand". And we did, in fact, understand. We helped her out with her navigation
and started our walk to the second lake, Przedni Staw Polski (1668 m).

![A small mountain beach of Wielki Staw Polski (1665 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-12.jpg)
![View from Przedni Staw Polski (1668 m) towards Schronisko PTTK w Dolinie Pięciu Stawów Polskich (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-13.jpg)

The trail between the lakes was super nice, well-marked and flat. Once we got
closer to the hut, a strong wind picked up and we realized we'd have to take a
break in the hut, having some hot tea along the way. While in the hut, we
checked the route on the map and realized we were way ahead of schedule. There
was no point in rushing, but we could keep the pace up and stay longer in the
hut at Morskie Oko (1395 m). But before that, we had the second steep ascent to
climb.

![View of the trail towards Morskie Oko (1395 m) from Przedni Staw Polski (1668 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-14.jpg)
![Chamois or mountain goats near the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-15.jpg)

I was feeling quite tired from the Kasprowy Wierch (1987 m) hike, dreading the
steep trail ahead. Looking at it from below made the trek ahead quite painful to
attempt. When I started walking, however, it turned out much better. The trail
was again nice and well-maintained. No ice on the road, no random wobbly rocks,
no big groups of hikers slowing us down (although we walked past quite a few
groups overall). We even got to see a group of some mountain goats on the slope.

![View from the trail back at the lakes in Dolina Pięciu Stawów Polskich](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-18.jpg)

The way up was not that tiring, and the break we took in the hut allowed us to
walk to the top without much trouble. Although this was still a decent hike, it
was, as we planned, much easier than the Kasprowy Wierch (1987 m) trip. We then
looked across the valley towards Buczynowa Strażnica (2242 m) and saw a small
trail between the pines weaving its way up to a saddle on the ridge. I was
completely taken aback by that, especially the section of that trail crossing
some water streams on a steep slope. I assume this trail is only accessible in
summer, when there is no snow and the streams dry up.

![View from Świstowa Czuba (1763 m) towards Buczynowa Strażnica (2242 m), with the small trail going across the slope and up to the ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-16.jpg)

From there we kept walking up the slope and soon came to Kępa (1683 m), one of the
highest points of the hike. From there, we only had to go down towards
Morskie Oko (1395 m). We double-checked the time and realized we were still far
ahead of schedule; the navigation app showed us arriving back at
Palenica Białczańska at around 16:00. We decided to keep going and take a break
at the Schronisko PTTK Morskie Oko hut.

![A look from Kępa (1683 m) towards Morskie Oko (1395 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-20.jpg)

The trail down was unexpectedly challenging. Pavlo mentioned the same trail when
talking about his previous trip here. He said he was taking it during an
unexpected rainfall that made all the stones on the trail slippery. It was
mid-summer and tons of hikers were taking this route, so immediately the
movement slowed down to a crawl as everyone was falling down and trying to get
back up without knocking others down with them. This time, the stones were yet
again slippery, but we had snow and shoe spikes to help us out, so it worked out
quite well.

![Pavlo making his way towards Morskie Oko (1395 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-21.jpg)

Once we got down and walked over to the lake, I saw Rysy (2499 m) for the first
time. The mountains were marvelous, towering over the lakes like a cold stone
wall. There was an insanely popular trail to the top across the lakes and up the
slope, but I had trouble finding it after snowfall.

![View from Morskie Oko (1395 m) towards Rysy (2499 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-22.jpg)

P.S. As I was writing this post, I spent the entire evening looking at pictures
of Rysy (2499 m) and Wysoka (2259 m). Truly incredible mountains, although some
sections of the trails leading there are quite sketchy, and the choice to use
metal chains instead of proper cables with carabiner support baffles me.

I took a deeper look into the chains question and found some interesting
information. Most metal chains there were first installed between 1890 and 1920,
when that was the de facto standard way of securing routes in the mountains. The
philosophy at the time was to give people handrails to assist them while
climbing, not prevent falls with specialized via ferrata sets and other
equipment. And the tradition remains to this day. Plus, the chains are much
nicer to hold with bare hands. Nice to know!

![A group of deer grazing near the road](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-24.jpg)

At the hut, we had some food and set off for the way back. As we reserved a seat
by putting our belongings onto it and left for a few minutes to order food, we came
back to our stuff on the ground and a family occupying our space. After some
arguing I left Pavlo to deal with it, while I brought the trays with the food we
ordered back to the table. When I arrived, all I heard was the conclusion of the
discussion, with the wife saying: "sorry, but I'm shocked," — and leaving for a
different table. Anyway...

![The end of the ridge with Turnia nad Dziadem (1901 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/morskie_oko/2026-11-15-23.jpg)

We walked back on the asphalt road, slipping on ice all the time after taking
our spikes off at the hut. The road back was very long and tedious, but with so
many other tourists taking it, we didn't have time to complain and just kept
walking. At some point near the middle of the segment there are some shortcuts
one can take. Think twice before doing that, they get very slippery in cold
seasons!

All in all, this is a great accessible hike that I can recommend to everyone.
Getting to see those lakes is amazing in any season, and I'll definitely be
returning here for a trip to higher mountains later on, probably in summer.
