When I was browsing OsmAnd for cool hikes around Walchensee, I kept looking at
[Krottenkopf (2086 m)](/hikes/2025-11-16-krottenkopf_bischof/),
but at the time it seemed like too difficult of a hike. When I looked around
this peak, Simetsberg (1836 m) caught my attention, and it seemed like a good
option for a DAAD Freundeskreis hike.

![View from the bottom of the mountain](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-00.jpg)

When we got on the train, we met an acquaintance of mine taking her brother
on a [hike to Herzogstand (1731 m)](/404/).
The brother never showed up, so she decided to depart herself. I offered her to
join our route instead and she happily agreed. Getting to the spot was not very
easy. It takes quite a walk from the lake to even enter the trail. Once we did,
we quickly moved uphill along a wide road, passing some huts on the way, and
even a Shrek-themed village toilet.

![A village toilet on the Simetsberg (1836 m) slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-07.jpg)

Soon after the huts the wide road ended, and we had to take a small overgrown
trail instead. It was clearly not used very often, but that didn't stop us from
moving forward. The trail is quite narrow and windy in this area. At first, we
were following the route nicely, taking turns upon turns.

![View from the Simetsberg (1836 m) slope towards Walchensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-01.jpg)

Next, every loop of the trail between turns became steeper, and the hike became
considerably more difficult. While the DAAD FK members were more or less
keeping up, my acquaintance wasn't as comfortable. We started taking more and
more breaks, making the hike noticeably longer.

![View from the slope of Simetsberg (1836 m) towards Soiernspitze (2257 m) and Hoher Kranzberg (1391 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-04.jpg)

During one of those breaks, we were passed by a mountain biker going down the
same trail. It was an incredible showing of skill to look at. His arms were
shaking, his pumped legs trembling, but he continued to carefully go down the
trail, jumping from one rock onto the other. I would need years to get anywhere
close to that level.

![View from the hut on the slope of Simetsberg (1836 m) towards the summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-02.jpg)

When we reached a hut quite close to the summit, my acquaintance said she
couldn't continue and decided to sunbathe on the slope instead. We agreed to
meet back up in the same spot, said our goodbyes just in case, and parted ways.
The rest of the group at that point was also quite tired, and it seemed some of
them made suboptimal clothing choices.

![View from the summit of Simetsberg (1836 m) towards Soiernspitze (2257 m) and Hoher Kranzberg (1391 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-05.jpg)

Simetsberg (1836 m) is a very appealing mountain. While there are multiple ways
to climb it, each one offers great views in all directions. To the south, you
can see [Soiernspitze (2257 m)](/hikes/2025-01-19-soiernspitze/)
and [Hoher Kranzberg (1391 m)](/404/).
And in general, it offers a great view towards the border, including
Westliche Karwendelspitze (2385 m) and Wettersteinspitzen (2305 m). To the
north, you can see Walchensee and surrounding mountains, including
[Herzogstand (1731 m)](/404/)
and [Jochberg (1565 m)](/404/).
Towards the west, you can spot [Krottenkopf (2086 m)](/hikes/2025-11-16-krottenkopf_bischof/),
[Wank (1780 m)](/404/)
and even get a tiny sneak peek at [Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2025-06-21-zugspitze_reintal/).

![View from Simetsberg (1836 m) towards Krottenkopf (2086 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-06.jpg)

When we started getting close to the top, we came across a few other pairs and
small groups of hikers going up and down. We reached the top, took a bunch of
photos and enjoyed a small lunch with the snacks we brought along. With the
great views around us, we took our time on the summit, but eventually it was
time to go down.

![Me going down the slope of Simetsberg (1836 m) towards Walchensee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-03.jpg)

When we reached the hut we passed on the way, my acquaintance was laying there
enjoying the sun. We talked a bit about what we saw, but didn't linger for too
long, since the sun was still high and we wanted to cool down in the lake. The
treck down took us quite some time, but at the end we made it to the parking
lot at the bottom of the mountain. We then walked along the road all the way
to the lake and enjoyed swimming in it until the sun went down.

![Walchensee shore with a view towards Jochberg (1565 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/simetsberg/2024-09-07-08.jpg)

To sum the hike up, it's definitely an interesting and rewarding experience. A
bit annoying to get to, so I'd either suggest combining it with a swim in
Walchensee like we did, or take a car to the bottom of the slope. Another
option would be to start or end the hike Wallgau, where the trail is a bit
more accessible.
