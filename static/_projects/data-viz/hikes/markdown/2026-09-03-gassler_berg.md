After my questionable adventures in the dense forests near [Hochalm (1427 m)](/hikes/2026-08-23-hochalm/),
I wanted to take it easy and enjoy a more relaxed hike. I dug into my backlog
and saw I missed some peaks near [Hausham](/hikes/2025-10-27-auer_berg/) that
I wanted to explore. Did I visit the missing peaks? Yes, I did. Did I enjoy the
hike? Absolutely! Do I need to buy new hiking attire again? Potentially...

The day before I barely had any sleep due to personal reasons, so I started the
hike quite late. I arrived in Gmund around noon and after some work-related
phone calls and started the hike around 13:30.

First on the map was Buchberg (996 m). There are several different ways to
reach the peak, but I chose to approach it from the north, since I've never
been there. The hike starts with me walking through the village of Gmund and
then heading into the forest. It takes quite a bit to get there, but the path
is surrounded by beautiful pastures and meadows, so I didn't mind it.
The meadows near Gmund remind me of the [Neufahrn (Niederbayern) hike](/hikes/2026-05-01-neufahrn_niederbayern_landshut/).
Cows here and there, short grass, occasional trees and pathways between them.
Ideal place to be at on a chill day.

![Cows on a meadow near Gmund](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-00.jpg)

As soon as the fields end and the forest begins, you're up for a decent climb
on a tractor road. As a pleasant surprise, the whole path towards the peak is
covered in blackberry bushes, so I stopped every few meters to pick some fresh
ones. Although the hike started quite late, I decided to take my time and enjoy
the snack regardless.

![Blackberries on the way up to Buchberg (996 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-01.jpg)

The rest of the way up was a bit overgrown and there were some fences to limit
cattle access, but crossing them for a human is not an issue. The last part of
the track to the top goes through a narrow trail on the "ridge", through the
ferns and bushes. Still, it's quite manageable. The view from the top was
disappointing though, as the peak is covered in trees and there is no view.

![View from the top of Buchberg (996 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-02.jpg)

From there, I continued to the next peak, which is Gassler Berg (1178 m). The
way between Buchberg (995.5 m) and Gassler Berg (1178 m) was closed off with
barbed wire, but it looked like the owner made some crossings near the road.
I expected the same hike as last time, tearing a second pair of hiking attire
and climbing through thick woods and thorns for hours. It turned out much
nicer: there was a lot of walkable space between the trees in the forest, and
many abandoned forestry roads I could easily walk on. The only downside were
the spiky fences marking territory boundaries, but even those were crossable.

![Path from Buchberg (996 m) towards Gassler Berg (1178 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-03.jpg)

Around halfway between the two peaks, I found a small clearing with a nice
view towards Tegernsee and [Wallberg (1722 m)](/404/). I took a break there
for some photos, and checked the weather forecast. It was still cloudy, but
at least no rain was expected. I also checked the map and noticed a monument
for Hans Schreier up ahead. As it was right on the trail, I went and checked
it out. Surprisingly well-kept for a monument in the middle of a
barely-accessible "ridge" trail.

![View from the Gassler Berg (1178 m) ridge towards Tegernsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-04.jpg)
![The Hans Schreier monument](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-05.jpg)

From there, the way to the top is quite steep and requires climbing over yet
another fence. The summit itself is flat and features no view or cross again,
but at least the path to the top is not overgrown and is easy to walk on.
Although it's the highest mountain on the route, it was not the most
challenging one.

![The summit of Gassler Berg (1178 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-06.jpg)

Next up was Oeder Kogel (1135 m). The original way towards it leads all the
way down to the farmland and then back up again, although the map shows some
disconnected roads that could be used as a shortcut. I decided to risk it and
quite soon got my leg punctured on a rotten tree trunk I stepped into.
Otherwise, the shortcut was quite nice and definitely worth it.

![A makeshift twig shelter on the way to Gassler Berg (1178 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-07.jpg)

I also had to climb over a few streams and some wetlands, but from there on the
climb was nice, although there was no trail in sight. The path was really
lovely, actually. The path was covered in spruce needles and the forest was
quite open. The stones littering the ground were covered in moss and the
occasional tree trunk was lying around. The top of the mountain was as usual
covered in trees, but I could see the meadows towards Munich peeking through.

![The view from the summit of Gassler Berg (1178 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-08.jpg)
![The road through the wetlands near Gassler Berg (1178 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-09.jpg)

The path to the next mountain, Schußkogel (1134 m), was even more difficult.
First, I went down to the wetlands and had to cross them in order to get to
the normal road on the other side. It was possible to not get your feet wet,
but I imagine other hikers might have a hard time with that. After that, I had
to climb down a steep path down the trail, and then climb back up the next
mountain.

![The mountain streams to cross on the way to Schußkogel (1134 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-10.jpg)
![A hut on the Schußkogel (1134 m) slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-11.jpg)

Once I reached the "saddle" between the two peaks, I had to cross yet more
wetlands and streams, followed by an abandoned tractor road from the local
forestry works. The road was overgrown with moss, but there was no tall grass
like [near Obergries](/2026-05-17-rechelkopf_semmelberg/), so it was easy to
walk on.

![The summit of Schußkogel (1134 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-12.jpg)

The last part takes you up to the summit on a steep grass slope with a few
trees. It was probably the easiest climb of the day, and I was happy to reach
the top. The view from there opened up towards [Gindelalmschneid (1335 m)](/404/)
but its northern side is covered in trees, so it wasn't that exciting.

![The road down from Schußkogel (1134 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-13.jpg)

The road down, on the other hand, was horrible. Although I found and followed a
tractor road, the grass on it was getting taller and wetter by the minute. The
local spiders and bees were covering the occasional flowers in the grass, being
not too thrilled of me stomping on their homes. The blueberry stems and thorny
plants were cutting into my legs. I immediately realized I'll have to pull out
ticks when I come home.

![Crossing Eckerbach near the bottom of Schußkogel (1134 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-14.jpg)

This continued for almost an hour of painful walking, until I finally crossed
Eckerbach, a small stream gathering water from the surrounding mountains and
flowing into Schlierach. From there, I decided I had enough tall grass and
scratched legs and followed the nice gravel road to the top of
Wallenburger Kogel (936 m).

![The summit of Wallenburger Kogel (936 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-15.jpg)

This is definitely the most boring peak of the day, but also the most adequate
one. I decided to follow one of the old forestry roads down instead of going
back the same way, which was definitely a mistake due to more overgrown grass,
but at least the road was short. I also met deer multiple times on the way,
both near Wallenburger Kogel (936 m) and Schußkogel (1134 m), which was a nice
addition.

![Mykola taking a break from hiking to do some productive work](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-16.jpg)

When I finally reached the bottom, I was quite tired from all the climbing
through wild plants, and wanted some rest. I found a nice spot with a wooden
bench and a table, where I had my snacks and did some work on the laptop I
brought along.

![The bike road between Gmund and Hausham](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-17.jpg)

From there, I walked along a hiking/biking road towards Hausham. The way was
very well-kept, and I met a lot of bikers staring at my tired and dirty
posture. I was happy to finally reach the village and head to the train
station.

![Sunset over pastures near Hausham](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/gassler_berg/2026-09-03-18.jpg)

The hike was a decent experience, and I was happy I got to complete it. The
berries were definitely a highlight, and it was nice seeing people from time
to time on the "normal" sections of the hike, when I wasn't climbing through
thorns, fences, streams, or wetlands. That being said, I wouldn't recommend
this hike to others. None of the peaks are easily accessible, they don't
have any views, and the paths are overgrown and difficult to walk on. If you
want to enjoy the smaller mountains of the Bavarian Alps, I highly suggest
visiting [Gindelalmschneid (1335 m) and Auer Berg (1252 m)](/hikes/2025-10-27-auer_berg/)
instead.
