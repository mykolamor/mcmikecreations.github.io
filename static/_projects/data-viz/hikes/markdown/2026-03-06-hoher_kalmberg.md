The experience started all the way in Bad Goisern, where I approached a local
older ski enjoyer on the train station and had a lengthy conversation about the
local area and the best mountains to visit. After noting down his choices, the
man, Pavlo and I all took the bus in the direction of Gosau and then got off at
the Ramsau stop. From there, we started our hike towards the ridge ahead of us.
This was the first successful 1000 elevation meter hike for Pavlo, so I was
really looking forward to his feedback after we finish the hike.

![View towards Gosau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-00.jpg)
![Forest on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-01.jpg)

The first section of the hike was quite easy. Hikers have a choice of either
following the gravel road leading to a neighboring mountain hut or taking a
more direct trail through the forest. We opted for the former and enjoyed the
scenic views while walking uphill. Both of us got an international roaming call
at the same time, Pavlo talked to his internet provider telling him about
increased fees, while I had a quick chat with a friend who offered to play
tennis some time soon.

![Christmas Rose up close](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-02.jpg)
![A meadow of Christmas Roses](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-03.jpg)

After laughing from the coincidence, we continued our hike and soon reached the
first mountain hut on our way, Iglmoosalm (1206 m). The hut was obviously
closed for the season, but we took a break there to eat some snacks and put on
our gaiters. From that point onwards, the trail was covered in snow and ice, so
we needed the extra protection for our legs.

![Iglmoosalm (1206 m) from above](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-04.jpg)
![Last patches with no snow on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-05.jpg)

We continued our hike, sometimes taking shortcuts through the forest to avoid
the icy patches on the main road. While it wasn't too difficult to traverse the
terrain, navigating through the snow and finding the right path was a bit
tricky.

![The ski path off the main trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-07.jpg)
![Going up the ski tour route](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-08.jpg)

Eventually, we reached an old wooden sign pointing off the main road uphill
with many foot, snow shoe, and ski tracks leading in that direction. My offline
map also pointed me in that direction, so we followed the trail and soon found
ourselves on a ski tour trail leading up the mountain. The trail was quite
bumpy, with constant ups and downs, but we made steady progress. I checked the
map and realized that we were going practically parallel to the main road,
so there was not much risk of getting lost, but also not much reward in terms
of gained elevation.

![Uneven terrain near the top of the ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-09.jpg)

As a result of this detour, we skipped the Schartenalm cluster of huts and
went straight to the saddle between the neighboring peaks. The huts were
closed anyway, but maybe the road upwards would have been more scenic than
going through the forest. As we came to the saddle to Goiserer Hütte (1592 m),
we saw a trail runner in shorts and summer gear enjoing a sandwich on a bench.
We talked for a bit, discussed the surrounding peaks and had a scenic toilet
break with a view of the Kalmberg peaks.

![View from the saddle on the ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-10.jpg)

We also discussed which route down we should take. We had four options:
going back the same way, going north down to Loskogel (1486 m),
going south along Niederer Kalmberg (1827 m) and Brenntenkogel (1640 m) to
the highway, or going straight down the north-eastern side of the ridge along
the cable car directly to Bad Goisern.

![Approaching Goiserer Hütte (1592 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-11.jpg)

Since logistically the last option sounded the best, we decided to try it out.
Before departing, the trailrunner told us he shortly tried to take the
north-eastern route down, but the snow was too deep and he had to turn back.
Having gaiters and shoe spikes, we were a bit more optimistic about our
chances. I then slid my hand across the wooden railing of the bench and got
a nice splinter stuck in my finger, which I couldn't pull out on the spot and
had to walk all the way down to the hotel with it.

![Face-shaped Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-12.jpg)

Before heading down, we put on our shoe spikes and took a detour towards the
highest summit of the ridge, Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m). The trail was quite
shallow and well-marked with footsteps from other hikers, we even came across a
couple going down from the peak, saying it's their first hike since last summer.
We made it to the top without too much trouble, only occasionally sinking our
feet into the snow. The views from the summit were breathtaking, with the
surrounding peaks covered in a blanket of snow, while the valleys below were
already green with spring coming.

![Visitor log book with stamps on the Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-13.jpg)
![View from Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m) towards Sonnenwendkogel (1638 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-14.jpg)

We checked the visitor book hidden in a box at the summit cross, and found out
that most people compare the summit cliff to a giant face. Now that I had some
time to look at my photos, it really does look like a head, with the summit 
cross being a little hairpin and the cliff being the face itself.

![View from Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m) towards Niederer Kalmberg (1827 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-15.jpg)
![The cross at Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m) summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-16.jpg)

We took a break for some photos and snacks, enjoyed the sun warming us up at
15°C, and then started our way back. The route back was nice and quick, and we
got to the hut just in time to catch some more sunlight for the descent.

![Avalanches on Angerstein (2100 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-17.jpg)
![Descriptory sign for Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-18.jpg)

The descent started off immediately rough. The snow was deep and we kept
sinking into it with every step. We were following the steps made by a previous
hiker, and somehow those steps were shallow, but we kept sinking deeper and
deeper as we went down. The gaiters kept sliding down and letting snow inside,
but we pushed through, knowing that we would eventually get to the bottom where
the snow melted.

![The deep snow on the descent](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-19.jpg)
![Looking back up during the descent](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-20.jpg)

At one point, we reached an old avalanche site, where the snow had slid down
the slope, taking down bushes and grass with it. The avalanche snow was packed
much tighter than the fresh snow around, so we decided to follow the avalanche
path down instead of going around, hoping it would be easier to walk on. It
was. Pavlo also tried taking some shortcuts through the vegetation, but the
snow was much deeper there, getting his clothes wet.

![The Wallmann-Poidl-Rast outdoor shelter](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-21.jpg)
![Looking back up at Hoher Kalmberg (1833 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-22.jpg)

As we kept going down, the snow got shallower and shallower, and eventually we
started seeing patches of grass and dirt again. Soon after, we reached the
Wallmann-Poidl-Rast outdoor shelter with a bunch of signs telling people to
pick up trash and not to litter. We took a break there to take off our spikes
and gaiters, and then continued down the forest trail. The feeling of relief
from having solid ground under our feet was short-lived, as the trail onwards
was covered in a layer of ice. Still, we couldn't be bothered to put on our
spikes again, so we just took it slow and steady, using surrounding branches
for balance where needed.

![Reaching the cable car station](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-23.jpg)
![Kalmberg ridge from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-24.jpg)

Eventually, we reached the bottom of the trail where the supply-only cable car
was located. It's just a small shed with a road leading up to it from he town,
but having it and a solid wide road to walk on was a huge help after the rough
descent. We followed the road all the way down to the town, hoping for a clear
gravel path, but the whole road was covered in ice from the sledging that took
place there during the winter. Still, we made it down safely, and went shopping
for some groceries before heading home.

![The Trockentannalm hut cluster](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hoher_kalmberg/2026-03-06-25.jpg)

Overall, it was an amazing hike with stunning views and a good challenge. A
solid 1000 meters of elevation gain and loss, accessible from the town and
the neighboring villages from the other side of the ridge. I would even go as
far as recommending it for all seasons, as most of the trail is well-marked and
the gravel roads are easy to follow. Just make sure to take the right equipment
for the occasion, preferably some snow shoes in winter and some good hiking
boots in summer.
