After my blackberry-rich hike with David, he messaged me for a new trip.
In-between, I had a tooth removed for an implant, so I couldn't do proper
hiking for a while. David didn't mind it and suggested doing an easy walk along
one of the south Bavarian lakes.

I was happy to do that, and we agreed on a round tour around Walchensee,
starting from the small café near the bottom of [Jochberg (1565 m)](/hikes/2025-12-14-lainbachfall_jochberg/).
At firstб I was thinking of reversing the route and ending the hike at the
café, but David suggested doing it clockwise, since that café wasn't that great
from his memory.

We started off nice and slow, with clouds covering the sun and a light breeze.
It was hard to decide if we should weare jackets or not, but we decided to keep
them on for now. The boat rental seemed to be closed already for the season,
although I could see some people in the distance swimming in the water, mostly
locals.

![Boat rental in Urfeld with a view towards Soiernspitze (2257 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-00.jpg)
![View towards Urfeld and Herzogstand (1731 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-01.jpg)

It was also nice to see familiar mountains in the distance, like
[Soiernspitze (2257 m)](/hikes/2025-01-19-soiernspitze/) peeking out from
behind the smaller summits closer to the lake. And, of course, the view
towards [Herzogstand (1731 m)](/404/) was amazing too.
I haven't been there in quite a while, but I remember it being an amazing
and very popular hike, rewarding visitors with a great view over the lake and
the surrounding mountains.

![Mykola looking towards Soiernspitze (2257 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-02.jpg)
![Crocus flowers on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-03.jpg)

As we continued, we reached Sachenbach, a small village on the eastern shore
of the lake. There was a beer garden there with a few people enjoying the sun
(although there wasn't much of it at that time). We decided to continue
walking, discussing recent events and our plans for future hikes.

![A bench with a view towards Simetsberg (1836 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-04.jpg)
![Sassau island on Walchensee with Simetsberg (1836 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-05.jpg)

Soon after, we came across a great bench with a direct view towards [Simetsberg (1836 m)](/hikes/2024-09-07-simetsberg/)
that I visited in 2024. It was really a trip down memory lane, and I was happy
to share my experience with David, who was also interested in doing hikes in
the area.

![Sassau island up close](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-06.jpg)
![Mushroom on the road to Fischberg (1164 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-07.jpg)

As we continued walking, I felt okay and thought that I should be able to climb
a small hill near the lake, Fischberg (1164 m). The road there seemed not steep
at all on the map, so I had high hopes of successfully reaching the top. The
road was a bit longer than I expected, and I came across many mushrooms and
frogs on the way, but eventually we reached the top.

![A tree trunk near Fischberg (1164 m) covered in mushrooms and moss](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-08.jpg)
![A toilet on the slope of Fischberg (1164 m) with a solar panel for the lightbulb](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-09.jpg)

Although the whole route was covered in trees, there was a small private hut on
the slope with a clearing towards the lake. We didn't trespass, but we managed
to snap some photos from the edge of the property. The last part of the climb
had us leave the tractor road and take a small path through the trees, which
was a bit more challenging, but still manageable despite my recent dental
surgery. I made sure to take it slow and manage my heart rate, so I wouldn't
overexert myself.

![A from crossing the road under my feet at Fischberg (1164 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-10.jpg)
![Mykola excitedly staring at a frog at Fischberg (1164 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-11.jpg)

The top of the mountain was similar to what I saw on my previous hikes to small
peaks, like [Großer Laber (1466 m)](/hikes/2026-09-06-grosser_laber/),
[Gassler Berg (1178 m)](/hikes/2026-09-03-gassler_berg/), and [Rechelkopf (1330 m)](/hikes/2026-05-17-rechelkopf_semmelberg/).
It was a small clearing with a few spruce trees and no views towards the lake.
The ground was covered in a mesh of spiky metal wire, clearly meant to be put
up around some area, but forgotten to lay there forever. It was covered in a
thick layer of leaves, dust, spruce needles and moss, so it was there for quite
some time, making it hard to see.

![The summit of Fischberg (1164 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-12.jpg)
![The painting at the entrance to the Waldschänke Niedernach restaurant](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-13.jpg)

From there, we descended back to the lake and headed to the restaurant
Waldschänke Niedernach, which David found on the map the day before and really
wanted to go there for some pork. I wasn't against it, although I wasn't sure I
could chew it properly, but I was happy to try. From there, we headed straight
towards our final destination, the bus stop at Einsiedl. I wasn't feeling like
getting more elevation, so we took the side of the road that was closer to the
lake.

![An artificial river cascade near Niedernachkraftwerk](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-14.jpg)
![A view towards a boat hangar near Fischberg (1164 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-15.jpg)

It was a very long walk back to the bus route, and we were quite tired by the
time we reached the end. Still, on the way we stopped at Biergartl on the south
side of the lake for a small refreshment. It was definitely a long trip, but
having company made it much more enjoyable. I was happy to have a chance to
walk around the lake and enjoy the views from the bottom of the mountains,
since I've seen the top-down views from several of them.

![View from Einsiedl towards Herzogstand (1731 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/fischberg/2026-09-13-16.jpg)

I'd definitely recommend this hike to people of all ages. If you have a car or
a bike, it can be even more enjoyable, as you can stop at different points along
the lake and experience the best views from all angles without walking over
20 kilometers. The lake is beautiful, and the surrounding mountains are
breathtaking. I'll definitely be returning here in the future.
