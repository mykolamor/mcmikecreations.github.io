Natalia, an organizer from "Wandern für glückliche Menschen", sent an invite for
a hike to Roßstein (1698 m) intended for Ukrainians. Since I haven't hiked with
them since [our trip to Fockenstein (1564 m)](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/),
I took it as a good chance to catch up and asked her if I could join.

After a long confusing back-and-forth throughout the week, on Saturday evening I
got a confirmation that I could join the event. Unfortunately, the bus to the
Bayerwald (855 m) I took last time [on my solo outing](/hikes/2025-10-05-buchstein_only/)
wasn't operating on weekends anymore, so I had to find other means of transport.
One of the hike members thankfully had a car, so I joined him, meeting him
early in the morning at a train station near Munich. It turned out we had
already met on the train from my [DAAD FK Bodenschneid (1669 m) hike](/hikes/2025-11-01-bodenschneid/) where I was told by a bar visitor to
"go back to my home country".

Anyway, we arrived at the Bayerwald (855 m) parking lot, joined the rest of the
group waiting for us, and started the ascent. The route there is quite easy,
just a well-kept mountain path through the forest, with frequent wooden steps
to make it more accessible. After around 80 minutes of walking, we arrived at
Sonnberg Alm and saw Buchstein (1701 m) from below.

![Buchstein (1701 m) from Sonnberg Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-00.jpg)

For many, this hike was their first view of Tegernseer Hütte (1650 m), so it
was nice showing them around, telling them about neighboring peaks, and discussing
potential future outings. While the group was snacking near the huts, I looked
at the map and saw a few peaks close by. As of now, this website has a map-style
hero for the home page, so the more hikes I have with 5 or more peaks, the
more variety shows up on the home page.

![View from the Sonnberg Alm beergarden hut towards a small secluded hut also labeled Sonnberg Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-01.jpg)

I bid the group farewell and left to walk across the Sonnbergschneid ridge
towards Friedbergkopf (1406 m). Ahead of time, I looked online for other
mentions of the ridge and the peak, but barely found anything (links can be
found in sources). I was a bit worried, since experienced hikers tend to
underestimate the difficulty, but it turned out very easy.

![The beginning of the Sonnbergschneid ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-02.jpg)
![View from the Sonnbergschneid ridge start towards Leonhardstein (1452 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-03.jpg)

While I was walking away from the group, (another) Natalia joined me on this
endeavor, collecting peaks for her Komoot account. We crossed a low barbed wire
fence and moved onto the ridge. What stood out immediately was the trail
running along the ridge, probably perfectly walkable in any season.
Although the lady wasn't too sure how far she would get and was ready to turn
back when things got sketchy, they never did.

![View from the ridge towards Filzenkogel (1444 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-04.jpg)
![The Friedbergkopf (1406 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-05.jpg)

The rest of the ridge to the summit was unimpressive but equally safe, with a
clearly visible, albeit slightly overgrown, trail across the whole slope.
When we reached the top, we got an interesting sideways view of
Silberkopf (1540 m) and Hirschberg (1670 m).

![View from Friedbergkopf (1406 m) towards Silberkopf (1540 m) and Hirschberg (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-06.jpg)

We saw the trail going down to Wildbad Kreuth. We weren't planning to descend,
but there are photos of it in the sources I linked, so you can check them out.
Seems like a normal trail.

From there, we turned back, walked to Sonnberg Alm, took some exclusive photos
(which turned out to be similar to the normal ones, of course), and made yet
another detour, this time towards Sonnberg (1576 m).

![View from Sonnberg (1576 m) towards Hochalm (1427 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-07.jpg)

While Friedbergkopf (1406 m) was a questionable choice of a summit,
Sonnberg (1576 m) was a popular, well-known peak. I'm not sure why there's no
official trail to the top, but you can get there by just walking up the slope,
and most people do. It's much smaller than Roßstein (1698 m), so the offered
views are more basic. Still, we met some guys there launching drones to take
cool shots of themselves with Buchstein (1701 m) in the background.

![View from Brotzeitfelsen towards Roßstein (1698 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-08.jpg)
![Climbing the grade A via ferrata towards Roßstein (1698 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-09.jpg)

We took a few photos and turned around to walk down the slope towards
Tegernseer Hütte (1650 m). Since I thought I saw the group at the top (it wasn't
them, but they were indeed at the top), I suggested we take the Roßsteinsteig
via ferrata instead of taking the long trail around the mountains. Natalia
agreed, and we quickly caught up with the others.

![View from the Roßsteinsteig towards Buchstein (1701 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-10.jpg)
![The cross at Roßstein (1698 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-11.jpg)

While I was away, the group did the obligatory group photos, a picnic and more.
All that was left to do was take my personal photos, chat with other hikers
(I use these chats as an opportunity to practice my German), and head towards the
huts. Natalia the organizer was really excited to climb Buchstein (1701 m) too,
but I immediately informed her that I'd be staying below. You can read about
my personal climb of Buchstein (1701 m) in similar weather conditions [here](/hikes/2025-10-05-buchstein_only/).

![The majority of the group taking their group photo on Roßstein (1698 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-19.jpg)
![My solo Roßstein (1698 m) photo](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-12.jpg)

Once we came down to Tegernseer Hütte (1650 m), we decided to take the easier
trail down to Sonnberg Alm instead of climbing down the via ferrata. This was
actually the wrong move, because the northern side of the mountain was fully
covered in ice and mud, while the southern via ferrata was dry and warm in the
autumn sun. Natalia the organizer did a head count of people wanting to climb
Buchstein (1701 m) itself, and after some discussion the group made the smart
decision to skip it, and we followed the trail down to Roßsteinalm.

![View of Buchstein (1701 m) from Roßsteinalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-13.jpg)
![View of Hirschberg (1670 m) from Hochplatte (1592 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-14.jpg)

From there, I somehow managed to convince the group to also visit
Hochplatte (1592 m), although the organizer made sure to tell me several times
that the views from there suck, the views from Roßstein (1698 m) sucked too,
and we should have visited Buchstein (1701 m). The trail to Hochplatte (1592 m)
was uneventful, a slope actively used by cows grazing on the meadows, with no
dangerous sections. A 15-minute detour up and a 5–10-minute walk down for an
average hiker.

![View from Mühlriedeck (1461 m) towards Ochsenkamp (1594 m) and Hirschberg (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-15.jpg)
![Evening view from Roßsteinalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-16.jpg)

Once we got to the top of Hochplatte (1592 m), the group took a few photos and
started chatting, having completely switched out of hiking mode. I took this
opportunity to leave them be and make another detour to Mühlriedeck (1461 m),
walking through some mud along the way. This next peak was also heavily used as a
pasture, and the road leading to the summit was covered in cow hoofprints and
mud. When we came back to Roßsteinalm, the rest of the group was still waiting
for us. We joined them and started heading down to the Bayerwald (855 m) parking
lot.

![Last evening view from Sonnberg Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-17.jpg)
![Sunset near Sonnbergalm Niederleger](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/sonnberg/2026-11-08-18.jpg)

As we hurried down, the group split into several small clusters of hikers, but
we all reached the road without trouble. The other hikers had already packed their
things into their car and were waiting for us slower walkers to say goodbye.

Conclusion time! Normally, I don't recommend routes like these. You can find
more accessible options right below, in the alternatives and variants sections.
This one is different though. All the peaks that I visited, including the ridge,
Friedbergkopf (1406 m), Sonnberg (1576 m), Hochplatte (1592 m), and
Mühlriedeck (1461 m), were super accessible to anyone of any fitness level, as
long as you get to Sonnberg Alm. Roßstein (1698 m) is always crowded, so if you
are looking for a quieter experience, any of those peaks does the trick.
