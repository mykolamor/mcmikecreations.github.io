Having fond memories of the Austrian [Vorderskopf (1858 m) hike with Annette](/404/),
I was very excited when Bohdan offered another route in the area. Nowadays, the
hiking bus can be taken with the Deutschlandticket, so there is no reason not
to travel across the border for some summer fun. And, oh boy, was it fun!

![River Rißbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-00.jpg)

Bohdan gave me two routes to choose from: a 15 hour hike through a few ridges
at once, or a shorter backup plan, if the original would be "too long and
difficult". I obviously went straight for the backup and started planning the
details for the hike.

![View from the bottom of the slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-01.jpg)

We left the bus at the Kaiserhütte stop, although one can ride until the
Tölzer Hütte stop. The weather was already quite hot, but the morning dew was
still giving us some cooling. There were tons of people near the road and
parking lots, making me forget the distance and elevation of the route we
chose.

The first 6.2 km and 950 elevation meters went by quite fine. I wasn't in my
best shape, since I just submitted a scientific conference paper the evening
before, but I was hopeful that I'd manage to complete the route.

![View at Soiernspitze (2253 m) from the Tölzer Hütte (1825 m) plateau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-02.jpg)

The view starts being nice quite early, but the neighboring peaks like
Vorderskopf (1858 m) or Östliche Karwendelspitze (2538 m) are hidden behind
trees and tall grass, so you need to get high up to start taking photos.

Bohdan brought out his pair of sports Meta glasses and took some footage of me
hiking. An interesting device for sure, especially in terms of portability, but
the video and image quality leaves a lot to be desired. Since you usually carry
a phone anyway, using it makes way more sense unless you're looking
specifically for hands-free POV shots.

![View at Tölzer Hütte (1825 m) from above](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-03.jpg)

Anyway, speeding past most hikers (except a few sportier ones) we got to the
crossroads leading to Tölzer Hütte (1825 m) and took the scenic route. The next
section to the hut was quite tedious, with a lot of horizontal tracks taking
time away from gaining elevation. The rockier terrain makes up for it though,
giving a vast panorama of the surrounding mountains, all the way to
Talelespitze (2104 m).

![Musicians at Tölzer Hütte (1825 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-26.mp4)

Once we got to the saddle-looking Alpine plain with Tölzer Hütte (1825 m), we
saw a band of folk musicians playing their instruments, catching me completely
off guard. We stayed a bit listening to the music and I wished this was an easy
social hike so I could stay there longer. Alas, we had to go climb our next
target - Schafreuter (2102 m).

![View towards Schafreuter (2102 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-04.jpg)
![Cows chasing a dog near Tölzer Hütte (1825 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-27.mp4 "autoplay")

Before doing that, I took one last glimpse over the edge of the hut fence to
check out the Delpsjoch (1945 m) ridge and saw a dog being chased by a herd of
cows. At least some entertainment on a tough hike!

![Scabiosa flower along the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-05.jpg)
![The cross at Schafreuter (2102 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-06.jpg)

Bohdan said it was time for his trail running exercise and ran up
Schafreuter (2102 m). I took my time, running out of breath every other minute,
burning under the sun (despite the forecasted clouds).

![View from Schafreuter (2102 m) towards Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-07.jpg)
![Mountain flowers growing on the rocks of Schafreuter (2102 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-08.jpg)

The last section to the peak was quite steep, so I took off my backpack (like
most other hikers chilling on the summit) and headed there with no extra weight
except my own.

![View from Schafreuter (2102 m) towards Sylvensteinsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-09.jpg)

Schafreuter (2102 m) is an amazing mountain to visit. It opens a breathtaking
view of all surrounding areas: Walchensee with [Herzogstand (1731 m)](/404/)
and [Jochberg (1565 m)](/hikes/2025-12-14-lainbachfall_jochberg/),
Sylvensteinsee with Hoher Zwiesler (1376 m) and Demeljoch (1924 m), Mittenwald
with [Soiernspitze (2257 m)](/hikes/2025-01-19-soiernspitze/)
and Hochkarspitze (2481 m) and many more. The neighboring Delpsjoch (1945 m)
also looked great from across the valley, so we decided to head there next.

![Chilling on Delpsjoch (1945 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-10.jpg)

After a quick photoshoot, we descended to the hut, where the concert was
already finished, and took the other way up. Bohdan as usual ran to the
Delpsjoch (1945 m) summit, while I took my time suffocating from the hot air
and lack of sleep.

![View from Delpsjoch (1945 m) towards Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-11.jpg)

Once I got to the top and took a breather, Bohdan gave me a choice: either we
go down and continue north-east as originally planned, or we take the unmarked
ridge with no trail and reach Baumgartenjoch (1939 m).

![View from Baumgartenjoch (1939 m) towards Fleischbank (2028 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-12.jpg)

With my stupid [chase of all hike-able peaks](/hikes/tag/Web/) I suggested to
visit the extra summit taking the ridge there. The track between the peaks
wasn't that bad. It was an area of mountain goats and even occasional cows, so
you could find some trampled paths in the grass. The slope wasn't crazy steep
on one side (on the other it was just a straight drop), so there was no sense
of immediate danger.

![Mykola starting the descent from Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-13.jpg)

When we reached Baumgartenjoch (1939 m), the view we saw was largely the same.
Bohdan gave me another set of options: either we go back west, traverse the
route east across the almost-vertical slope, or we take the slope south-east
in hopes of finding a good way down to our original route.

![View down from Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-14.jpg)

I chose the last option, since I was already tired, and from the top it looked
like the least effort. We took the slope and started heading down along the
ridge. I kept taking glances over the edge, trying to find a spot to safely
descend, but the vertical drop continued all the way along the ridge.
As we kept going down, the slope started becoming more and more steep, and
before we knew it, we found ourselves having to carefully climb down instead
of simply hiking downhill.

![The wild slope of Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-15.jpg)
![The stone slope of Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-16.jpg)

Bohdan in his usual manner ran off ahead, leaving my line of sight. Meanwhile,
I slipped on some long wet grass and fell down, grabbing random ferns with my 
hands to slow down. It worked, but I badly hurt my elbow and finger. After
[breaking another finger near Vordere Kesselschneid (2001 m)](/404/)
and soon after recovering [falling off a cliff at Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/404/)
this winter, I try to avoid such incidents, but they keep finding me
nonetheless.

![Bohdan descending from Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-28.mp4 "autoplay")

Although I called Bohdan plenty of times, he was too far to hear me and help
out, so I gathered my strength and kept going. A few minutes later he peaked
over a steeper section asking if I was coming down, since he made quite a bit
of progress and was waiting for me to catch up. He also suggested climbing down
backwards with my ass towards the sun, which demanded more endurance, but let
me keep my balance and find footing more easily.

![Mykola resting after climbing down from Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-17.jpg)
![View back up Baumgartenjoch (1939 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-18.jpg)

After almost 3 hours of climbing down 900 elevation meters, we finally reached
the Baumgartenbach stream and took a small rest. By then, I was bruised from
falling, scratched from the spiky plants, itchy from ants and nettle, blundered
by rocks and pebbles, and even covered in weird plant juices. The only thing I
wanted was to get home, but the way back was still very long and I barely had
strength to keep going.

![View at Jagdhütte Roßangerl](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-20.jpg)

The sky was getting dark and the clouds looked rainy, so we hurried up to get
on the original trail and head back down. Walking past the Jagdhütte Roßangerl
I quickly realized that I'm too tired to hike properly, barely moving along the
trail. There was not much time left until the last bus, which got me quite
worried. A few locals passed by us in their cars, and we waited in the ditches
for them to drive by.

![View from Baumgartensattel (1554 m) onto the valley](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-19.jpg)

After passing Baumgartensattel (1554 m), we felt the first drops of rain. Soon
after, the thunderstorm followed, soaking us in fresh yet cold water. Although
I had a waterproof jacket and a backpack cover, everything from my waist down
got completely wet.

![Cows near Baumgartensattel (1554 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-21.jpg)

With a bit over one hour left before the last bus towards Lenggries and 2 hours
of hiking ahead, Bohdan told me he wants to make it alone. He suggested that I
should take the trail up to Tölzer Hütte (1825 m), while he will trail run downhill to
our point of origin near the Kaiserhütte bus stop. Although it was a bitter
situation, I said my goodbyes and kept pushing towards the hut, seeing Bohdan
disappear into the mist of the thunderstorm.

![Rainy slope of Delpsjoch (1945 m) from Tölzer Hütte (1825 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-22.jpg)

As I arrived to the hut, all wet and dirty, I was greeted with amused looks from
numerous hiking groups inside. I was by far not the only one seeking shelter
there, with tons of people hanging their clothes in the drying room at the
entrance. After some negotiations, I got a place for the night and tried eating
dinner. After everything that happened, it was difficult to push food down my
throat, so I decided to multitask and plan for the next day.

![Wet mountain panorama from Tölzer Hütte (1825 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-23.jpg)

I had a dentist appointment near Munich at 9 AM, meaning I'd have to take the
6:45 AM train from Lenggries. It takes 35 minutes to drive to Lenggries and
1.5 hours to hike down in dry weather. All in all, I had to get up at roughly
4 AM to continue my descent. The biggest downside of this plan was the drive
to Lenggries. The bus in the valley starts operating at noon, so I had to
either hitchhike or grab a taxi. I found some 24/7 Lenggries taxi numbers and
went to sleep.

![Resting spot on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-24.jpg)

After hours of bed bug torture, bleeding from the scratches, and muscle pain
from the climb, the alarm rang and I set off, trying to call the taxi on the
way. To my complete surprise, the 24/7 taxi didn't respond at all, the others
didn't pick up, and the only operator I reached told me they start operating
at 10:00 AM and all the cars are already booked until 13:00. In other words,
my only hope was catching a local car going shopping or working in Lenggries
or Bad Tölz. This reminded me of my [Mount Fujisan (富士山) (3776 m)](/404/)
hike where I also spent the night walking on an empty road towards
civilization.

![View at Vorderskopf (1858 m) at dawn](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenjoch/2026-08-02-25.jpg)

I put on my head lamp and headed downhill in the dark of the night. The way
down was as long and annoying as the way up, but the aforementioned long
horizontal sections actually played in my favor this time, allowing me to pick
up speed. I tried looking around at the views a bit more while going down, but
the sky was still too dark to make out any silhouettes or mountain shapes.

After roughly 80 minutes of walking down, I reached the parking lot and saw a
car speed past me. Being desperate for any vehicle, I stayed on the road
towards Lenggries, until I eventually reached the same car parked on the side
of the road. Inside was a local poacher scouting the hillsides with his
binoculars, carrying several hunting rifles on the back seats of the SUV. He
was not pleased with my presence at all, threatening me to back off and
suggesting I wait another 6 hours until the bus comes.

I let him be and kept going, eventually crossing the border back into
Germany. Several cars drove by in the opposite direction, but I was hopeful
I'll find at least someone to pick me up. After more walking, I heard a car
behind me and immediately made myself seen by waving and jumping all over the
place.

A local lady working in the Bad Tölz Kanzlei took me with her and dropped me
off at the Lenggries train station! We chatted about work, local housing
prices, education, weather, hiking and nature. I was trying to keep her in
good company while not being too annoying with questions. Afterwards, I made it
just in time for the dentist appointment and arrived there all dirty and dead
inside.

All in all, I would suggest to *NOT* do this hike. We had plenty of chances to
handle the hike better, but kept making wrong decisions. First, doing
Schafreuter (2102 m) and Delpsjoch (1945 m) while tired is already strenuous,
we could have easily skipped one of them. Second, walking the ridge towards
Baumgartenjoch (1939 m) is not the best idea if you don't know the terrain, we
could have gone back to Tölzer Hütte (1825 m) without taking on this ridge. Third,
climbing down a 50% incline for three hours is not the most optimal route to
take, we could have taken the same ridge back to the huts. Fourth, this whole
trip could have been shortened or skipped due to the potential thunderstorm
showing up on the weather forecast. Fifth, we could have hitchhiked one of the
cars that drove past us, I'm sure they were going towards Lenggries, or at
least towards civilization. We made the wrong move every time, and this is what
happened. You should learn from my mistakes!
