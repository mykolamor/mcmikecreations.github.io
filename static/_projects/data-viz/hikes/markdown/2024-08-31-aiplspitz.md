When I was on a hike to [Seebergkopf (1538 m)](/404/),
I noticed a very nice mountain in the distance. It was even visible from the
train to Bayrischzell. That mountain was Aiplspitz (1759 m). Although it's
not the biggest mountain around, especially compared to [Rotwand (1884 m)](/hikes/2024-09-29-rotwand/),
it still offers great views over the Spitzingsee valley and towards
[Wendelstein (1838 m)](/404/). I sent out a DAAD FK announcement for the hike
and two members agreed to join me!

![Dirt road towards mountain huts near Geitau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-00.jpg)
![View towards Wendelstein (1838 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-01.jpg)

We started easy, following a dirt road towards some huts near Geitau. Since
the weather was extremely hot, everyone was craving shade and water, and we saw
tons of cows on the slopes either hiding under trees or drinking from barrels
and troughs. We also got to see [Seebergkopf (1538 m)](/404/) that I visited
with DAAD FK before.

![Cows enjoying water with Heißenplatte (1593 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-02.jpg)
![View towards Aiplspitz (1759 m) and Kleinmiesing (1666 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-03.jpg)

As we kept going, the dirt road ended and we entered a typical dry and dusty
trail. Although it was quite steep, walking on it wasn't a big issue. Or so I
thought. Soon after, the terrain changed and we were climbing up gravel.
Although I've been on quite a few mountains by now, I don't think I've seen
trails like these (although I'm sure they are pretty common on higher peaks).
We were slipping sideways and down, hitting our toes against the rocks,
breathing in dust and being grilled by the sun. All in all, nothing terrible,
but not a pleasant hiking experience either.

![Entering the gravel section near Aiplspitz (1759 m) looking back at Wendelstein (1838 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-04.jpg)
![Rocky section on the Aiplspitz (1759 m) ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-05.jpg)

After the gravel section is over, the next section is pretty much a climb. You
start off light, having to do high steps on some semi-polished rocks that many
hikers use. As the road keeps going, you have to use your arms more and more.
My group started struggling quite a bit with this section, taking their time
and making frequent brakes due to the effort and hot weather. While I would
normally recommend taking more water, keep in mind you'll have to carry all of
it while "climbing" upwards.

![The ridge towards Aiplspitz (1759 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-06.jpg)
![Looking back from the slope of Aiplspitz (1759 m) towards Heißenplatte (1593 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-07.jpg)

Once you get to the ridge leading to Heißenplatte (1593 m), you can catch your
breath under the shade of some pine bushes and turn towards the rest of the
path up to the summit of Aiplspitz (1759 m). While I wouldn't call this trail
risky, as there is always something to grab onto, and the rocks themselves are
stable and big, I wouldn't recommend coming here after rain or snow. The ridge
is moderately thin and has exposed sections. If you get dizzy from the heat,
nothing will stop you from falling down and getting heavily injured (same as
on many other mountains). Since most of the way is exposed, make sure to get
enough cover from the sun.

![The climb to the top of Aiplspitz (1759 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-08.jpg)
![Looking over the Aiplspitz (1759 m) ridge towards Rotwand (1884 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-09.jpg)

The route was quite crowded on the day of the hike. At the crossroads towards
Nagelspitz (1555 m) we met a pair of older ladies trying to get their bearings.
They came from Hammer and were considering either going to Lochgrabensattel or
descending the way we came. We warned them that all the roads were quite tough,
and they chose the latter option. On the way up, the sections kept getting more
and more exposed, with large portions of rocks and not that many pine bushes.

![View from Aiplspitz (1759 m) ridge towards Kleinmiesing (1666 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-10.jpg)
![Maksym enjoying a snack near the top of Aiplspitz (1759 m) while looking at Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-11.jpg)

Apart from the heat, we started noticing more and more bugs flying around us.
At this time of the year, they are looking for each other to build groups and
the easiest way to find others for them is to fly up to the highest point. So
all of the local peaks were covered in them, getting into every photo we were
trying to make, flying into our eyes and ears, preventing us from enjoying our
food and drinks. I've experienced that quite a few times, so I was calm, but
others were really annoyed.

![The cross at Aiplspitz (1759 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-12.jpg)
![Mykola relaxing at Aiplspitz (1759 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-20.jpg)

The top of Aiplspitz (1759 m) wasn't that impressive, since the main focus was
definitely the panoramic view towards the lakes. We still had quite a way
to go, so we took off. Quite soon, when we approached Tanzeck (1703 m), we were
faced with a via ferrata section. It was just a few meters wide, nothing too
dangerous, but it was enough to create a traffic jam with hikers constantly
passing it in both directions, some of them being scared of heights or exposed
terrain.

![Via ferrata section at Tanzeck (1703 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-13.jpg)
![Trail towards Benzingspitz (1735 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-14.jpg)

After some waiting we managed to pass the section and head further. We didn't
have a concrete plan in mind, since I didn't know how long it would take our
group to climb the previous sections. Thus, we decided to head to
Jägerkamp (1746 m), passing by Benzingspitz (1735 m). While I didn't feel too
tired, the sun was really annoying, and everyone wanted to cool down somewhere.
It wasn't possible on the slope, so we were in a hurry to go down.

![My group heading past Benzingspitz (1735 m) with a view towards Aiplspitz (1759 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-15.jpg)
![View from Benzingspitz (1735 m) towards Wilde Fräulein (1615 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-16.jpg)

We passed some more hikers on the way and reached Jägerkamp (1746 m). It wasn't
as exciting as I imagined, the whole summit was covered in bushes and bugs. We
did it more for a challenge and didn't have a photo session in mind, so we
headed straight down from there, passing Schönfeldhütte (1410 m).

![Schnittlauchmoosalm (1616 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-21.jpg)
![View from Schönfeldhütte (1410 m) towards Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-18.jpg)

We took one final glance at the peaks we just visited and headed down. The
trail was well-kept and we saw many more hikers hoing down towards the lake.
Once we reached it, we couldn't hold off and went swimming. I went further into
deep water and managed to get cramps on both legs, but nothing bad happened.

![Silver Thistle near Schönfeldhütte (1410 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-17.jpg)
![The Spitzingsee church](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aiplspitz/2024-08-31-19.jpg)

I highly recommend this route to everyone who seeks a challenge. It is not as
long as some of the other once, but packs a punch in terms of elevation. While
not for everyone, it offers great views towards the valleys and lakes around.
My main suggestion would be to take more water with you and some sun protection
for your head.
