After going on the [PR1 hike](/hikes/2025-04-04-pedra_rija/), because of
bad weather forecasts for the rest
of our stay, I and Serhii decided to tackle another hike
on the same day. Arriving to Parque das Queimadas by taxi, we took a quick
look around at the local traditional huts and started walking. The hike follows
the PR9 route until the Caldeirão Verde waterfall, since the rest of the route
was closed off due to flooding.

![Traditional hut at Parque das Queimadas](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-06.jpg)

The hike starts off in a lush forest, full of giant trees and bushes covering
the view of the mountains and the sun. There were a lot of people on the route,
far more than I've ever seen in Bavaria, so I had to practice dodging them while
advancing ahead while they were strolling along. The number of tourists from
different countries was also interesting, having the majority of them come
from Germany (obviously, duh), the rest from France and England. It's not unusual
for me to meet a stranded German hiker in the middle of nowhere in Japan or the
Azores, but seeing well-equipped groups from the UK or France was new to me.
Strangely, despite all the winter boots and hiking sticks they took, nobody
except us opted in to take raincoats.

![Giant trees of the PR9 route](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-00.jpg)

Going further along the hike, we started coming across some waterfalls, some
of which were flowing across the track. While the waterfalls didn't pose any
physical challenges, there was a big queue to take a photo of each stream of
water, which meant further delays for us. It was getting quite late and we
wanted to get back to civilization at a reasonable hour to get some transport
back to Santana, so waiting every time to take some photos was a big deal.

![Unnamed waterfall along PR9 at 32.7828182, -16.9135953](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-01.jpg)
![Greenery below the trail covering the landscape view](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-02.jpg)

As we kept going, the trail became much narrower, forcing us to make breaks
and jump over the canal to hug the rocks in order to let other hikers walk
back. Considering how popular the PR9 route is, this happened almost every
minute, severely slowing us down. Still, it was nice that the canal was
mostly paved on the sides, allowing us to dodge all the mud puddles around
it.

![Narrow trail along the canal](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-03.jpg)

It's important to mention that the length of the hike on the signs along
the trail and the net walking time posted online is quite exaggerated if the
road is empty, but it does match if there are others on the route. There's not
much space to let others through, so every time you have to do an awkward dance
where you either hug the wire fence, the canal or the rocky wall on the other
side of the trail to let others through. Some more experienced hikers know to
wait on wider spots to let others pass by, but others stubbornly push forward
and create traffic jams every time.

![Entrance to a canal tunnel](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-04.jpg)
![Dark and moist insides of a tunnel along PR9](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-07.jpg)

Eventually, we reached some tunnels barely marked [on the map](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/698103146#map=19/32.783998/-16.926455)
and headed inside. At first, everything was going smoothly; we were ducking
under the low ceiling and swiftly moving forward using the head lamp I
brought from my [Pyramidenspitze (1997 m) hike](/hikes/2025-02-02-pyramidenspitze/).
Later on, however, it started to rain and we faced a flooded tunnel with ankle-deep
water, forcing us to slow down, duck down even more and try to skip on the pebbles
sticking out of the water. At some points it wasn't possible, or we weren't
careful enough, so we got our feet wet and had to keep moving carrying water
puddles in our shoes.

![Caldeirão Verde waterfall](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/caldeirao_verde/2025-04-04-05.jpg)

At last, we reached Caldeirão Verde and met some people chilling on the rocks
enjoying the waterfall. The route forward to Madre da Levada do Caldeirão Verde
was closed off due to flooding and landslides, so we took some photos at the
waterfall and rushed back to finish the hike before dusk. A random passer-by
at the beginning of the hike told us that the park closes at 17:30, which went
by a long time ago at that point, so we hoped she misunderstood the signs and
sped up towards the finish line. Getting our feet wet at the tunnels again, we
kept going towards the start/finish, running by other hikers returning from
the waterfalls. To our surprise, we even met some hikers that just started the
route, making us wonder what they wanted to do there with no light and/or gear
to pass the running water and flooded tunnels. When we reached the parking lots,
we went to the shops near the ticket shop to get some souvenirs and treated
ourselves to some hand-made poncha, wrapping the hike up.

In summary, this was a worthwhile hike with some wonderful green scenic views,
but you have to be really careful to choose the right time to take the route.
In summer, I assume, it's dry and easy to pass, but there are dozens of
tourists at every step. In spring/autumn, there are fewer hikers (but still
tons of them), but the route is mostly wet, muddy and slippery, with some
flooded areas closer to the midpoint. In winter, from what I've heard, the
majority of the route is inaccessible.
