I checked the weather on Saturday as usual and saw that next week was filled
with snowstorms and cold weather. The obvious conclusion was that this weekend
was my last chance to retrieve the hiking stick I lost on the way down from
[Sonnenberg (1622 m)](/hikes/2026-02-15-oberammergau_sonnenberg/).
Given the spot where I fell down, I thought it might be possible to descend
nearby and climb back up to the spot where the stick was located.

![I found Jesus on the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-00.jpg)

I sent out a message, or more precisely a whining remark about the weather
obscuring my chances to get my stick back, and several people replied willing
to hike to the same cliff with me and help me find the stick. Stanislav,
who joined me on the hike to [Trainsjoch (1707 m)](/hikes/2026-01-18-trainsjoch/),
even took a snow shovel and some glacier spikes in case the terrain is rougher.

![Josefs Kapelle near Unterammergau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-01.jpg)
![Pürschlinghaus from below near the lower turn onto the ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-02.jpg)

We started off slow and steady, following the dirt road towards Pürschlinghaus,
eventually passing the known landmarks, such as Langtalalm and the hillside
Josefs Kapelle. Soon after, we started seeing the Alpine hut itself, which was
currently closed until the spring season comes into full swing. We kept going
up, passing the point where I exited the forest and joined the road after
falling down from the cliff.

![A look over the ridge towards Kienjoch (1953 m) and Zugspitze (2962 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-03.jpg)

When we walked to the trail going to the cliff top, it was fully covered in
thick snow. As we didn't want to spend too much time getting stuck in it, I
suggested following the road to the saddle on top of the ridge and walk
backwards from there.

![The via ferrata section on the sourthern side of the ridge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-04.jpg)

The others didn't have any strong opinions about our path, so they agreed and
we went all the way to the top. After getting there, I was really surprised to
see some footsteps leading in the direction of Sonnenberg (1622 m). On the
other hand, I started getting a bit anxious too, since the chance someone took
my stick was getting higher. Still, we already got to see some nice views on
the other side of the ridge, including a glimpse of [Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2025-09-18-zugspitze_hoellental/),
the tallest mountain in Germany that I ascended twice before.

![The view towards Unterammergau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-05.jpg)

We followed the marks left in the snow by the previous hikers, although they
were quite far from the official path, and eventually got to the first
via ferrata cable on the northern side of the ridge. At this point, the
other footsteps took a sharp turn downhill and we never saw other footsteps
again, meaning those people decided to abandon this route altogether.

![The view from the steeper ridge section towards Pürschlinghaus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-07.jpg)

This reassured me that I might still find my stick, but also meant the way
ahead was more difficult than it already was. When we approached the secured
area up close, we realized the cable was hidden under a big layer of ice and
snow, and the surrounding slope was so steep that we would definitely fall down.

![The view towards Graswang and Notkarspitze (1888 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-12.jpg)

Thankfully, Stanislav took out his shovel and we took turns digging the snow
and ice until we were able to properly go down and grab onto the cable. We
fiddled a bit with our hiking sticks (I had one left, but the others were using
the pairs they had), getting them down and across the cable-secured zone, but
eventually we crossed it and approached a big split between two giant rocks.

![My triumphant pose at the split between the rocks with my hiking stick](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-06.jpg)
![Sonnenberg (1622 m) from below with the climbing route visible starting at the trees](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-08.jpg)

This was the last cliff before Sonnenberg (1622 m), and we could already see
the cross at the top, so the question about my stick came up. A few seconds
after Stanislav asked: "So where would your stick be?", he shouted: "Stick!
I see it!" The hiking pole was just dormant on the surface of the cliff, a
few meters down from where I left it when I fell down the cliff myself. It was
nothing short of a miracle, to see it on the bare surface in a largely
accessible location. While the way to the mountaintop was leading right up one
of the rocks at the split, the other hikers took a left turn and climbed up
the other rock to catch a few rays of sun, waiting for me to retrieve my stick.

![A fresh picture of the cliff I slipped down from](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-09.jpg)

I borrowed the hiking poles of one of the other hikers and started climbing
downhill to reach the spot where my pole was laying. At first, everything was
going smoothly, although I did slip a little here and there. But when I
approached my target, the ridge got much steeper, being bare rocks covered
in a layer of ice and a bit of snow. There was nothing to hold onto and nowhere
to secure my footing. I had to kick the ground ahead of me to make stable holes
in the ice, while using the borrowed hiking poles and my freezing hand to keep
balance. One time I even slipped down and started sliding the same way as the
month before, but after much hardship I did reach the stick.

Now, holding three sticks in one hand and using the other for balance, I
started walking backwards retracing my steps to join the others. Having anxiety
from the previous fall and feeling tired overall, I knew that I had only one
chance to walk back safely, otherwise I wouldn't have the strength to climb
uphill back onto the main path.

![The view from Sonnenberg (1622 m) towards Zahn (1615 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-10.jpg)

Being very slow and careful to the point where I annoyed the other hikers
waiting for me, I finally reached them, gave them all the sticks to hold, and
climbed over a few final trees to get back onto the trail. From there on, we
walked under a Sonnenberg (1622 m) rock overhang onto the crossroads leading
to the top, Stanislav put on his glacier spikes and we got to the top.

![The top of the climbing route to Sonnenberg (1622 m) with a view of Pürschling (1566 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-13.jpg)

Everyone commented about how I was lucky to be alive at all after a fall from
the cliff, and how it was a miracle I didn't break anything. Although a month
ago I was filled with adrenaline from the fall and didn't think much of it,
looking back and seeing the cliff from a different angle made me realize that
they were absolutely right, and that at my current experience level and lack
of winter Alpine equipment, I should refrain from doing such dangerous trips.

![Our group posing at the top of Sonnenberg (1622 m), photo taken by the older guy](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-14.jpg)

With the main goal of the hike accomplished, the only job left was to get back.
But when I turned around, I noticed a 60-ish year-old guy climbing the side of
the Sonnenberg (1622 m) cliff after following our footsteps. When he got to the
top, he explained that he was spending the night on a nearby mountaintop and
was going to Unterammergau to get home, but he saw our footsteps entering the
ridge and decided to follow along. When asked about his further plans, he
packed up and told us he was done for the day. We assumed he meant going back
to August-Schuster-Haus (also known as Pürschlinghaus), but he just smiled
and started walking downhill straight towards Unterammergau. I assume his plan
was to walk straight to Langtalalm or even Kolbensattelhütte.

Regardless, we got over our amazement by the whole situation and our adventure
overall, and started going back by following the path we carved out previously.
To my big relief, nothing major happened, we safely got to the via ferrata
cable, climbed over a few fallen trees, then walked a while and climbed over
the closed gate at the entrance to the ridge path. The gate itself was
completely burried under a big layer of snow, so climbing over wasn't much of
a challenge.

![Alpine chamois near the ridge path](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-15.jpg)
![Langtalalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-16.jpg)

Leaving the ridge, we saw a pack of Alpine chamois having a toilet break, took
a look at the views and went down. Passing by tons of hikers of all different
levels on the dirt road, we reached the Schleifmühlklamm, a gorge with a bunch
of waterfalls very close to Unterammergau. Since we already missed the chance
to catch our train on time, we decided to visit the gorge instead, and had a
lovely time looking at all the big and small waterfalls on the way. After
reaching the town and walking past the snowmobile that saved me last time, we
got to the train station and headed home.

![Upper waterfall at Schleifmühlklamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-17.jpg)
![Lower waterfall at Schleifmühlklamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-19.jpg)

Overall, I would say this is a medium hike when done in summer and should not
be attempted at all in winter. With the constant snowstorms raging in the area
every few weeks, the northern side of the ridge where most of the hike takes
place doesn't have enough time to melt the snow. The steep slopes of the cliffs
make it even more dangerous to stay there outside of the warm season. Even at
the second half of March, there was still a lot of snow with no signs of it
melting.

![Anemone hepatica on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-18.jpg)
![The snowmobile that took me down from the August-Schuster-Haus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/unterammergau_sonnenberg/2026-03-22-20.jpg)

As for the summer variant, the ridge is pretty safe and secured in all
dangerous spots, so it can be attempted by medium-level hikers. I would say
that it can be compared in difficulty to [Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/hikes/2025-08-31-brecherspitz/).
Having plenty of water and good shoes would definitely help though.
