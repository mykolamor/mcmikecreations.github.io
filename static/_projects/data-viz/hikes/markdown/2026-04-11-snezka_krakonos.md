Mikuláš and I took off in a giant continuous line of tourists going up the
asphalt road to the bottom of the mountain. It was a lush mixed forest, mostly
covered in various pines. Some of the people on the trail were the same ones we
saw on the bus we took.

![Entering the National Park](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-00.jpg)
![Llamas on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-01.jpg)

We had grandiose plans for the route, so we couldn't stay in the crowd. We gave
ourselves a boost and rushed past most people tagging along together, soon
walking along and catching up to early bird groups.

![The forest with the dirt road up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-02.jpg)
![Looking back at the valley](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-03.jpg)

The forest started to clear up and the road has become way more rocky than
before. Random patches of snow or ice started to show up on the sides, but the
walk was still pretty comfortable.

![Water supply building in Obří důl](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-04.jpg)

A few times we walked past barricaded entrances to local mines all the way back
from the 16th century. We even saw some backup ventilation shafts! The
waterfalls from the water streams on the slopes were also cool. But despite the
older age of these mountains, the streams haven't carved out that big of paths
for themselves.

![Ferns and shrubs closer towards the saddle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-05.jpg)

As we ascended higher, we started seeing the surrounding summits and the
saddles between them. Back then, it looked like a typical saddle you'd find in
Austrian or German Alps. What I didn't know, however, was that the top of the
mountains was a relatively flat tundra plateau occasionally covered in shrubs
and small trees. It was so unusual!

![Approaching Dom Śląski](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-06.jpg)
![The view from Dom Śląski towards the Polish territory](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-07.jpg)

Soon after, we reached Dom Śląski at the flat top of the saddle and made our
first short break. The restaurant is located on the Polish side already, so you
can say I had a double-country hike again. While the way up was relatively
empty, with occasional small groups we passed by, the top area was quite
crowded.

![Sněžka (1603.3 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-08.jpg)

There was a single paved road leading to the observatory at the top of
Sněžka (1603.3 m), with a single continuous line of hikers going up and down.
The snow has already melted revealing the ice that covers the paved trails.
With the hikers attempting the route mostly without shoe spikes, quite many of
them were slipping and/or falling.

![The Czech-Polish border with Studniční hora (1555 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-09.jpg)

We decided to skip the spikes on the way up, but we couldn't skip all the
viewpoints overlooking the surrounding ridges! Every dozen of meters, there was
a small offshoot of the trail leading to a scenic panorama. We made sure to
visit all of them while avoiding the constant flood of other tourists.

![Observatory and chapel at the top of Sněžka (1603.3 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-10.jpg)

The top of Sněžka (1603.3 m) was a nice surprise too, with a few buildings for
any and all use cases. First, visitors are greeted with an observatory on the
Polish side and a viewing platform towards Karpacz.

![The view from Sněžka (1603.3 m) towards the mountaintop plateau with Luční bouda restaurant in the middle](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-13.jpg)

Next, you see the St. Lawrence's Chapel (Kaplica pw. Świętego Wawrzyńca) in the
middle of the summit area. I'm used to seeing crosses of all sizes on
mountaintops, but I don't recall seeing a chapel there ever since my [Brünnstein (1619 m) hike](/hikes/2025-07-06-bruennstein/).

![The view from the cable car station downwards](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-12.jpg)

On the Czech side, we were greeted with the [Sněžka postal office](https://www.postovnasnezka.cz/),
where you can send and receive actual mail, although it also functions as a bar
and a souvenir shop. It was cool to see so much infrastructure on the peak, but
the prices in the shop were expectedly steep.

![The cable car station on the Czech side of Sněžka (1603.3 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-11.jpg)

Lastly, there was the cable car station. Since the snow already mostly melted
and summer hasn't yet started, the station was closed, but it still offered
nice views along the ridge. Also, the path along the cable car poles was more
popular, with an even greater flow of tourists going up and down.

![Returning to Dom Śląski](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-14.jpg)
![Posing in front of Studniční hora (1555 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-15.jpg)

After spending so much time on the summit, we contemplated if we should keep
going towards e.g. Vysoké kolo (1509 m), but the time was not on our side and
we decided to stick to the original plan. Our next target - Krakonoš (1422 m) -
the peak carrying the same name as the mountain ridge area.

![The flat road towards the Luční bouda restaurant](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-16.jpg)
![The Luční bouda restaurant with Luční hora (1555 m) in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-17.jpg)

We went down to Dom Śląski and continued to the Luční bouda restaurant, a giant
restaurant and hotel on the mountaintop plateau, visible from all surrounding
summits. We decided to skip the restaurant and head to the peak first. By this
point, the flow of tourists stopped, and we only met occasional pairs of more
eager hikers.

![Route signs on the way to Krakonoš (1422 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-18.jpg)

We traversed through the snow and the ice slush, trying to stick to the
outlined trails, and eventually reached the Krakonoš (1422 m) peak. It was much
different from Sněžka (1603.3 m), being closed to what you'd typically see in
Bavaria, like the Großer Traithen (1852 m) ridge I [visited in 2025](/hikes/2025-01-26-grosser_traithen/).

![The view along the ridge of Krakonoš (1422 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-19.jpg)

A steep slope to the sides with bare sections on the way down, a thin ridge
lowering into the valley, small spruce bushes near the top holding a layer of
snow, making it dangerous to walk closer to the edge. Some footsteps led
further down the ridge, but we saw no point following them.

![The way down from Krakonoš (1422 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-20.jpg)
![The spruce forest further down the slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-21.jpg)

After getting a snack while enjoying the views, we turned back and headed down.
Another interesting point to mention is the appearance of the mountains in the
area. In Bavaria in the pre-Alps region, you'll typically find forested slopes
for most of the routes, with smaller trees and bushes near the top. The hills
are covered in grass and shrubs, making it a great place to graze for local
mountain goats. In Austria, the higher peaks are much more rocky, with bushes
covering the lower sections of the slopes and the higher parts being covered
in gravel and other small rocks, being a perfect for landslides and avalanches.

![Frozen water stream on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-22.jpg)
![The advanced Czech trail markings showing the way up](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-23.jpg)

Here, however, there was a mix of everything. Tons of little stream sources
carving away the rocks and joining into Bílé Labe and other rivers. Small and
big rocks with a greenish tint, rich in earth metals and covered in moss.
Trees and bushes covering the rest of the slope, shrubs and various ferns
filling in the gaps. Most of the paths were paved with big stones or wooden
platforms, ice and snow covering them. Although the hike was quite long, I
still feel like I could spend much longer there, taking in the views and the
change of scenery.

![Ferns and bush trunks near the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-24.jpg)
![An avalanche warning at the start of the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-25.jpg)

On the way down, I was wondering where all the tourists went. The route was
mostly empty, and we didn't have to overtake anyone for quite a while. But
soon after, once we got closer towards the Bílé Labe restaurant hut, we
started coming across mostly German hiking groups going up or down. That
answered my earlier question. It seems the more local hikers have already
concluded their routes for the day, while the Germans continued, being used to
longer tracks. Approaching the restaurant we saw a snowmobile, which brought
back memories from my hike to Sonnenberg (1622 m) where I [lost](/hikes/2026-02-15-oberammergau_sonnenberg/)
and on a later attempt [found my hiking pole](/hikes/2026-03-22-unterammergau_sonnenberg/).

![A snowmobile near the Bílé Labe restaurant hut](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-26.jpg)
![Berry cake in the Bílé Labe restaurant hut](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-27.jpg)

When we got to the hut, I asked Mikuláš to stop there for a meal, since I would
feel miserable missing out on local cuisine. We bought some česnečka, a garlic
soup with chunks of meat, sausage, and vegetables. It was amazing, much more
filling than the Bavarian Alpine hut soups, and having much deeper flavors. To
finish the meal, we ordered some berry cake with some jam on top. A great treat
for any visitor, I highly recommend [the place](https://www.boudabilelabe.cz).

![The interior of the Bílé Labe restaurant hut](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-28.jpg)
![Bílé Labe waterfall on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-29.jpg)

Since we took our time in the hut and on the peaks, the rest of the way was in
a hurry. We kept walking as fast as we could to reach the last bus at 6 PM. At
one point we assumed we were already there, but it was just a glitch in the
navigation app. We still had more than a kilometer left. In the end, we got to
the bus, did our stretches, and enjoyed the drive back to Prague.

![A WWII bunker near Špindlerův Mlýn](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-30.jpg)
![A bridge across Bílé Labe in Špindlerův Mlýn](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/snezka_krakonos/2026-04-11-31.jpg)

To conclude, this is a great area for hiking and the route we took is a great
introduction to the highest ridges in the Czech Republic. Accessible to
families and somewhat accessible even to disabled people, this is a must-visit
for any enthusiast. Especially if you're used to the Alps, this area offers a
nice change of pace. The abundance of local huts and restaurants allows to stay
on the ridges longer and enjoy the local dishes and overnight rooms. Finally,
the trails along the river streams cool down the valleys nicely, while the
trees provide the much-needed shade in summer. Go there if you can!
