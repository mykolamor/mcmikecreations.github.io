On my quest to complete the [Hiking Web](/hikes/tag/Web/) I was diligently
going through all named and some unnamed summits in Oberbayern, especially in
the Tegernsee-Schliersee regions. I've had great progress [this year](/hikes/year/2026/),
and with a sick leave under my belt after a doctor's visit, I was ready to
tackle the next challenge: the Krettenburg (1494 m) and Westerberg (1328 m)
summits.

The weather was perfect for a hike, with clear skies and a gentle breeze after
snowfall the day before. I packed my gear, forgetting mittens, and set off. The
route started off nicely with Trautweinweg, a gravel road [used for sledging](/hikes/2024-12-14-spitzingsee_schlittenfahrt/)
in winter. The bus dropped a bunch of hikers off at Spitzingsattel, and I
quickly overtook the others going uphill.

![Trautweinweg up to Obere Firstalm (1369 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-00.jpg)
![Krettenburg (1494 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-01.jpg)

After staying on the forest road for some time, I reached the
Obere Firstalm (1369 m) and started preparing for the first climb of the day:
the cliffs of Krettenburg (1494 m). In the distance, a group of school children
were slowly getting up to [Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/hikes/2025-08-31-brecherspitz/).
I, however, had no time to lose.

![Slope up to Krettenburg (1494 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-02.jpg)
![View from Krettenburg (1494 m) over Spitzingsee towards Taubenstein (1693 m) and Rotwand (1884 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-22.jpg)

From the well-known path to Bodenschneidhaus (1365 m), I took a sharp turn and
started climbing straight up the ridge of one of the Krettenburg (1494 m)
cliffs. There are multiple of them of roughly the same height. The problem is
that all of them are heavily overgrown with pine shrubs, bushes, trees, and
have some really steep, almost vertical sections. I chose the closest of the
cliffs to conquer, aiming for views towards Spitzingsee rather than
Bodenschneid (1669 m). The ascent was steep and challenging, but the view from
the top was worth every step, overlooking Spitzingsee, [Taubenstein (1693 m)](/404/),
and [Rotwand (1884 m)](/hikes/2024-09-29-rotwand/).

![View from Krettenburg (1494 m) down to Obere Firstalm (1369 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-03.jpg)
![View from Krettenburg (1494 m) towards Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-04.jpg)

On the way up and down the cliff, I had to climb both rock formations and
trees, with the latter being more challenging due to the slippery pine needles
covered in snow and wet moss. After some scratches and slips on the way, I was
hurt and freezing, but happy to be back on the path. One of the times I
slipped, my fingers were so cold that I couldn't close them around a branch to
hold myself. Plus the bleeding from the doctor's visit was still there, so I
had to be extra careful and recover before the other climbs of the day.
The Bodenschneid Sommerweg was [as slippery as always](/hikes/2025-12-23-rainerkopf/),
but I managed to not fall down this time.

![View from Bodenschneidhaus (1365 m) up to Rinnerspitz (1611 m) and Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-05.jpg)
![Warming up inside Bodenschneidhaus (1365 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-06.jpg)

When I entered Bodenschneidhaus (1365 m), I was greeted by a warm and cozy
atmosphere. The owner was chatting with some other hikers, while I could barely
string a few words together. Still, I managed to order some coffee and cake
(of course, I forgot my DAV membership card to buy some hiking food), and the
owner lit up the furnace to warm me up and dry my dirty clothes covered in
snow. The smell of freshly brewed coffee and the sight of hikers sharing
stories created a welcoming environment. I took a moment to warm up, rest, and
gather my strength for the next part of the hike. Meanwhile, I was reading some
blog posts from other hikers climbing Krettenburg (1494 m),
Brunnerstein (1243 m) which I ended up skipping, and Westerberg (1328 m). All
of them seemed to have an experience similar to mine (adapted to the season).

![Trail from the Bergwacht hut towards Eckspitz (1386 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-07.jpg)
![View from Eckspitz (1386 m) towards Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-08.jpg)

From there I went past a Bergwacht rescue hut and up to Eckspitz (1386 m), a
small peak covered in spruce trees. Although the view was limited, there was a
perfect trail leading to the top, and [Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/hikes/2025-11-01-bodenschneid/)
was visible from behind the trees. Compared to the cliffs, this was a breath of
fresh air. Turning back, I saw Wasserspitz (1552 m) and the ridge leading to
it, reminding me of [my old adventures with Mountain Carts](/hikes/2025-09-07-wasserspitz/).

![View from the Eckspitz (1386 m) ridge onto Wasserspitz (1552 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-09.jpg)
![Forestry road towards Westerberg (1328 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-10.jpg)

From there I headed down the normal path going to Neuhaus and Schliersee, but
later changed onto a forestry road going past Westerberg (1328 m). Although the
map shows a barely visible "very horrible" path up the slope, I found a much
better maintained trail on the side. Although eventually I left it to climb up
to the summit, it seems to go fully around the Westerberg (1328 m) peak and
lead down to Schindelgraben or Trattengraben streams. I feel like I should
explore this trail in the future and update the map with it.

![Overgrown path up the Westerberg (1328 m) slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-11.jpg)
![View from Westerberg (1328 m) towards Rainerkopf (1463 m), Bodenschneid (1669 m), and Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-12.jpg)

On the top I found a tree trunk with some stashed belongings and a bottle with
some dust and a note inside. I didn't want to take my chances if those were
someone's ashes, so I just observed it from the distance and walked away to
enjoy the views a bit more. I have to say, from all of the lesser-known peaks
I visited this year, this one was one of the most rewarding. It's located
in a perfect spot to have visibility into multiple valleys and towards many
summits.

![Stashed belongings in a tree trunk on the Westerberg (1328 m) peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-13.jpg)

In the direction of the slope I climbed were [Rainerkopf (1463 m)](/hikes/2025-12-23-rainerkopf/),
[Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/hikes/2025-11-01-bodenschneid/), and [Brecherspitz Vorgipfel (1643 m)](/hikes/2025-08-31-brecherspitz/).
To the west, I could see [Wallberg (1722 m)](/404/) and [Lahnenkopf (1416 m)](/404/).
To the east, unmistakable peaks of [Dürnbachwand (1260 m)](/hikes/2026-09-20-duernbachwand/),
[Hirschgröhrkopf (1272 m)](/404/), and [Breitenstein (1622 m)](/hikes/2025-05-18-breitenstein_wendelstein/)
were staring back at me, with [Breitensteinfensterl (1350 m)](/hikes/2025-07-20-breitensteinfensterl/)
appearing from under the treeline.

![Descent from Westerberg (1328 m) towards Brunstkogel (1251 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-14.jpg)
![Overgrown forestry road between Westerberg (1328 m) and Brunstkogel (1251 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-15.jpg)

The rest of the hike was similar to my [Wasenkogel (1211 m) outing](/hikes/2026-10-04-wasenkogel/).
Overgrown but perfectly walkable sections of animal trails and forestry roads
down to the saddle, then a steep climb up to Brunstkogel (1251 m), I noticed
some completely covered forestry tracks on the Brunstkogel (1251 m) slope.
Given that some fallen trees had clean cuts, and the young trees covering the
road were all roughly the same age, I assume that this road was used for
logging at some point in the past, but has been abandoned for a long time.

![Ascent to the summit of Brunstkogel (1251 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-16.jpg)
![Views from the top of Brunstkogel (1251 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-17.jpg)

There were no views at the top because of all the trees. When I climbed down,
I considered also visiting the smaller summits on the way to Schliersee, but I
was already tired and wanted to get back to the train station. The forest road
I got onto and followed down to the valley was well-maintained and easy to walk
on, with occasional clearings in the forest towards [Gindelalmschneid (1335 m)](/404/)
and [Auer Berg (1252 m)](/hikes/2025-10-26-auer_berg/).

![Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m) peaking from behind Hirschgröhrkopf (1272 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-18.jpg)
![View from Krainsberg towards Rainer Berg (1169 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-19.jpg)

One time, I saw a cool mountain in the distance and was confused about what I
was looking at. Later at home I realized that was [Breitenstein Westgipfel (1575 m)](/hikes/2025-07-20-breitensteinfensterl/)
with its characteristic cliffs and paths. As I descended into the valley, the
forest opened up to meadows, and I saw some cows grazing. They also saw me from
a distance and started running towards me, although I didn't have anything to
offer for their curiosity.

![Cows on the meadow near Krainsberg](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-20.jpg)

When I came down into the village of Schliersee, the sun already set, and I had
to hurry to the station. Still, I took one last look at the lake and the
surrounding mountains, enjoying the panorama in front of me: [Hirschgröhrkopf (1272 m)](/404/)
and [Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/hikes/2024-08-31-aiplspitz/) to the south-east,
[Dürnbachwand (1260 m)](/hikes/2026-09-20-duernbachwand/) and [Brecherspitz (1685 m)](/hikes/2025-08-31-brecherspitz/)
to the south-west.

![Schliersee during evenfall; Hirschgröhrkopf (1272 m) and Jägerkamp (1746 m) on the left; Dürnbachwand (1260 m) and Brecherspitz (1685 m) on the right](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/krettenburg_westerberg/2026-10-22-21.jpg)

All in all, I would highly recommend the forest roads of this area for hiking,
as you get amazing views for a relatively low effort. The peaks that I visited
on this trip are definitely not for the average hiker, although they were not
the hardest wild detours I climbed this year. In any case, this was a great
addition to my collection, and the stay at the hut made it even more memorable.
