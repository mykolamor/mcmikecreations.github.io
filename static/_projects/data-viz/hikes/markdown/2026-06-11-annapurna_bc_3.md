With the [second section](/hikes/2026-06-10-annapurna_bc_2/) the day before
being quite challenging, I mentally prepared myself for the upcoming trip. 
[Parlad Dharel](https://www.instagram.com/parlad_dharel/), my guide, assured me
that only the first half of the hike has a steep ascent, the rest is a
"mountain flat" path to the Annapurna Base Camp (ABC).

I was worried about this section, since this is where the elevation sickness
usually kicks in, and the monsoon rains could very well completely cover the
sky and hide all views from us.

In any case, we had our breakfast and set off to the Annapurna Base Camp with
some small stops on the way. I haven't even finished packing up when the rain
started again, making me really upset I'll have to wear the rain coat and would
look goofy in all photos. But it's not like I had a choice.

![The steps towards Deurali](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-02.jpg)
![Mykola posing in front of Deurali](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-05.jpg)
![The view from Deurali towards the valley](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-09.jpg)

The path to Deurali, the next village on the way, was very uneven. We climbed a
lot of steps to a temple under a big rock, then had to climb down and up again
to reach the village.

![The shrine at Hinku Cave](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-04.jpg)
![Deurali with a view of Gangapurna (7455 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-07.jpg)
![A small shrine between Deurali and Hinku Cave](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-10.jpg)

We walked past the Hinku Cave, a historic shelter with a shrine for pilgrims
walking the trek. When I was there, I haven't realized it was a functional
shelter, so I took pictures of the shrine and left without looking deeper into
the cave.

![Cascading waterfall near the Hinku Cave](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-08.jpg)
![Leftover snow from the last winter season](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-12.jpg)
![Himalayan Poppy near the road](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-13.jpg)

The village itself was just a few huts grouped together. It seemed not many
people were staying inside at the time. To my surprise, they had a radio tower,
so I had perfect 4g while taking a break there.

![The waterfall crossing on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-06.jpg)
![A "mountain flat" section of the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-14.jpg)
![A view back towards Deurali](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-15.jpg)

This was our last major resting stop until Machhapuchhare Base Camp (MBC).
There were no more houses on the way, only occasional locals fixing the road or
chopping down weeds. First, we walked across a waterfall near the Rodolfo
Viewpoint. That's the only waterfall that needs manual crossing with no bridge
or planks across it, with signs saying not to stop or take breaks during the
crossing. I have successfully missed those signs and stopped right in the
middle for some photos. Thankfully, nothing bad happened.

![A glacier waterfall hole nobody is allowed to enter](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-16.jpg)
![A local dog near the Machhapuchhre Base Camp (3700 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-17.jpg)
![Parlad looking at the slow hikers behind us](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-18.jpg)

After lots more climbing, we finally reached MBC. The sign for it is much
closer to the main trail compared to the actual camp houses, so you still need
to walk for a few minutes before you can rest. Still, the area already has some
greenhouses and garden-like areas growing cabbages and other vegetables, which
made me at peace knowing I'm back in civilization.

![Wishbone Flower near MBC](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-19.jpg)
![Mykola happy to reach the MBC](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-20.jpg)
![The Machhapuchhre Base Camp (3700 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-24.jpg)

We had lunch at one of the tea houses there, discussing the leftover way in
front of us. From this point onwards, the road is a slow and steady incline,
with no surprises or steep sections. I assumed so high up the vegetation would
be barren, but instead I was happy to see tons of shrubs, flowers, and some
bushes.

![Lali Gurans (Nepali Rhododendron), the iconic national flower of Nepal](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-21.jpg)
![The way from MBC towards ABC](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-22.jpg)

Soon after, we reached the Paradise Pond, also known as the Mirror Pond. Made
from melting snow and rainfall, its water reflects everything around it,
including Machapuchare (6993 m). In my case, one of my worst fears came true
and the whole area got covered in rainy clouds and fog, so there was nothing
but gray sadness reflected in the pond.

![View of the Baraha Shikhar (Annapurna Fang Peak) (7647 m) behind the clouds](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-25.jpg)
![The Paradise Lake reflecting the gray clouds](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-28.jpg)

It wasn't all bad though. I still had hope that the sky will clear up by the
time we reach the Annapurna Base Camp or later in the evening, so I just kept
going. Once we reached the camp, we hanged up our wet covers and clothes to dry
and headed for a snack. Then, Parlad suggested I should have some sleep and
he'll wake me up if the sky clears.

![Nepal Cinquefoil (Potentilla nepalensis) on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-27.jpg)
![The road to ABC](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-29.jpg)

A few hours passed and at 6 PM the guide told me to come out and take my
pictures. There was no chance for the sky to fully clear, but it was good
enough to see all the mountains around. I took my photos, called up the other
European hikers, and we went to the "top" of the camp to look over the ridge
at the glacier area and the slope of the mountain.

![Mykola at the Annapurna Base Camp (4130 m) with a conveniently tied Ukrainian flag](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-31.jpg)
![The Annapurna (8091 m) ridge from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-33.jpg)

It's a weird, surreal experience. You're there staring at the slope. The
glacier looks the same as on the
[Höllental route to Zugspitze (2962 m)](/hikes/2025-09-18-zugspitze_hoellental/),
the rocky bottom of the slope is similar to
[Cima Sophia (2360 m) in the Dolomites](/hikes/2025-12-29-cima_sophia/) while
the snowy slope reminds me of [Frau Hitt (2270 m) in Austria](/hikes/2025-03-23-frau_hitt/).
All peaks that I've been at and connected with.

![View back at Fish Tail/Machhapuchhre (6993 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-34.jpg)

And then it hit me. Yes, all of this looks similar, but where is it? What's the
scale here? At some point I saw a small flag planted near MBC on the slope of
Annapurna III (7555 m). It was easy to spot on a white background, but the
giant flag was like a spec of dust on a big wall. My whole field of view was
taken up by a single mountain far in the distance.

![Numerous passport photos of people visiting ABC](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-32.jpg)

It takes four kilometers to get to the base of the Annapurna ridge. Then four
vertical kilometers to any of the summits, although approaching the ridge from
the south is incredibly technical and hasn't been attempted in a long time. So
the mountains I was seeing in front of me were closer to a distant panorama
than a tangible set of mountains I'm used to climbing. It was like I'm looking
at a picture book in reality, I didn't feel present in the moment at all.

![A field of Himalayan Anemones near the camp](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-30.jpg)

Then there's also the matter of all the dead bodies along the way. There's not
as many of them as on Everest, but enough to wonder if it's worth it. Although
I'm not planning to go up any time soon, maybe ever, I was curious of how the
process normally works. Well, you take 28 days in total, with 4 camp stops
along the climb, overnight stays in villages along the way to the base camp
(on the northern side under the ridge), many acclimatization days and just a
few hours of climbing per day. The elevation and distance per day actually
don't seem all that crazy to me, something I normally do almost every weekend,
but here comes the fun part.

**The lack of oxygen.** When I came to the base camp and got some tea with
cake, I was expecting a delicious meal followed by an enjoyable nap. I got the
food and the drink and by instinct stopped the fork before chomping on the
cake. Despite being hungry and thirsty, for some reason, I couldn't get any
food inside. I took it slow, ate my portion (it was, in fact, tasty), and
headed to sleep feeling a bit dizzy, assuming that was just me being tired from
three consecutive days of hiking.

When I was in bed, as soon as I got into a horizontal position, I got a
migraine. I would turn to one side, spend 15 minutes waiting for the migraine
to subside, fall asleep only for it to come back. I would then turn to the
other side and the cycle would repeat. This continued for the whole time until
Parlad called me outside.

![Himalayan Poppy in full bloom](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-23.jpg)

When I came out, the nausea and dizziness came back, while the migraine
disappeared. Those symptoms weren't as bad, and I've been dizzy while climbing
the [Höllental via ferrata](/hikes/2025-09-18-zugspitze_hoellental/), so I
knew the feeling and could compensate for it. Same with nausea, everyone has it
from time to time. So I managed to enjoy the photoshoot and the views without
problems, had my dinner, and went back to sleep. My question on why it takes so
long to do an actual summit climb got answered at the base camp. Knowing me and
my sensitivity to altitude, without those rest days getting used to the oxygen
levels, I'd probably just collapse.

![Silverweed (Potentilla anserina) near the Paradise Lake](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-26.jpg)

When I fell back asleep in the evening, my body was already undergoing the
acclimatization changes, and by morning I felt fine. There was a tiny hope left
that the sky would clear up at night like it sometimes did during the previous
days, but for my trip that wasn't the case. During breakfast I met a very
peculiar database engineer from Oracle Belgium and talked to him for a bit. In
case you're reading this, reach out to [contact@mykolamor.com](mailto:contact@mykolamor.com)
or to my [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mykola-mor), I'd love to
connect.

![Compilation of the third day of the Annapurna Base Camp hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_3/2026-06-11-35.mp4)

All in all, this was a great experience despite the weather. Although it was
rainy and cloudy during the monsoon season, there were benefits to this time of
the year. The flowers were in full bloom, and I've been told you don't get to
see them in any other season. The waterfalls are full and impressive, while
drying up otherwise. And the snow from the colder seasons already melts by this
point, so you don't have to bring winter clothing or shiver in the tea houses
at night.
