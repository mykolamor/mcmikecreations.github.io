After submitting my paper, I got lucky not having tasks to start on a Monday.
With the [Krettenburg (1494 m) hike](/hikes/2026-10-22-krettenburg_westerberg/)
being under my belt from last time and not going that great (with me being cold
after the cliff ascent), I wanted a safer experience. I remembered David from
my [Tegelbergsteig via ferrata climb](/hikes/2026-09-27-tegelbergsteig/) taking
the route up to Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) with his mom, so I knew it was
doable.

The main target was set. Given the peaks nearby and on the ridge were still
unexplored by me, it was a great chance to visit them too. The only problem was
time. I had table tennis practice in the evening and even with my best efforts
didn't have time to go home and change, thus, I took all 10 kilograms of spare
clothes and equipment with me on this hike.

The train arrived in Tegernsee without delays and the tourists, locals, and
hikers started pouring out of it. I was really surprised how many people came to
the lake on a gray working day, but what could I say, I was one of them.

![Chapel Schneekapelle near Tegernsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-00.jpg)

Quickly leaving the town, I entered the slope going up to
Gindelalmschneid (1335 m) and started gaining altitude. There were both locals
and tourist hikers on the route, some bringing their cars to the parking lot
under the mountains.

![A look back at Tegernsee from the Pfliegeleck (1065 m) slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-01.jpg)

Now that I walked on asphalt for some time, I turned to a small path near
Schneekapelle and headed to the first summit, Pfliegeleck (1065 m). The trail
there is great; not too steep, with good markings, absolutely perfect to walk
your dog. I didn't feel that great though, experiencing the weight of all my
equipment on my back.

![View from Pfliegeleck (1065 m) towards Tegernsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-02.jpg)

Without even realizing how close I was, I turned a corner and appeared out of
the bushes near the summit cross of Pfliegeleck (1065 m). Although the
relatively small mountain was covered in trees, there was a small opening
where I came from giving a glimpse of Tegernsee in the distance. I didn't have
much time to enjoy it though, as many more summits and other sports were waiting
for me.

![Road from Pfliegeleck (1065 m) towards Kleintegernseer Berg (1106 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-03.jpg)

The road to the next summit, Kleintegernseer Berg (1106 m), was really nice. A
proper gravel road with no real destination, as it ends at Pfliegeleck (1065 m),
going past the upcoming mountain, Berggasthaus Riederstein (1080 m), and all
the way down to Tegernsee. It was definitely used in logging, but maybe also for
bikes? The peak itself was not even visible, with no markings to define it. The
map also showed a bench near it, but I couldn't find it with my eyes wide open.

![Coming up to Berggasthaus Riederstein (1080 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-04.jpg)

From there, I walked to Berggasthaus Riederstein (1080 m) with a lonely tourist
taking a break there looking at the Tegernsee panorama. While the main road, as
I said before, leads down to Tegernsee, hiking route 686 continues on a smaller
trail to Riederstein (1207 m), a cliff overlooking Tegernsee with a chapel on
top and a pilgrimage route towards it.

![Riedersteinkapelle on top of the Riederstein (1207 m) cliff](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-05.jpg)

As I was walking up, all the hikers from neighboring routes and peaks flocked
together to visit the chapel. Although I wasn't sure, I had a gut feeling I'd be
seeing them on the subsequent mountains too. Walking up the stairs to the chapel
was surprisingly difficult with the backpack on, but I just kept going and soon
after reached the views. There were two tourists taking photos at the top, and
we helped each other make the day more memorable.

![View from Riederstein (1207 m) towards Rottach-Egern](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-06.jpg)

From there the trail to Rohrkopf (1316 m) got much smaller and less maintained.
I had to start paying attention to the tree roots under my feet, while also
keeping a good pace to finish the hike under 5 hours. While the original planner
on [LigMapper](/404/) promised me 4.5 hours, it didn't take detours to optional
peaks into account, so I knew the real trek would be longer and harder.

![The forest clearing on Rohrkopf (1316 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-07.jpg)

After passing a trailrunner and some hikers ascending the same trail, I reached
Rohrkopf (1316 m) and looked around. It was hardly a summit, with a small stone
denoting a point on the administrative boundary. Still, a whole group of hikers
came up to me from nowhere asking for directions to Baumgartenschneid (1448 m),
and I showed them the way. Theoretically, I could have joined them, but I
couldn't afford slowing down.

![Final stretch of the trail towards Baumgartenschneid (1448 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-08.jpg)

The remainder of the trail to Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) was quite easy. First,
the path continued through the forest, but soon after we reached a meadow that
stretched across the whole mountain, making it a great target for
Instagram-oriented tourists. While I was taking a break and the previous hiker
group somewhere behind me was taking photos, a pair of faster hikers overtook me
and reached the summit first, sitting down on a bench there to enjoy the views.

![The pair of hikers enjoying the Tegernsee panorama](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-10.jpg)

![The slope of Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) with Tegernsee in the background](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-09.jpg)

I've seen plenty of the scenery before, but I still stopped in awe of the
breathtaking expanse in front of me. Three years of hiking; three years of
(mostly unintentional) peak bagging between Lenggries, Tegernsee, and
Schliersee; all of it was in front of me. Although I still get some of the
smaller Tegernsee mountains confused, it was nice to recall the recent
adventures to [Wasenkogel (1211 m)](/hikes/2026-10-04-wasenkogel/),
[Semmelberg (1101 m)](/hikes/2026-05-17-rechelkopf_semmelberg/), and
[Zwergelberg (1113 m)](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/).

Knowing I'll be back, I left my backpack closer to the actual on-map summit of
Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) and headed to the first optional peak of this trip,
namely Lahnenkopf (1416 m). I read online that this detour is quite easy,
requiring just normal hiking skills and a bit of forest traversal. Knowing my
luck, I was prepared for much worse conditions, but the route was truly easy.

![View from Lahnenkopf (1416 m) towards Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-11.jpg)

First, I descended a moderate slope down to a meadow you can find on the map.
There's a faint trail leading down, but you might just as well find a way for
yourself. I was trying to follow it, but looking back at the recorded GPX, I was
off quite often. From the meadow, you just continue walking uphill in the same
direction, enter the forest on the slope, and soon after reach the top. It's
that easy. The views from the top are not too bad and not too great. A small
glimpse at Schliersee and some views of Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m). I came back
the same way and picked up my backpack, untouched by the numerous hikers walking
by. My next target was the aforementioned Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m), with only a
single entry on hikr.org.

![View from Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) towards Gindelalmschneid (1335 m) and Auer Berg (1252 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-12.jpg)

![Field gentians on the slope of Baumgartenschneid (1448 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-13.jpg)

Although the path down from Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) to Baumgartenalm (1368 m)
is marked as T2 online, I find it hard to believe. A steep trail made of sand
and gravel quickly escaping from under your feet. Mud in autumn and snow in
winter, with no alternative trails nearby. But after you get to the hut, the
path becomes more clear and safe to walk on, albeit slippery due to the mud. I
continued all the way to a rain shelter at the crossroads leading to
Gindelalmschneid (1335 m); that's where the ascent towards
Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m) begins.

![View from Baumgartenalm (1368 m) towards Wendelstein (1838 m) and Breitenstein (1622 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-14.jpg)

Along the way, among the trees, I saw a rare view all the way towards
[Wendelstein (1838 m)](/hikes/2025-06-10-wendelstein/). Now, this probably
doesn't surprise you, since one can see it from a lot of high places around
Schliersee and Tegernsee. But with so many summits covered in trees, this was
really an unusual sight. Anyway, back to the trek. I left my backpack at the
shelter unsupervised again, and started climbing the slope. At the beginning, it
was overgrown with random bushes and small trees fighting for sunlight. Soon
after, the slope cleared up and was occupied only by larger trees and
fallen-over logs. Although I had a great time climbing, I was wondering about my
way down.

![The overgrown top of Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-15.jpg)

When I reached the summit, I saw absolutely nothing. The trees weren't that
dense, but the fact that other trees down the slope were also high enough to
occupy my vision meant I won't get any panoramic sights from this spot. I then
remembered about a cross on a cliff right near this summit, and decided to head
there and investigate. Sure enough, the views were there all along!

![The cross on the cliff right next to Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-16.jpg)

![View from the Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m) cliff](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-17.jpg)

I got to the cliff, found a small trail between the pine bushes leading to the
cross on top of it, and took in the views. Although not much, I could see
Gindelalmschneid (1335 m) and some huts from the nearby villages, so I was more
than satisfied. I then discovered the peak log book and decided to write my
greeting in there too. It startled me a bit that the book had almost daily
entries, but the Hikr website barely had a single visit on it.

![The steep descent from Kreuzbergköpfel (1273 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-18.jpg)

The way down wasn't so fun though. Occasionally, I could see something similar
to a trail going down, and I would follow it, but ultimately I was just climbing
down a steep slope. I knew there was a proper trail wrapping around this hill
somewhere below, and a proper wide gravel road even further down, but I had to
focus on the here and now to not slip.

When I got down to the trail and started making my way back to the shelter, I
got taken aback by how crappy the trail was. It was very narrow, with some
chunks already falling down the extremely steep slope towards the river below.
A few times, I had to climb over fallen down trees, making this even more
dangerous. On my visit, the trail was at least dry, but I'd say it's very
dangerous after rain- or snowfall.

After getting my stuff back at the shelter, I headed down the somewhat popular
trail towards Prinzenweg and down to Schliersee. I've met an elderly hiker on
her phone, another elderly couple slowly walking to the village while enjoying
views, and some younger hikers returning after their adventures.

![View from Schliersee towards Huberspitz (1051 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-19.jpg)

![The obligatory view from the Schliersee beach towards Anklspitz (1112 m) and Jägerkamp (1746 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/2026-10-26-20.jpg)

As usual, I noticed I was quite late for my train, so I had to rush all the way
to the village, getting myself extra tired before my sports training. Still, I
enjoyed this hike. The autumn colors made it a pastel wonderland, and the views
from Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) were some of the best I've seen close to
Tegernsee. I definitely recommend readers to visit at least some of these peaks
and see the panorama for yourself.
