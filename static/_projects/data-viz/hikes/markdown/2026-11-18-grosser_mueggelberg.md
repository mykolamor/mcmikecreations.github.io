Usually, I skip hikes like these, or combine them with other hikes in the area.
The following route is an exception to this rule, for the sake of achieving
something most of my colleagues didn't even dare to attempt.

I was on a trip to Berlin for the [Hatsune Miku Expo 2026 concert](https://mikuexpo.com/europe2026/)
together with my good friend and TUM colleague Tobias Götz, who also joined the
[TUM visit to Wendelstein (1838 m)](/hikes/2025-06-10-wendelstein/)
that I organized last year.
After the event itself, we had more than half a day to spend in the city, so I
suggested completing a Komoot challenge and visiting the highest peak of
Bundesland Berlin.

We had our breakfast in the hotel, took an S-Bahn and headed to the Müggelberge
forest, changing to a bus along the way. It had rained the day before, so I was
expecting the forest to be muddy, but it was quite fine. We spent almost
90 minutes commuting, and finally were ready to set off.

![Tobias exhaustedly pointing at the road to Großer Müggelberg (115 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-00.jpg)
![The cross at the summit of Großer Müggelberg (115 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-01.jpg)

We took the forest road up to the Großer Müggelberg (115 m) summit, taking some
longer detours instead of the direct shortcut to avoid getting our feet wet or
dirty. The whole way up takes around 20–30 minutes depending on the pace, but
the elevation difference is minuscule compared to other hikes on this website,
so I'd say it is very doable regardless of your fitness level.

![A radio tower near the summit giving good internet connection during the whole hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-02.jpg)
![Observatory and television tower building near Großer Müggelberg (115 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-03.jpg)

From the summit, we decided to take a longer road down, going past the
Teufelssee (34 m) lake and towards a different bus stop. We first walked past
a nearby TV and radio tower, followed by the respective control building, also
doubling as an observatory. Around this point we started to hurry up,
since the road back to the main station was going to take more time than we
had hoped for.

![View towards Müggelsee from the viewpoint](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-04.jpg)
![A playground hut near Teufelssee (34 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-05.jpg)

The trail from the viewpoint towards Teufelssee (34 m) was way steeper than the
rest of the route. We had to descend carefully, trying not to slip on the autumn
leaves. Eventually, we got to the bottom and headed past the lake towards the
exit of the forest. Although we took longer than initially planned, we had a
great time there, and even enjoyed some trivia printed on boards near the lake.
Did you know that the Berlin Alps were shaped by a glacier during one
of the previous ice ages? I wonder if we'd be able to find one of the acorns
the ice age squirrel hid around here.

![View from the lake shore (specifically Teufelsseemoor Köpenick)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-06.jpg)
![A prehistoric ship on a playground near the exit of the forest](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/grosser_mueggelberg/2026-11-18-07.jpg)

From there, we took the road out of the forest past a playground, walked to the bus stop and left in time for our train back. If you're in the area and want to see some nature, this is a good place to visit in any season. Enjoy!
