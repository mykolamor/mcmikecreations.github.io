The original plan when I [hiked to Setzberg (1706 m)](/hikes/2026-01-11-setzberg/) was to attempt
Risserkogel (1826 m). Back then, when I got to the emergency shelter on the
saddle, the route forward seemed too risky. Knowing myself and my inability to
turn back and give up, I chose the easier option of going towards
[Wallberg (1722 m)](/404/). This time, I'm back for revenge!

![Horses in Kreuth](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-00.jpg)

The morning bus was more crowded than usual, with many hikers enjoying the
mountains for the first time since last year. All of them, however, left at the
Wallberg (1722 m) cable car station, so my route up was lonely and tranquil.

![A stream on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-01.jpg)

As I approached Setzbergeck (1179 m), I was blocked off by a Wildschongebiet
sign both on the way up and down towards the summit. I had to make a detour
onto the hiking trail, but it turned out great, with shade from the forest and
greenery below.

![The blocked road towards Setzbergeck (1179 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-03.jpg)

It was already warm on the hillside, but the usual stuffiness of summer air
wasn't there yet. The dust from the ground and the evaporating juices of the
grass make up quite a challenge on the hottest days, but for now I was able to
enjoy the route without facing it.

![Wood Anemone near the road](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-02.jpg)

I saw quite a few mountain bikers on the tiny trail I stepped on. Most riding
downhill, but some even riding up there. Last time I saw a mountain biker was
on [Simetsberg (1840 m) near Walchensee](/hikes/2024-09-07-simetsberg/).
Back then, when I saw the trembling arms and the veiny legs, I knew that it
would take me years to do anything comparably difficult.

The next stop on my way was Daffenstein (1546 m). It's a bare rock in the
middle of a steep slope, not secured with any via ferrata cables except for two
ropes leading up to the start of the climbing route.

![View from under Daffenstein (1546 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-04.jpg)

I took one of the ropes and climbed as high as I could without equipment
(within reason), took some photos, and climbed back down. I really wanted to
see the cross they allegedly had at the top, so I hiked around the rock and
found the cross on the other side, accessible without equipment but with some
climbing to do.

I left my belongings and climbed there, taking some photos and quickly climbing
back down again before the adrenaline wears off. It was time to continue
towards the saddle, and the map was telling me to go all the way down the steep
slope towards the popular trail. I decided against it and kept pushing upward.

![The lower cross at Daffenstein (1546 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-05.jpg)

The trails around the rock were very faint, with goat droppings, so probably
not man-made. At that point, I didn't care. I just wanted to get back to the
normal hiking route.

![The slope where I got burried under the snow in winter](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-06.jpg)

Going up and leaning towards the main trail, I passed the places I dug through
with knee-deep snow on my way to [Setzberg (1706 m)](/hikes/2026-01-11-setzberg/).
It was surreal to see them without the pile of snow and the cold embrace of
winter, but I didn't want to reminisce for too long, since I haven't done even
half of the planned hike.

![View from the saddle towards Risserkogel (1826 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-07.jpg)

When I arrived at the saddle, two local hikers resting there told me that the
route towards Risserkogel (1826 m) is still quite dangerous, and the route towards
Spitzingsee from the peak is even worse. I wanted to see it for myself.
Usually, I take risks like that for the sake of a more interesting adventure,
but after all my injuries, I try to stay on the safer side.

![The rescue hut at the saddle where I rested in winter](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-08.jpg)

While I was talking to them, a much bigger and less prepared group came by and
said that the route is completely fine if you have hiking sticks. With the
newfound motivation, I set off to the peak of Grubereck (1664 m), which was on
the way to my goal.

![The flock of paragliders around Wallberg (1722 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-10.jpg)

In the meantime, all the paragliders around Wallberg (1722 m) have also set off
on their journeys, filling the sky with colorful parachutes. It was a great
sight to behold, and a nice view with Tegernsee in the background to send to my
parents.

![The sign near the top of Grubereck (1664 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-09.jpg)

The road quite quickly got covered in snow that hasn't melted since winter. It
wasn't anything dangerous though, with tons of people passing me on their way
down. The summit of Grubereck (1664 m) only had a plaque describing the
mountain and the area, so I didn't wait for too long and continued my hike.

![View from the ridge towards Halserspitz (1862 m) and Guffertspitze (2194 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-11.jpg)

After that, the 30 minutes to the summit of Risserkogel (1826 m) were
uneventful. The trail is a bit steeper there, but nothing a moderately fit
person can't manage.

![View from Risserkogel (1826 m) towards Blankenstein (1768 m) and Wallberg (1722 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-12.jpg)

The greatest benefit of this route is the scenery you have around. Towards
north, you have Blankenstein (1768 m), [Setzberg (1706 m)](/hikes/2026-01-11-setzberg/)
and [Wallberg (1722 m)](/404/).
All frequently visited by groups of hikers. In the distance, closer to
Tegernsee, you have the paragliders and the glider planes flying around.
Towards east, you're greeted by [Roßkopf (1579 m)](/hikes/2024-09-21-rosskopf/)
and [Stümpfling (1506 m)](/hikes/2025-09-07-wasserspitz/),
and the saddle in-between leading to Spitzingsee. To the south, check out
[the Blauberge ridge, including Halserspitz (1862 m)](/hikes/2025-06-01-blauberge/).
And right behind them, already in Austria, you can spot Guffertspitze (2194 m).
Towards west, [Buchstein (1701 m)](/hikes/2025-10-05-buchstein_only/),
[Roßstein (1698 m)](/hikes/2025-05-11-buchstein/),
and [Leonhardstein (1452 m)](/404/)
stand out among others with their sharp peaks and steep inclines.

![View from Risserkogel (1826 m) towards Stümpfling (1506 m) and Roßkopf (1579 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-13.jpg)

If you're lucky, on the road towards Risserkogel (1826 m) you can spot wild
chamois, mountain eagles, alpine choughs and other animals. The only downside
are the ant hills. If you stop in the wrong place for even a moment, you'll be
bitten for sure! At least I was...

![View from Risserkogel (1826 m) towards Bayerischer Schinder (1796 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-14.jpg)

When I reached the summit of Risserkogel (1826 m), the only people there were a
pair of foreign hikers with a Labrador. They really wanted to go down to
Wildbad Kreuth, but had no idea which route to take. I gave them some basic
directions and offered to descend together, since going alone started getting a
bit boring. They wanted to stay at the summit a bit longer, and I had a bus to
catch, so we parted ways.

![View from the Risserkogel (1826 m) slope towards Hinteres Sonnwendjoch (1986 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-15.jpg)

My initial idea was to go north-east towards Spitzingsee. Peaking over the
slope on the summit, I saw the steep trail and the overabundance of snow on the
route ahead, so I switched my plan to also go to Wildbad Kreuth instead.

![View from the hillside towards Wildbad Kreuth](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-16.jpg)

The trail was quite steep at first, but soon after it got better and the bushes
opened up to reveal the panorama of the Austrian Alps just across the German
border. I distinctly remember my fascination with that view from
[my hike to Blauberge](/hikes/2025-06-01-blauberge/), when all the mountains
were covered in dried-up grass and ferns. This time, the Alps were still
covered in snow, offering a new unique perspective I haven't seen before. At
this point, I can be certain the view there is great in any season, as long as
it's not fully covered in clouds.

![A chamois on the slope](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-17.jpg)

From this point onwards, I followed the trail down to the crossroads leading up
to Grubereck (1664 m). There were some nice streams and tiny waterfalls around,
but the main attractions were still the views towards Austria.

![Spring Gentians on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-18.jpg)

At the crossroads I met a hiker I've come across on my way to
Risserkogel (1826 m). As he greeted me, his foot slipped off a loose rock, and
he tumbled to the side. After checking up on him, we started going down
together, but he quickly stopped and I continued on my own.

![Crossroads towards Bernaueralm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-19.jpg)
![Crossroads near Ableiten Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-20.jpg)

While the panorama in the distance didn't change much as I was going down, the
surrounding scenery did. The higher sections had bushes and dry grass from last
year, and I slipped a few times on the dry dusty gravel trail leading me down.

![A Wald-Wild-Schongebiet Stopp sign](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-21.jpg)

As I got lower, the spruce trees got higher and mostly replaced the bushes. The
trail was covered in shade and there was a pleasant breeze helping me stay
fresh. Soon after, I started seeing trees with leaves, and the gravel trail got
replaced by a proper wider dirt road. 20 or so minutes later, I came down to a
big dirt road coming from deep in the mountains towards the asphalt road.
Instead of walking on it, I stayed on a smaller trail going parallel to it,
having flowers, greenery, and shade unlike the bigger road.

![The mixed forest hillside near Riedlerspitz (1429 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-22.jpg)
![Scheuereralm on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-23.jpg)

Near the bus stop, I discovered I had to wait for an hour and decided to visit
the local Schwaiger Alm. I got 0.3l of Almdudler for 4.50€ and a decent piece
of Apfelstrudel for 7€. Make of the prices what you will, but the food was
quite ok.

![A bridge in the woods near Schwaiger Alm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-24.jpg)

After my dinner break, I headed towards the bus stop parking lot across the
smaller trail going near a waterfall and a small swimming area underneath it.
It seems very tempting to visit it in summer, but the mountain stream water
will probably not be that much warmer compared to now.

![A waterfall with a swimmable area below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/risserkogel/2026-04-26-25.jpg)

The bus stop itself was also surprisingly nice, with a small picnic area right
next to it, and an asphalt parking spot for drivers too afraid to drive onto
the gravel. There was a random sports car parked there, so the reasoning
checked out.

All in all, I highly recommend this route. Despite what some others say, the
trail from Kreuth to Risserkogel (1826 m) is very approachable in the warm
seasons. The views it offers and the experience you get are irreplaceable, so
put it on your to-do list! As long as you're moderately fit and have decent
hiking shoes, this route is for you. The route doesn't require any other
equipment, although hiking poles are a plus.

From the top, you have plenty of options for the descent. You can either go to
Kreuth Wildbad, back to Kreuth Rathaus from where the route starts, to
Spitzingsee over Roßkopf (1579 m) or to Rottach-Egern over Setzberg (1706 m)
and Wallberg (1722 m). I highly recommend the last option, as it offers the
best views and some interesting educational plaques along the way. It takes
around an hour to hike between Grubereck (1664 m) and Wallberghaus
(which closes at 17:00). From there you have plenty of options, including
buggies, a cable car, paragliding, or just going down to the Wallberbahn
bus stop.
