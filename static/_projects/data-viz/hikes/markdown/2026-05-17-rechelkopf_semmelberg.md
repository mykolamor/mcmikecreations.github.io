Ever since I did the [trip to Blomberg (1248 m)](/hikes/2026-03-15-bichl_blomberg/),
I wanted to do another hike in the same area, since there were so many small
summits to choose from. I chose them all! I decided to do a hike from
Rechelkopf (1330 m) to Semmelberg (1101 m), visiting all other peaks along.

![Obergries bridge over Isar](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-00.jpg)

I briefly looked at the map and saw that some peaks were missing roads leading
up to them. Remembering my recent [Via Nova trail experience](/hikes/2026-05-14-neufahrn_niederbayern_eggmuehl/),
I wasn't too worried about it. I assumed that there were trails not marked on
the map, made by tractors after dragging chopped down trees or cleared out
ferns. Although I had 3 vaccines two days before, I was confident I could
complete this route in my condition.

![View from the Schürfen Kopf (1096 m) slope towards Brauneck (1555 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-01.jpg)

Following my "Web of Hikes" idea, I started the route leaning a bit to the
west. I passed a nearby bridge during my [Bad Tölz to Lenggries](/404/) hike,
so I wanted to pass it again this time around to connect the routes together.
Next, I headed straight east across the fields and the horse farms towards the
first summit.

![The cross at Schürfen Kopf (1096 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-02.jpg)

Although there is an unnamed 861 m mountain nearby, my choice fell onto
Schürfen Kopf (1096 m). I started the hike a bit later in the day, so the trail
leading up was full of people, mostly older hikers enjoying the weather and
younger trail runners setting their personal best paces. The cross for the
mountain is located slightly to the side of the panoramic view area.

While the mountain itself is nothing special, it offers great views towards
Zwiesel (1348 m), the highest peak near the Blomberg ridge. There were a bunch
of people with dogs enjoying the clear sky, smiling towards me when I got to
the top. I greeted them and kept going.

![The cross at Schwarzköpfel (1140 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-03.jpg)

The next summit was Schwarzköpfel (1140 m), a random hilltop in-between other
mountains. There were some warnings to stay on the trail when I was approaching
it, which I did, but there wasn't much sense getting off the trail anyway.
There was a simple handcrafted cross made of two sticks at the top, which I
quickly passed and kept going.

After I went down to Schwaiger-Alm, I met two confused elderly hikers trying to
figure out the route. I had my headphones on in pass-through mode, but I guess
they got intimidated by me and didn't ask for directions. I kept going and soon
reached an opening with a cross and a bunch of people. It was
Rechelkopf (1330 m), the main destination for most hikers in this region. You
can get there from all sides, even from the south through an unmarked trail
created by woodchoppers.

![The view from an opening at Sulzkopf (1279 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-04.jpg)
![The view from an Rechelkopf (1330 m) towards Benediktenwand (1801 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-05.jpg)

Before going there, I took a quick detour to Sulzkopf (1279 m). While there's
nothing there except a bench, I met several hikers with dogs doing some light
trail running, so that was something.

![The cross at Rechelkopf (1330 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-06.jpg)

The view from the Rechelkopf (1330 m) peak is much greater than from the
previous summits. You can see Benediktenwand (1801 m), which
[I visited with Maksym a while ago](/hikes/2024-07-21-benediktenwand/),
and even further beyond! For some reason it snowed the day before in the middle
of May, covering all the mountains in quite a thick layer of snow. I was hoping
that the lower mountains I was visiting on this trip were safe from the frost,
but I started slipping on the snow quite soon. I took some photos trying to
avoid getting caught on other peoples' camera views, took a short break near
the cross, and continued.

![The view from Mitter Berg (1280 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-07.jpg)

There was another "summit" on the same "ridge", clearly just a spot for cutting
down pines. The road to Mitter Berg (1280 m) was covered in tractor tracks,
and I was drowning in mud jumping from one higher spot to another, while trying
to avoid the puddles and random metal spikes. When I got to the highest point,
I wasn't able to see much, and the spot itself wasn't marked. I did it to mark
the peak as visited, then turned back and started heading down to the next
target.

![The road towards Rechelköpfel (1182 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-08.jpg)
![The view from Rechelköpfel (1182 m) towards Rechelkopf (1330 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-09.jpg)

This section of the road was particularly awful. The mud and turf on the way
made me slip and get my clothes dirty. Other hikers I came across had the same
experience. When I turned to Rechelköpfel (1182 m) on the crossroads, the
surface of the road changed. I started seeing a lot of branches and woodchips
mixed with the dirt, and stepping there became much easier. The peak itself
was again nothing special, just a hilltop with a bunch of cut down trees. The
views from it were completely covered by the remaining pines. The road leading
there wasn't marked on the map, so I made a mental note to put it there when
I come home. On the way back I got to see a view of Fockenstein (1564 m),
which I [recently visited](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/)
with a Ukrainian hiking group.

![The road back with a view of Fockenstein (1564 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-10.jpg)
![A tall hunting tower on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-11.jpg)

The next summit, which I had to backtrack to a bit, was Gronetseck (1278 m).
While it was similar to Schwarzköpfel (1140 m), the ferns on the way were very
appealing. Unline the previous summits in a barren pine forest, this one had
bushes and various plants around, so it was much more interesting to get there.

![The trail towards Gronetseck (1278 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-12.jpg)
![The view from Gronetseck (1278 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-13.jpg)

I got a bit lost trying to figure out which side of a barbed wire fence to
take, at the end choosing the wrong one and having to climb over. There weren't
any markings on this summit too, so I just kept going along the trail towards
the next destination.

![The hut near Lucken Kopf (1370 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-14.jpg)
![The flat summit of Kogelkopf (1324 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-15.jpg)

When I reached the hut at the crossroads to Lucken Kopf (1370 m), I got a call
from a colleague needing some quick tech support. I spent a while talking to
him on my one bar of cellular connection. Meanwhile, I had some time to think
about my next summit. Since I was already close, I decided to visit
Kogelkopf (1324 m) on the way. I couldn't find any markings there too, so I
turned back and followed an unmarked tractor road towards Lucken Kopf (1370 m).

![The summit of Lucken Kopf (1370 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-16.jpg)
![The barely visible trail from Lucken Kopf (1370 m) towards Brauneck (1290 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-17.jpg)

While the Lucken Kopf (1370 m) summit didn't have any markings either, I found
traces of a fireplace and a pile of stones where the top was supposed to be.
The area here had a lot of taller ferns, so visually this section was more
enjoyable.

![The summit of Brauneck (1290 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-18.jpg)
![A cable on the way from Brauneck (1290 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-20.jpg)

I can't say the same about the next peak, Brauneck (1290 m). It's again a
hilltop on a ridge in the middle of nowhere, with no views because of trees
blocking the sightline. I found a weird thick cable on the ground though,
could be a communication line or something.

![The view from Spitzeck (1339 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-19.jpg)
![The panoramic view towards Fockenstein (1564 m) near Spitzeck (1339 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-21.jpg)

I passed a municipal boundary stone and continued along the ridge to
Spitzeck (1339 m). Yet again, there was nothing there, but I could see the
surrounding mountains from time to time between the trees. The trail I was
following was getting worse and worse. There were no tree markings or arrow
signs, so I had to closely follow the map and hope for the best. The whole
route in this area goes along a fence, sometimes crossing it, so taking this
trail is a huge pain.

![The views at Huder (1405 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-22.jpg)

Next up I was going to go south, but I spotted Huder (1405 m), the top of a
nearby hill. At first, I was hesitant to go there through wilderness with no
trail, but I spotted someone's footsteps in the snow and just followed them.
The person was hiking straight uphill from Auereck (1113 m). I tried
backtracking their footsteps down to the summit, got lost between the young
pine trees, somehow found the highest point using the map and turned back.

I expected the rest of the trail to the next summit to be nice and visible.
In reality, it was another track near a fence that I climbed under several
times. Soon after, the track ended and I had to find my way in the wild with no
visible trail or paint marks. I got seriously lost here and got my feet wet,
but somehow eventually reached the bottom of the slope leading to
Sattelkopf (1384 m).

![The weird tree at Sattelkopf (1384 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-23.jpg)
![Forget-me-nots (Myosotis) near Sattelkopf (1384 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-24.jpg)

Again, there was no trail to the top. Since I was close and really wanted to
visit this summit, I had to climb uphill on a steep slope. This was by far
the hardest section. Because of all the snow and mud, I kept slipping down,
getting wetter and dirtier. After lots of effort, I eventually got to the
flat hilltop with some weird old trees. For the way down, I found yet another
unmarked trail and followed it down.

![The view from Aueralm (1266 m) towards Hirschberg (1668 m) and Kampen (1607 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-25.jpg)
![The empty bottles at Aueralm (1266 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-26.jpg)

I can't express how overjoyed I was to enter a normal gravel road towards
Aueralm (1266 m). Although the hut itself closed for the day an hour before,
I was still happy to see some civilization. The owners were gathering empty
drink boxes and cleaning the place. I walked around taking photos of the
surrounding mountains, took a break for a snack, and prepared myself to head
down to Bad Wiessee. It was nice to see Hirschberg (1668 m), which I
[visited with a DAAD FK group a while ago](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/).
Kampen (1607 m) on the other side of the valley also brought memories from
a [recent snowy trip](/hikes/2026-03-29-lenggries_auerkamp/),
where I didn't get to see any views from the top.

![The view from Auereck (1113 m) towards Gindelalmschneid (1335 m) and Roßkopf (1579 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-27.jpg)

Although I had an option to take the short route down and finish the hike in
1.5 hours, I settled for a longer option going through 2 more peaks, the first
of them being Auereck (1113 m). Along the way, I got to see some views of
[Gindelalmschneid (1335 m)](/404/)
and [Roßkopf (1579 m)](/hikes/2024-09-21-rosskopf/).

![The views near Auereck (1113 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-28.jpg)

It was yet another forestry road leading along the ridge. This time around, the
area was quite empty and I could walk around the dirtier sections. There were
also occasional markings for bikers and hikers, so I felt much better than
before. The Auereck (1113 m) summit itself wasn't marked, so I kept going to
the next one.

![The cross at Semmel Berg (1101 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-29.jpg)

My last stop was Semmel Berg (1101 m), a peak with an actual cross and the last
point of a tractor road used for bringing wood down the hill. I quickly walked
past it and headed down.

![Sunset on the way to Bad Wiessee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-30.jpg)

The rest of the hike was quite filler, although it was nice going along the
Zeiselbach river. Soon after, I got to Sonnenbichl, a restaurant and a hotel
near a ski slope. It was quite empty at this time of the year. Afterward,
I kept going and finished my hike at a nearby bus stop.

![A lilac bush near Bad Wiessee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-31.jpg)
![A short video edit of the route](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/rechelkopf_semmelberg/2026-05-17-32.mp4)

Despite visiting 15 peaks on this hike, I can't recommend any of them to
others. I can name the more popular options - the hut near Lucken Kopf (1370 m)
with a view towards Reichersbeuern, and Rechelkopf (1330 m) with a view towards
Benediktenwand (1801 m). You can do those as two separate easy hikes, both
accessible by hikers of all ages.
