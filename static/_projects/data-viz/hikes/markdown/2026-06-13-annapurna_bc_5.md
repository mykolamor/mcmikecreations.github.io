After spending the evening in [Chhomrong](/hikes/2026-06-12-annapurna_bc_4/)
and resting after the long journey from the Annapurna Base Camp (4130 m), it
was time to finish the trip. [Parlad Dharel](https://www.instagram.com/parlad_dharel/),
my guide, reminded me of the plan to visit the hot spring on the way to the
parking spot in Samrung.

I was excited to see it, although I wasn't quite sure what the spring rules in
Nepal are. I asked if anything else was planned for the day, but Parlad said
that the plan was to go straight back to Pokhara where I can enjoy the rest of
the day.

![Last look from Chhomrong towards Annapurna South (7219 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-01.jpg)

From the Luck Guest House we walked past the donkeys grazing nearby and started
the descent towards Taulung. I kept glancing back at the mountains trying to
capture the last view of the tallest peaks in the world, being sad to leave
them behind.

![A kitten near the Green View Lodge and Restaurant](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-02.jpg)
![A view from Chhomrong towards Annapurna III (7555 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-03.jpg)

From there the road was familiar and easy. The cellular tower near the
"Excellent View Top Lodge and Restaurant", the huts and tea houses along the
way, the local puppies and kittens. I've seen it all before, but it was still a
pleasure to walk through the hills and enjoy.

We quickly passed Taulung and walked past some locals repairing the roads. They
get granite and other stones right from the mountains and use them for paving
the trails, making the hike to the Annapurna Base Camp feel like a long walk
instead of a trek through the mountains. Next we reached Jhinu Danda and Parlad
told me to leave all things behind, taking only spare socks and underwear with
me.

![A local place of worship near Jhinu Hot Springs](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-05.jpg)

After packing my bag and placing it inside a tea house, we walked through the
back yard and entered a trail leading to the hot spring. There was a small hut
right at the top of the trail with locals charging a small fee for the visit
(which Parlad paid out of the fund we had for the trip). The hot spring was
well-maintained, so I didn't mind the fee. Although the trail looks quite
long, with Parlad estimating it would take us 30 minutes to go down, I think
we made it in 15. On the way we passed an overgrown place of worship, but the
locals were already on the way clearing the trail of weeds and grass, so maybe
they took care of the shrine too.

![Jhinu Hot Springs and the nearby waterfall near Jhinu Danda](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-04.jpg)

The hot spring itself was really nice, with a small showering area and quite a
big pool with hot water. Parlad didn't want to get in having 35°C in the air
and around 40°C in the water, but I was happy to enjoy the spring since I had
been looking forward to one ever since [Parque da Grená on the Azores](/404/)
and [Fujisan in Japan](/404/).

![A shop in Jhinu Danda selling hiking snacks and supplies](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-06.jpg)
![Mykola on the bridge to Samrung](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-08.jpg)

I spent a good amount of time in the hot spring, while the workers nearby
were constructing a gravel road across the waterfall, presumably between
Chhomrong and Himal Pani. They've gotten quite far overall in their endeavor
in both directions, especially past Chhomrong near Tilche. I assume that
sooner or later a road will connect the settlements all the way to Sinuwa.
There's a hydroelectric power plant in Deurali (3200 m), which would also be
cool to connect, but the river there and the surrounding slopes are too steep.

![View from Samrung into the valley](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-07.jpg)

After the hot spring, we walked back up, took our belongings and headed to
Samrung. The road downhill was especially easy, and we quickly reached the
bridge. Knowing this is the end of the trek, I asked Parlad to take a few more
pictures of me on the bridge.

![Contact numbers of lodges and huts on the way to the Annapurna Base Camp](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-09.jpg)

Since I was already there and walked past some informational signs, I took
a picture of the contact numbers of the lodges and huts along the way for
readers interested in the trek. Not sure how many of them will still be valid,
but Parlad was actively using those numbers to book lodging on the same day, so
they're worth a try.

![Compilation of the fifth day of the Annapurna Base Camp hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_5/2026-06-13-00.mp4)

To reiterate, this trek to the Annapurna Base Camp (4130 m) is an amazing
experience, and I highly recommend it to nature enjoyers of all levels. I
personally didn't find it difficult, although I can easily see how it can be
tiring for some. Also, if you have asthma or other respiratory issues, I would
recommend consulting a doctor before heading to Nepal, as even lower 2 km
altitude mountains can be challenging or cause health issues. The trek is well
worth it if you're allowed to try it out, and the views are breathtaking.

And thanks again to [Parlad Dharel](https://www.instagram.com/parlad_dharel/)
and [Outfitter Himalaya](https://outfitterhimalaya.com/) for making this trip
possible and enjoyable. I would be interested in trying out this or other treks
as an organizer instead, but this gave me valuable insight into the local
challenges and the work that goes into making a trek possible.
