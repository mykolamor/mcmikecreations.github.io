Topo from [bergsteigen.com](https://www.bergsteigen.com/touren/klettersteig/ferrata-fortica-omis/):
![Topo of the climb](/images/hikes/topo/omis_fort_climb.png)

After seeing a group of people in harnesses on my
[visit to Tvrđava "Stari grad" (303 m)](/hikes/2025-08-13-omis_fort/)
the day before, I couldn't get the idea of climbing the via ferrata
out of my head. So I decided to return to the Omiš hillside alone the next day
and give it a try. Of course, I only had my trusty socks and sandals and an
Insta360 camera with me, so I had to risk doing the climb without any safety
equipment.

![Basin on the way to the cliff](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_fort_climb/2025-08-14-00.jpg)

The beginning of the climb was a bit hard to find. You need to pass the car
tunnel and then look for a small trail leading up the cliff immediately to the
right of the tunnel exit. Once you find and ascend it, you come across a place
of worship near the start of the actual via ferrata route.

From there, the climb begins in earnest. The route is well-equipped with
metal rungs, cables, and ladders, making it accessible even for beginners
coming from abroad, as I observed from the group I saw the previous day.
The views keep getting better as you ascend, with stunning vistas of the
Brač Channel keeping you company all the way to the top.

![View of the town after the first climbing section](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_fort_climb/2025-08-14-01.jpg)

Regardless of footwear, the climb is not overly easy. It's marked as [grade B](https://www.ferratago.com/fortica-omis/),
and it's easily doable with a safety harness and a helmet. Without them, and
holding my camera in one hand (and occasionally in my mouth), wearing sandals
on the spiky surfaces, I had no room for errors. There were moments when I
had to stop and compose myself before proceeding, especially on the steeper
sections.

The section before the ladder with a slight negative incline was particularly
annoying, as I had to bite the camera to free both hands for climbing. But I
managed to get through it without any mishaps.

![A look deeper into the surrounding mountains](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_fort_climb/2025-08-14-02.jpg)

The last portion of the climb is really nice, not as steep and with a big
flat surface to climb on. Once you reach the top, you're rewarded with a
fantastic view of Omiš, the Adriatic Sea, and the surrounding islands. On the
opposite side, you're protected from winds by the fort walls, with a small
opening to climb through to reach the paid entrance.

Unfortunately, when the lady charging the entrance fee saw my socks and sandals,
she refused to let me in, saying I was reckless and set a negative example for
others. As I had no good arguments to counter her point of view, I had to
accept her decision and leave without exploring the fort itself.

![A look deeper into the surrounding mountains](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_fort_climb/2025-08-14-03.jpg)

The way down was uneventful, retracing my steps from yesterday. This time, I
knew the slippery sections to watch out for, so I could speedrun the descent
without any issues.

![Full via ferrata climb](/404/)

Overall, the via ferrata climb to Tvrđava "Fortica" was an exhilarating
experience, with or without proper gear. The views were spectacular, and the
sense of accomplishment upon reaching the top was well worth the effort.
**Of course, I highly advise anyone attempting this climb to use proper safety
equipment and footwear to ensure a safe and enjoyable experience.**

P.S. Obviously, my mom was not happy when I told her about this adventure later
that day.
