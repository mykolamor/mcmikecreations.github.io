The [first section](/hikes/2026-06-09-annapurna_bc_1/) of the Annapurna Base
Camp trek was quite short and easy, giving me a taste of what was to come.
[Parlad Dharel](https://www.instagram.com/parlad_dharel/), my guide, was very
helpful showing the way, measuring a good light pace, and telling me about the
villages, temples, and waterfalls we came across.

After we finished the first section the day before, all I did was rest and
sleep, so I was excited about the path ahead. We agreed to start quite early,
around 6 AM, because the monsoon rains at noon would kill any pace or progress
we'd gather.

![Flower pots at the Lucky Guest House Hotel in Chhomrong](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-01.jpg)

When I woke up, the sun was still rising over the ridges of nearby mountains.
The whole valley was covered in sun rays and light mist, and the clouds have
dissipated overnight to give us a clear view of Annapurna South (7219 m).

![View from Chhomrong towards Annapurna South (7219 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-00.jpg)

The second day had us leave Chhomrong at 7:00 and start our trek to the
Himalaya village. I had far better rest than I expected, with the mild
temperature and fresh air of the mountains helping my deep sleep. Although
some people report altitude sickness even at this elevation below 2000 meters,
these issues avoided me for now.

![Gumba shrine near Chhomrong](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-02.jpg)

I had a discussion with my guide about his work and the profession in general.
He started being a porter at 16 years old, working as a source of income for
his bachelor's studies and his family. At some point in his third year, he
didn't pass an exam and dropped out, focusing on being a porter full-time.

After working for 4 years and being a guide to popular destinations too, he was
allowed to do a 45-day Sherpa course and pass an exam to become certified. As
he explained, the government has different exams and certifications for
trekking guides, jungle experts, city guides, etc. He passed the exam and
started working for a tourist company, otherwise he would have had to go
trekking for another 2 years before he'd be allowed to take the exam again.

![Rice fields near the Chhomgrong Khala river](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-03.jpg)

We quickly descended from Chhomrong to the Tilche village on the other side of
the river, waited for some mules to pass and started going up.

![View back to the valley from the Tilche village](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-04.jpg)

The road here is also very well-kept, with paved stones and occasional support
rails all the way. We then reached Sinuwa, our potential hotel for the night on
the way back. This is the point where the mules stop, as the road ahead is
closer to a forest trail and is not that comfortable for animals to pass
through.

![View forwards towards Machapuchare (6993 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-05.jpg)
![A Sinuwa cat](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-09.jpg)

Nevertheless, there is a horse carriage service you can pay for that goes all
the way to Bamboo, Dovan, or even Himalaya. Along the way, most of the time
you can see Machapuchare (6993 m), also known as Fishtail for its shape.

On our way, we passed a small stone bench area with mantras hanged up overhead.
I was explained that the different colored "flags" all have the same prosperity
prayer, and walking under them bathes you in spiritual blessings, bringing
good fortune and positive energy.
We took a small break and continued towards the next village. Sinuwa is the
last point you can reliably buy stuff, as the following villages and tea
houses don't get goods delivered that often.

![Resting spot near Sinuwa](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-06.jpg)

Next up was a long trek to Bamboo village. We passed quite a few bridges over
waterfalls on the way. The road here is less steep and goes through a shaded
mixed forest with lush trees and bamboo groves.

In good weather you can see Gangapurna (7455 m) most of the time. In our case
it was cloudy, but instead we got nice views of clouds passing by in the
valley of the nearby rivers and over the mountain ridges.

![Parlad leading me through the forest](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-08.jpg)

We stopped in Bamboo village for a tea break and some snacks. That's when I
noticed the power plugs they had around. Of course, Nepal like India operates
on a weird permutation of European plugs, but in the mountains they also had
USB-A sockets along the rest. If you have a recent device cable with USB-C,
make sure to get a converter!

It's also important to have some small change in the local Nepal currency to
pay for water, food, and drinks. A liter of water is around 250 Nepal Rupees,
drinks are between 150 and 350, and food is 500 to 800.

![View from the helicopter pad at the Bamboo village](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-07.jpg)

The last reachable helicopter pad is located right next to the Bamboo village.
If someone gets injured on the way, including locals, they either go here, to
one of the base camps, or trek all the way back to Samrung.

![Apparently a "Nice bathing" waterfall](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-10.jpg)

After the break in the Bamboo village, we kept going for a bit more and reached
Dovan. The path between is quite comfortable, but has many ups and downs, so
you have to prepare your legs for quite some exercise.

![Rain catching me and some British blokes off-guard](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-11.jpg)

This section features more nice waterfalls, especially those falling 100s of
meters from the ridges above. My guide also asked if everything was fine with
the stay in Chhomrong last night, since many older tourists usually write
complaint letters after the trek is over saying they expected other conditions
or disliked something. Be nice to your guide!

![Cake shop in Dovan](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-12.jpg)
![AI slop on a Dovan cafe menu](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-13.jpg)

Dovan itself is split into several smaller sections. We walked past the lower
ones, went up some more steps and took the lunch break at Greenland guest
house. Around this point the monsoon rain came, and we had to think about what
to do next.

![Pojenhyam Baraha Than Temple under heavy rain](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-14.jpg)

We didn't have much time to think, so we put on our rain gear and headed to the
next village. I even forgot my water bottle there.

When we reached our destination for the day, Himalaya, I was already drenched
from sweat from the raincoat.

![The 108 Chahara waterfall](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-15.jpg)

I still took some photos along the way, getting my phone wet too. There was a
really nice stupa near a bunch of waterfalls on a vertical cliff, a perfect
photo spot if it wasn't for the weather.

![The evening view towards Gangapurna (7455 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-16.jpg)

The rest of the road was uneventful but difficult, covering a forest and some
small waterfalls we crossed. It was nice, but by the time we finished I was
very tired.

![Compilation of the second day of the Annapurna Base Camp hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_2/2026-06-10-17.mp4)

Once in the Himalaya village, I had dinner, rested, and took a cold shower with
a bucket. My phone was completely soaked from the rain and refused to turn on
or charge, so I put it in the pillow hoping to warm it up and dry the USB port.
Thankfully, by next morning it was back in business. What an eventful day...
