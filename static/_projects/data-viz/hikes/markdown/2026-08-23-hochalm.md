This was supposed to be the grand hike from Lenggries to Mittenwald over
Soiernspitze (2253 m) that I've been planning ever since
[my trip with Mikuláš to the same mountain](/hikes/2025-01-19-soiernspitze/).
Although the transport around Mittenwald is quite decent, I didn't want to
repeat my run between Krün and Mittenwald to catch the last train like I did
last time, so I cut down on the number of mountains, thinking of splitting the
hike in two.

After planning the hike with my [LigMapper app](/404/), I set off from Munich
as usual. What wasn't as usual was the train delay, although the bus thankfully
waited on the stop at the train station. We rushed towards the bus, filling it
up like tuna in a can, and started driving towards Hinterriß. Our drive was cut
short though, since police and local government workers blocked the way forward
due to an active police investigation. Later in the day I learned the
[full story](https://www.augsburger-allgemeine.de/bayern/bayern-polizei-ermittelt-nach-leichenfund-am-sylvensteinspeicher-115062478):
a dead body was found early in the morning, the police did an autopsy and
investigated the area, but everything pointed to a natural death.

Still, the path was blocked indefinitely for the investigation, and the bus
couldn't turn around on a narrow road. My only option was to hike back to
Lenggries. I quickly assessed my options and found Hochalm (1427 m) to be an
attractive detour back, although the hiking plan was looking underwhelming so
far. Upon closer inspection I saw a saving grace. The summit was placed in the
middle of a long ridge with several peaks and some iffy roads which were barely
visible on the map.

![Getting stuck at the dam](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-00.jpg)

After having fun in Omiš on [my Čeline (455 m) hike](/hikes/2026-08-08-omis_celine/)
climbing through the wilderness, I somehow ended up getting into trouble again.
My instincts were telling me that following the ridge was a bad idea, but I was
quite certain the life risk was low.

![The view at the dam from the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-01.jpg)
![The invisible trail towards the first peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-02.jpg)

The first challenge when you start this route is the barely visible trail near
the Sylvensteinspeicher. The tall grass kind of reminded me about my last
[Geierstein (1491 m) hike](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/),
specifically Schlagkopf (1330 m). Although to be fair, the unkept road was
closer to [Hahnenkopf (1735 m)](/404/)
or [Himmelschrofen (1791 m)](/404/)
near Oberstdorf. I distinctly remember feeling like nobody touched those trails
in ages.

![The dense forest to climb through](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-03.jpg)
![A snail in the grass on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-04.jpg)

As I continued upwards, the somewhat visible trail got harder to traverse due
to all the fallen trees nobody bothered to clear up. Walking around or over
them was a doable challenge though, so I continued.

![An old arrow pointing towards the summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-05.jpg)

My first target on the way was Brandkopf (1258 m). I hoped to reach it and
consider leaving this road to go down towards Röthenbachalm. The navigation was
starting to look annoying and the trail was visible less and less. I
occasionally looked back and the first few times still noted my bus staying at
the dam, meaning I made a somewhat good call to leave on foot.

![A trunk marking the Brandkopf (1258 m) summit](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-06.jpg)

As I was climbing up, the only thing on my mind was my
[trip to Wildbarren (1448 m)](/404/) that started equally randomly, but ended
up with me hiding from the rain in a random hut. The forecast was promising
this time around, so I tried to ignore the clouds in the distance and hike like
usual.

![Vorderer Kirchstein (1670 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-07.jpg)

The path forward was really annoying, with many ups and downs made even more
difficult due to the lack of any marked trail. At some point I did see some
markings, although they appeared to be ancient. Nevertheless, I somehow reached
the first peak. As expected, there was no mark or cross at Brandkopf (1258 m),
so I took an obligatory picture and continued.

![View down from Geißkopf (1352 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-08.jpg)

The road to the next summit, Geißkopf (1352 m), was non-existent. Although most
map providers don't show any route there, it is marked on openstreetmap as an
abandoned mountain hiking path with bad trail visibility. Checks out. Most of
the time I was just walking in the proximity of the trail, since the official
marked path was also off at times, and often the trail wasn't there anymore at
all.

![The view south-east from Geißkopf (1352 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-09.jpg)

Eventually, I got to the top of Geißkopf (1352 m). There was nothing there too,
just some trees and a steep slope down with a barely visible trail. I was
already quite exhausted, but it was too late to turn back, so I carefully
descended to the flatter area up ahead.

![The view from Gerstenrieder Kopf (1421 m) towards Hochalm (1427 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-10.jpg)

My next stop, Gerstenrieder Kopf (1421 m), was a short distance away. The trail
was slightly better visible. The peak itself was quite close to the main
attraction of this whole hike, so I didn't linger much and headed down to the
saddle that connects to Hochalm (1427 m).

![Approaching Hochalm (1427 m) from south-west](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-11.jpg)
![View from Hochalm (1427 m) towards Lenggries](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-12.jpg)

After climbing one of many barbed wire fences for cattle along the way, I
headed up to the main summit, Hochalm (1427 m). Not only is it a publically
accessible peak with multiple roads leading to it from different sides, it
even has hikers frequenting it, so I finally had some company for the first
time during this trip.

![View from Hochalm (1427 m) towards Buchstein (947 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-13.jpg)
![Willow gentian near Hochalm (1427 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-14.jpg)

After so much time being lost in the woods on a ridge, it felt amazing to
sit down near the cross, surrounded by people and cows, eating my snacks. The
view was also great: [Buchstein (947 m)](/hikes/2025-10-05-buchstein_only/)
towards the north-east, [Geierstein (1491 m)](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/)
towards the north, [Brauneck (1555 m)](/hikes/2026-06-27-brauneck_only/)
towards the north-west, etc.

![The view from Rosskopf (1403 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-15.jpg)

Although there was no trail marked on the map from Hochalm (1427 m) towards
Rosskopf (1403 m), there was a hut near the next summit on the map, so I
assumed the trail should be there. I was right, after climbing over some more
fences I found myself on a small overgrown trail towards the hut. I also
noticed a path marked to the south-east of Rosskopf (1403 m), and believed I
should end up there if everything goes according to plan. It did, and after
climbing up for some time, I got to Rosskopf (1403 m), not marked in any way
as usual.

![The hut at Rosskopf (1403 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-16.jpg)

Taking the trail down, I ended up on a well-kept tractor trail covered in
stones and packed dirt. Another amazing feeling after all the mush of dried
needles and moss I had to climb today. I walked down to the saddle connecting
a bunch of tractor roads together and continued up one of the roads towards
Breiten Kopf (1347 m) and further.

![The stones at Breiten Kopf (1347 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-17.jpg)
![A crystal shrine near Breiten Kopf (1347 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-18.jpg)

The peak itself was empty as usual, but this time at least I found some stones
in a neat pile nearby. They were covered in moss, so they've lasted in that
pile for a long time. The road from there on the map was looking even better,
so I headed east considering if I should take the road towards north or south.

![Road down from Breiten Kopf (1347 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-19.jpg)

As I was going down, I checked the map again and saw yet another peak nearby,
Saurüsselkopf (1364 m). Although I was very tired at this point, this was the
last hike on the ridge, with Sonnberg (1576 m) to the east belonging to a
different mountain range. Thus, for completionist points, I decided to make
a detour and capture it. How hard could it be?

![View from the ridge towards south](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-20.jpg)

At first, I found an overgrown tractor road leading up the mountain from the
normal road I was walking. It quickly deteriorated and I was walking on the
worst path so far. Full on trees covering the tracks completely, thorns and
spiky bushes covering the rest of the area, tall grass hiding stones and
spikes on the ground. I managed to completely tear my hiking trousers, but it
was too late to turn back.

![The ant hill at the top of Saurüsselkopf (1364 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-21.jpg)

After finding an opening and ascending quite some height, I was graciously
greeted by yet another dense forest area that I had to climb through. It was
just as bad as the first one, and I got some ticks sucking up my blood there.
After lots of hardship I reached the top only to see nothing yet again. The
GPS signal was jumping all over the place, but it circled somewhere around an
ant hill on the top. I walked around it to make sure Komoot accepts this peak
as visited, then headed down.

![A crystal shrine on the slope of Saurüsselkopf (1364 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-22.jpg)

On the way down I was trying to find a better route. Backed up by my watch
showing me the route up, I tried to walk around it while keeping close to the
original track. It started off good, and eventually I reached a small opening
in the woods that had some apples for animals to eat and a photo-trap to
photograph them. I probably ended up on the footage too, oh well.

![The overgrown tractor track leading down from Saurüsselkopf (1364 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-23.jpg)

The opening had no other way to go. I climbed through yet more bushes and trees
and ended up back on the overgrown tractor tracks leading to the normal road.
At this point I was close to losing it from having to climb through so much
dense vegetation, but I overcame my frustration, got onto the road and headed
down. My legs were already hurting, but I was happy I covered the whole ridge
in one day.

![The Klaffenbach stream](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-24.jpg)

At the bottom near the Klaffenbach stream, the navigation app lead me right
through it instead of following the gravel road. I didn't argue and followed,
having to climb over more tall grass, getting myself very dirty. I walked
onto an asphalt road with frequent bike traffic and headed to Lenggries.

![The Klaffenbach stream](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-25.jpg)
![A flower growing out of a tree trunk](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-26.jpg)

After walking for some time, I noticed a shortcut skipping a detour around a
hill that the bikers have to take for a smooth way up. This shortcut wasn't in
a good condition, with sticks and fallen trees covering the road. Still, it was
much better than the whole trek before that.

![View towards Herrnköpfl (807 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-27.jpg)
![A cow lost in thought neaer Herrnköpfl (807 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-28.jpg)

On the way back to the village, I came across Herrnköpfl (807 m), another small
rock marked as a peak on the map. I walked around a fenced area and found a
semi-official opening with some tracks leading up. After some climbing between
pine trees I got to the marked summit, although it wasn't the real top of the
rock standing there.

![View from the Lenggries fields towards Hahn Berg (1321 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-29.jpg)

I circled the rock for a bit making sure the peak would be detected as visited,
then headed to town between the meadows. The evening views were amazing:
Hahn Berg (1321 m) and [Stangeneck (1603 m)](/hikes/2024-07-21-benediktenwand/)
to the west, Schönberg (1620 m) to the east, Grasleiten-Stein (1268 m) and
[Geierstein (1491 m)](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/) up ahead.

![Pomes berries at Mühlbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-30.jpg)

The route through Mühlbach and into Lenggries was uneventful. I overtook some
other hikers, but my legs were already giving out. Another issue that has been
bugging me for a large portion of the hike was my water supplies running out.
I remembered there was a single bar-like shop that worked on Sundays, so I
headed right there to buy myself a drink. I found a currants drink there that
I took without a second guess and enjoyed it on the way to my train home.

![Drinking currants juice near the train station](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/hochalm/2026-08-23-31.jpg)

To sum up this weird journey, I would *NOT* recommend this hike to anyone.
The only mountain I'd suggest others go to is Hochalm (1427 m), but you
can reach it from Hohenwiesen or Achenpass and skip the whole ridge.
It's tough to navigate, tough to traverse, and tough to bear. It costed me my
trousers and dignity, but I'm still happy I got to experience it.
