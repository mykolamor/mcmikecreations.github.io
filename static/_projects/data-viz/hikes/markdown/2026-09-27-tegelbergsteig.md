With my dental surgery still affecting me, I convinced David to lay off
difficult hikes and offered him to revisit Tegelberg (1706 m) a location I've
enjoyed several times in the past both for [Grade A/B via ferrata climbing](/404/)
and [normal hiking](/404/).

Topo from [faltershomepage.de](https://faltershomepage.de/index.php/de/ausgefuehrte-touren-2012/40-profitour-13-16juli?showall=1):
![Topo of Tegelbergsteig](/images/hikes/topo/tegelbergsteig.jpg)

The Tegelbergbahn cable car there offers amazing views onto the magical
fairytale castle of Neuschwanstein, while the cliffs under its route offer
great hiking and climbing routes of different difficulty levels.

On the way to the public transport I found a diamond wedding ring and gave it
to the lost and found office. I'm quite certain the original owner won't be
able to find it, but at least my conscience is clear.
We started off with a walk through a nicely paved asphalt road towards the
cliffs. Even there, you could already see the castles in the distance, giving
us a preview of the scenery to come. Soon after, we turned off the road and
onto a nice trail through the forest covering the whole slope, overtaking some
other hikers on the way.

![View from the Tegelbergbahn station towards Neuschwanstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-00.jpg)
![The forest trail on the mountain slope towards the via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-01.jpg)

Although I had to take some breaks and walk slower due to my surgery, we still
got to the bottom of the climbing section quite fast. We decided to put our via
ferrata gear there and use the short grade A section at the start to warm up
before the big climb.

![View onto the grade A section of the Gelbe-Wand-Steig via ferrata](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-02.jpg)

That spot, near a dried-up waterfall, was already amazing in terms of the
views. We were roughly on the same elevation as Hornburg (1101 m), looking onto
the lakes and fields below in awe. Other hikers also took breaks on the same
spot, enjoying the views and sometimes chatting with us. From the very start we
could already see quite a few people on the different sections of the route.

![David climbing the beginning of the grade C Tegelbergsteig](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-03.jpg)

After a bit of walking on the Gelbe-Wand-Steig grade A/B via ferrata after the
waterfall break, we quickly reached the start of the Tegelbergsteig, the
grade C via ferrata we've been aiming for. It starts off with a vertical ladder
followed by a quick left climb with no metal rungs, which poses as a good
beginner test to deter people who are not ready for the challenge. Although we
eventually did the section, we got stuck there for some time due to a queue of
other climbers, some of which brought their kids. Continuing on the route,
most following sections have rungs and the cable is tight, making it easier to
climb. The general rule of this route is: sevaral seconds of exposed climbing
with smoothly polished holds (grabbing onto the cable and pushing feet off the
rock) followed by a rung to rest and recover.

![The first resting section of Tegelbergsteig with a view towards Füssen](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-04.jpg)
![Paragliders launching off the Tegelberghaus (1706 m) take-off spot](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-05.jpg)

The first section of the climb gives climbers a good overview of the challenges
ahead, so once you reach the bench with a small register casket in the rock,
you should be able to judge if you are ready to continue or not.
My camera ran out of battery at that point, so we had to wait there longer and
recharge it with a power bank. This gave us a good opportunity to enjoy the
views and look at the numerous paragliders circling around us like birds of
prey.

![View towards Tegelberghaus (1706 m) from the top of Tegelbergsteig](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-06.jpg)

The rest of the climb was more of the same, although I could see David losing
strength and becoming more scared over time. He hasn't had the luck to fall
down at [Höhenglücksteig](/hikes/2025-07-26-hoehengluecksteig/) like I did, so
he was more cautious and careful. I followed him and had a few issues of my
own, mostly with stretching to reach holds and rungs, but at the end we managed
to get to the top of the via ferrata and enjoy the views towards the
mountaintop and the valley.

![View from the top of Tegelbergsteig towards Füssen](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-07.jpg)

At the top, we took a tiny detour to get on a cliff and take some photos of the
valley and the lakes near Neuschwanstein. The views were amazing, and the
paragliders surrounding us made it even more magical. We also tried taking
photos of each other, but I once again proved to be a crappy photographer.

![Paraglider flying over Tegelbergsteig](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-08.jpg)
![Views from the route between Tegelberghaus (1706 m) and Neuschwanstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-09.jpg)

From there, we made a short descent back onto the Gelbe-Wand-Steig via ferrata
and prepared to visit the restaurant. Unfortunately, we spent so much time
climbing that the restaurant was already closed, so we decided to head towards
Neuschwanstein through the forest trail. The walk was peaceful and scenic,
although we got completely distracted from it chatting.

![View from the trail towards Alpsee and Schwansee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-10.jpg)
![View from the trail onto Säuling (2047 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-11.jpg)

After a long walk across the forest on the slope of the ridge, we reached the
hillside leading to the castle. There are multiple viewpoints on the way, and
we stopped at some of them to take photos and enjoy the views. There were still
some tourists climbing up and down the mountain for the sunset scenery, but we
were lucky to have the castle and the surrounding area mostly to ourselves.

![The panoramic viewpoint overlooking castle Hohenschwangau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-12.jpg)
![Sunset over Neuschwanstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-13.jpg)

The lower viewpoints are, unfortunately, mostly overgrown with trees, and there
are usually heaps of tourists hogging the best spots. Despite the late hour, we
still had to wait for a few minutes to get a clearer view of the castle. Still,
we couldn't see much due to vegetation. After that whole adventure, we got down
to the Neuschwanstein entrance and were greeted by a fully closed venue. The
tourist shops, the souvenir stands, and the ticket office were all closed, with
some strugglers still trying to get some photos before leaving.

![Neuschwanstein from the overgrown scenic viewpoint](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-14.jpg)
![Hohenschwangau after sunset](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/tegelbergsteig/2026-09-27-15.jpg)

After we took the road from the castle to the bus stop, we got on the bus and
headed back to Füssen, where we had a short walk to the train station. At least
in the bigger town some cafés and shops were still open, so we didn't starve on
the way back.

![Full via ferrata climb](/404/)

To summarize this, it's an incredible experience for semi-experienced amateur
climbers. I've done plenty of A/B via ferrata climbs, as well as some more
exposed ascents with no safety cables, so this was a reasonable challenge for
me to take on properly this time, with a via ferrata set and a helmet. The
views are great, although the route gets quite crowded on the weekend. If
you're interested, I recommend going there and trying to complete the first
section of the climb, then checking the topo and deciding if you want to
continue. Best of luck!
