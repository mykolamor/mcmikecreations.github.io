After coming to the office for a normal 9-5 after our hike to the
[Tegelbergsteig via ferrata](/hikes/2026-09-27-tegelbergsteig/), David fell
sick, and I was left to plan a new hike for the weekend. I still had plenty
of routes in mind, but I wanted to take an easier hike to present a new version
of this website.

Although by the time you'd read this, the whole website may have changed, this
hike is where it started. There is a lot of information that landed in this
update, and I'll intertwine it with the hike itself.

![One of the fields of Tegernseer Golfclub Bad Wiessee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-00.jpg)
![A mushroom near the golf club](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-01.jpg)

Everything started off a bit confusing. I followed the route I built in my
[LigMapper](/404/) app beforehand, and it took me up a back-alley asphalt trail
to a parking lot near the Tegernseer Golfclub Bad Wiessee. As I was taking a
photo, I was immediately told to turn back and avoid stepping on the grass by
a player actively walking across the grass. Sure.

![The 697 hiking route](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-02.jpg)
![Branches blocking the way to Winneralm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-03.jpg)

From there I followed the 697 hiking route for a bit and immediately
encountered fallen branches and trees blocking the short road up to Winneralm.
I took the longer way up and stepped onto a beautiful forest road. Why did I
choose this route in the first place? Well, the peaks along this route were
still on my to-do list since my trip [from Gaißach to Tegernsee](/hikes/2026-05-17-rechelkopf_semmelberg/).

![The detour trail to Holzeralm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-04.jpg)

As part of my [Hiking Web](/hikes/tag/Web/) project, I wanted to visit as many
peaks in Oberbayern as possible, so the ones today were going to be a great
addition. Moreover, this hike was a trip I wish the previous few routes (like
[Hochalm (1427 m)](/hikes/2026-08-23-hochalm/)) were. Nice well-kept road,
other hikers coming by, perfect weather.

![The crossroads with the trail to Wasenkogel (1211 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-05.jpg)
![The slope towards Holzeralm and Wasenkogel (1211 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-06.jpg)

As I continued to Wasenkogel (1211 m), I had to leave the road (seemingly
leading to an unmarked peak for wood-cutting) and enter a forest trail, covered
in roots and moss. It seemed to be used as a mountain bike path too, with many
tracks showing through muddy spots. One time I looked to the side and saw a
mountain biker traversing the slope, away from any trails or clearings. You do
you, man.

When I was passing Holzeralm, I met some local hikers following the 698
hiking route and thought to myself: "Wouldn't it be nice to go down with them
on this easy shortcut, without having to proceed to the next peak?" That's when
it hit me: most of the hikes on the website are quite large and not meant for a
casual hiker. A beginner who wants to try out a route wouldn't want to go on a
full hike like this one. They'd prefer to bail out early after a nice peak or
two. Thus, the bailout points and routes were born. The hut itself was for rent
and had a nice terrace with a view of the surrounding peaks.

![View from Wasenkogel (1211 m) towards its cross, Hirschberg (1670 m), and Buchstein (1701 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-07.jpg)
![View from Wasenkogel (1211 m) towards Tegernsee, Wallberg (1722 m), and Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-08.jpg)

I kept going, walked past a cross below the summit (placed there to be seen
from below probably), and reached the summit with a biker enjoying the views
sitting on a folding chair. I took my photos and hurried along, letting him be.
I also saw a bunch of children running around the cross, so this route is
definitely accessible for families. It was really cool seeing all the mountains
in the area I already visited: [Hirschberg (1670 m) with DAAD FK](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/),
[Buchstein (1701 m) with David](/hikes/2025-05-11-buchstein/),
[Zwergelberg (1113 m) and Fockenstein (1564 m) wth "Wandern für glückliche Menschen"](/hikes/2026-04-05-fockenstein_geierstein/).
I've really come a long way.

![The path from Wasenkogel (1211 m) to Nesselscheibe (1258 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-09.jpg)
![Fallen tree near the "peak" of Nesselscheibe (1258 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-10.jpg)

From there I followed the wide forest road to Nesselscheibe (1258 m), although
the road quickly turned to the side following the 699 hiking route, and I was
left on a forest trail yet again. This one was very cool though. Slightly
overgrown in some parts, but completely walkable, covered in moss, ferns, and
mushrooms of all sorts and sizes. I tried coming as close to the peak as
possible and thus had to make a small detour off the path, but I came across
even more cool plants and mossy fallen trees.

![Mushroom on the way to the unnamed 1212-meter peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-11.jpg)

From there I left the trail and walked onto a wider path reconnecting with
the forest road near Nesselscheiberücken. I had to step over some chopped trees
there, but it wasn't much of a problem. The mushrooms though, the autumn smell!
I got a little thirsty though, so I took a water break, deciding to also add
water sources to the website.

![Owergrown road near the unnamed 1212-meter peak](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-12.jpg)

The rest of the way to the unnamed 1212-meter peak was way more overgrown, but
still fully walkable. Normally, I'd scratch myself on some branches on such a
trek, like I did at [Großer Laber (1466 m)](/hikes/2026-09-06-grosser_laber/),
but this time the route was good enough to avoid that. There was a bigger
opening at the peak itself with a small trail leading north, but I had my
sights on Gefällberg (1070 m) ahead.

![Mushroom on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-13.jpg)

Now this section was the real challenge, reminding me of my fight towards
[Saurüsselkopf (1364 m)](/hikes/2026-08-23-hochalm/). High dense spruce trees,
fallen tree logs, ferns holding onto you and scraping your legs, bugs flying
into your face, tall grass. This section had everything. Thankfully, it didn't
last too long, and after around 15 minutes I was back on a nice forest slope
with barely any grass growing between spruce needles covering the ground.

![Forestry markings and hunting outposts near Gefällberg (1070 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-14.jpg)

I kept going to an unmarked hunting outpost, where I looked down the slope
towards a hut at (47.7279997, 11.6589323) and saw a shirtless man sunbathing
in the middle of the forest on a foldable chair. As I was walking past, he saw
me and started dressing up. I didn't want to disturb his privacy, so I pushed
along towards the summit.

![The administrative boundary marking stone at the summit of Gefällberg (1070 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-15.jpg)

Gefällberg (1070 m) was as unremarkable as they get. A strategically positioned
hunting outpost with an overgrown trail to it, possibly used by the man below.
A stream nearby, so overgrown with trees and bushes that I couldn't see
anything. And no trail down, except for a vaguely visible way leading towards
the man and further down to the hut.

![Mushrooms growing out of a dead tree on the slope of Gefällberg (1070 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-16.jpg)

Since I took the executive decision to not go back up the way I came, and I
didn't want to interact with the man either, I chose to follow the slope
gradient and just go down to the forest road. Now the "path" here gets a bit
tricky. I was walking down a steep slope covered in fallen leaves and
mushrooms, avoiding branches and tree trunks that I'd usually slip or trip on.
I also did my best to avoid plants, flowers, and mushrooms.

![A small Gefällberg (1070 m) stream flowing into Gaißach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-17.jpg)

After edging my way down, I finally saw the forest road in the distance. Sadly,
there was an even steeper slope between me and the road, covered in some random
plants, so I needed to find an alternative way down. An opportunity presented
itself rather quickly though - a small mountain stream falling into Gaißach, a
mountain river flowing along the road.

![A lost bucket from a forager near the stream](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-18.jpg)

Although I'm not a big fan of walking along or through mountain streams after
my questionable [Branderschrofen (1881 m)](/404/) experience, I had no other
choice. I carefully descended the slope towards the stream, then stepped
inside. Although there was barely any water, the dirt mixed with rocks was
slippery and pulled my feet downwards. I had to be careful not to fall, while
climbing over yet more fallen trees, avoiding mushrooms on the way.

![Sheep grazing on the way to Marienstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-19.jpg)

Eventually I made it onto a flatter area, where I could step out of the water
and onto some semi-solid ground. I was almost at the road, but the shallow
slope with the mountain stream got highly overgrown there, prompting me to find
a detour. It wasn't difficult though, and I eventually snuck back onto the
road, immediately being greeted by tons of bikers, hut owners on their offroad
cars, and some fellow hikers.

![Evenfall over Platten (879 m) with sunrays shining through the trees](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-20.jpg)

I was happy to be back on the road, but I still had a long way to go. I
followed this road all the way to Marienstein, then turned west towards
Platten (879 m). It was so close to the road I was taking, it would be a crime
not to visit. Although I wasn't expecting much, I was met by the setting sun
shining through the trees, illuminating the moss and ferns on the forest floor.
The slight haze in the air made it even more magical, and I took a few photos
before even reaching the peak.

![Mushroom at the top of Platten (879 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-21.jpg)
![Mushroom on the way from Platten (879 m) to Marienstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-22.jpg)

The peak of Platten (879 m) was again unmarked, reminding me of my
[Auer Berg (1252 m)](/hikes/2025-10-26-auer_berg/) trip, but the mushroom grove
at the top made up for it. As I was turning back, a jeep driver drove past me,
making me question what he was planning to do there, since the road stopped at
the peak and didn't wrap to the other side. Anyway.

![A mining monument in Marienstein](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-23.jpg)

With the main part of my adventure behind me, I was walking through Marienstein
and thinking about the trek so far. Although most of it was easy, the steep
slope was a real outlier. I decided the website should have marked hazards,
optional sections, and difficulty ratings per segment.

![Views from Golfclub Margarethenhof](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-24.jpg)

From the mining town of Marienstein and to Schneiderhäusl, the road was easy
and uneventful, going through the rich resorts, restaurants, and fields of
Golfclub Margarethenhof. I was in awe of the beautiful houses and gardens, but
I remembered the earlier warning and stayed strictly on the road, avoiding
grass like my life depended on it.

From that settlement I turned south-east towards the remaining hill the area
offered me before Gmund. It has two official peaks - Hainzenhöhe (833 m) and
Kaltenbrunner Eck (842 m), both of which I wanted to visit. I went there
in-order, stumbling my way through the forest on somewhat visible trails and
forest roads.

![The panorama at Hainzenhöhe (833 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-25.jpg)

The Hainzenhöhe (833 m) summit was beautiful. It has a direct view of
Tegernsee, and from there of [Wallberg (1722 m)](/404/) and
[Bodenschneid (1669 m)](/hikes/2025-11-01-bodenschneid/). You can also see
the smaller mountains around, but I was too lazy to figure out which ones I
was looking at. The sun at this point already set, so I was racing against
the clock to reach a normal asphalt road towards Gmund by the time it gets
dark.

![The forest patch at Kaltenbrunner Eck (842 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-27.jpg)

I went straight towards Finsterwald across the Kaltenbrunner Eck (842 m)
summit, which is just an overgrown spot on the slope with no views or
interesting properties. It was properly dark by now, so I turned on my
flashlight and followed the trail down.

![A water well on the way down from Kaltenbrunner Eck (842 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/wasenkogel/2026-10-04-26.jpg)

The last part of the hike was just a normal walk through the town. As I
left the forest near a parking lot and entered the inhabited area, I saw
my train leaving the station and driving into the distance, so I had 30 spare
minutes to wait for the next one. I didn't think too long about it, and just
kept walking until I reached Gmund. The Gmund train station has an indoor area
with a vending machine, some benches, and toilets, making for a much nicer wait
for the train.

To conclude, this hike was a great addition to my collection. Although I would
not encourage anyone to attempt exactly the route I took, I highly recommend
beginners to visit Wasenkogel (1211 m) and moderate hikers to explore a bigger
loop. It's really worth it, and I'm pretty sure any weather conditions can be
managed on this trek. Let me know how it goes, my contacts are at the bottom!
