The weather forecast for this Sunday looked terrible, with rain and wind, so I was
hesitant to do anything. I decided that I'll double-check the weather on the
day and see if it makes sense to commute. Well, on the day of the hike the
forecast was promising snow, and after [freezing on Krettenburg (1494 m)](/hikes/2026-10-22-krettenburg_westerberg/) I wanted to experience the
"first snow" like a normal person and go for an easy hike.

Since it snowed only higher up in the mountains and the ground near the bigger
towns was covered in a mush of snow and mud, I opted for a shorter hike taking
care of the remaining peaks between Schliersee and Tegernsee:
Krainsbergkogel (1037 m) and Aschberg (1042 m).

The route started the same way it ended on my
[last attempt of Westerberg (1328 m)](/hikes/2026-10-22-krettenburg_westerberg/),
walking on the well-kept forestry road near Schliersee. I assumed this road is
only used for technical purposes, but I actually met a few hikers taking the
same route under the rain and snow, even in such questionable weather.

![The road from Schliersee towards Krainsbergkogel (1037 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-00.jpg)

The hike up was quite uneventful, since it follows the forest road with dense
trees hiding the surrounding peaks from view. I could have tried to get this
section over with faster, but I didn't feel like running, and my muscles were
still hurting from my [Baumgartenschneid (1448 m) visit](/hikes/2026-10-26-baumgartenschneid_kreuzbergkoepfel/).

![The overgrown road section leading towards Krainsbergkogel (1037 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-01.jpg)

From the well-kept forest road, I turned to a detour up the hill near
Krainsbergkogel (1037 m). There was another offshoot of the same road looping
around the other side of the mountain, but judging by the map it was more
overgrown, so I didn't risk it. This detour was still perfectly walkable and
barely had any grass on it, indicating recent use with the cut-down branches
laying around.

![The walkable detour from the main forest road onto the road wrapping Krainsbergkogel (1037 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-03.jpg)

From there, I got to the highest point of the forestry detour road, and started
looking for an opening between the trees to crawl my way up to the summit. There
were some promising holes in the dense bush layer, but I kept walking a bit
further and found another overgrown forest road leading way closer to the top.
Although not recently used by tractors, it still had a fresh trail carved in
one of the tire tracks, so I guess it was semi-popular?

![The view near the summit of Krainsbergkogel (1037 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-02.jpg)

Eventually the overgrown road ended and there was a small trail between the
pine trees leading to the top. Still perfectly walkable and less overgrown than
the road before, I entered the trail and found myself at the summit. Nothing
interesting there though, not even a stone for a territorial boundary. From the
summit I made my way back and kept walking towards Aschberg (1042 m).

![The gate at the detour to Aschberg (1042 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-04.jpg)

As I was walking along the main hiking/forestry road, I saw the crossroads
towards my target with a sign saying: "The road ends in 200 meters." I proceeded
onto this road, walked through the open gate and proceeded towards the summit.
The road was really well-kept and clear of any forestry leftovers. I didn't
even find any potholes from the usual tractor operations. This road eventually
made a sharp turn left close to the summit, and I walked the remaining few
meters through a very open forest.

![The view from the peak of Aschberg (1042 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-05.jpg)

From there I returned to the main road and kept walking in the direction of
[Rainerkopf (1463 m)](/hikes/2025-12-23-rainerkopf/). As I kept gaining
elevation, the air got colder, my fingers got tinglier, and the snow cap on
trees and grass got thicker. After having cold fingers on every hike
[since Mahnkopf (2093 m)](/hikes/2026-10-11-mahnkopf/), I brought my winter
mittens and put them on.

![Snowy road from Aschberg (1042 m) towards Rainerkopf (1463 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-06.jpg)

The wind picked up and I immediately got my mitten decision validated. At this
point I stopped seeing others on the trek, and it was past noon, so I assumed I
was the last hiker on the trail. I reached the shelter on the saddle between
Lahnenkopf (1416 m) and Rainerkopf (1463 m), deciding to re-pack my backpack,
and a bike rider blasted past me hurrying up towards Bodenschneidhaus (1365 m).

![Road down to Rottach-Egern past Kühzagl-Alm (1064 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-07.jpg)

When I packed my stuff, I noticed Brunnerstein (1243 m) on the map and tried
moving in its direction, but the steep slope, the snow covering slippery leaves,
and the water streams carved into the mountainside quickly discouraged me. I
walked down past the now-closed huts and proceeded back to civilization.

![Kühzagl-Alm (1064 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-08.jpg)
![View from the road towards Rohrkopf (1316 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-09.jpg)

The rest of the road was again uneventful. The gravel and dirt have started to
dry up, and the sun started showing in-between the clouds. I checked the train
schedule and realized I'll need to keep rushing the whole way down to the bus
stop in Ellmau.

![View from Kühzagl towards Wallberg (1722 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-10.jpg)
![Alpacas on a farm near Kirkofen and Ellmau](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-11.jpg)

To my surprise, as I was hurrying to the bus stop with only a few buffer minutes
to spare, I came across an older lady going in the same direction. Safe to say
she didn't make it in time, but I spent a section of the road with her enjoying
the views. I realized it back on my [Rottachfall hike](/404/) that the area
near Rottach-Egern is beautiful in any season. [Wallberg (1722 m) up close](/404/),
[Blauberge in the distance](/hikes/2025-06-01-blauberge/), easily noticeable
[Leonhardstein (1452 m)](/404/) and [Buchstein (1701 m)](/hikes/2025-10-05-buchstein_only/)
standing out from the crowd, [Hirschberg (1670 m) up north from them](/hikes/2024-11-17-hirschberg/). And, of course, Tegernsee itself as a cherry on top, beautiful in
any season.

![View from Ellmau towards Leonhardstein (1452 m) and Buchstein (1701 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/aschberg/2026-11-01-12.jpg)

All in all, I would definitely recommend this road in warmer seasons as a casual
beginner walk for hikers of all ages. There are no breathtaking views for most
of the route as the forest covers you in shade, but the accessibility of the
ordeal makes it worth the visit. Plus, the Kühzagl-Alm (1064 m) is open in
summer for a refreshing drink or some food.
