After a long discussion about where to go for the next hike, Stanislav and
Bohdan settled on Trainsjoch (1707 m). I was rock climbing in a gym at the time
of discussion, so I didn't have much say in the matter. The plan was to
start in Kiefersfelden and hike up to Trainsjoch (1707 m) via the pathway in the
valley between the ridges.

![Kieferbach near Kiefersfelden](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-00.jpg)

I was tempted to go on a similar hike ever since my trip to
[Großer Traithen (1852 m)](/hikes/2025-01-26-grosser_traithen/)
in January 2025, as I had seen Trainsjoch (1707 m)
from the summit and wanted to explore it further. Still, I didn't expect
to go there so soon and in winter of all times!

![We're entering paradise](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-01.jpg)
![The river along the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-02.jpg)

Regardless of my worries, we set off early in the morning and quickly arrived
at Paradis. We were walking along the Kieferbach river, seeing some beautiful
frozen waterfalls and ice formations along the way.

![The stunning water mill](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-03.jpg)
![Entrance to Untere Gießenbachklamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-04.jpg)

We also saw a cool water mill, which looked to be still operational, if it
wasn't for all the ice on it. After the mill, we continued up the trail and
quickly reached Untere Gießenbachklamm, a narrow gorge with steep walls and
tons of tiny waterfalls. The trail was closed, but many hikers, including us,
were ignoring the signs and continuing up the gorge. The ice formations
were stunning, and we took our time to admire them and take photos.

![The path over Untere Gießenbachklamm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-05.jpg)
![I got stuck exiting the gorge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-06.jpg)

After exiting the gorge, we crossed a beautiful wooden bridge over the river
and continued up the trail. The road was really wide and easy to follow. There
was snow and ice on the ground, but it wasn't too deep, nothing compared to
my [Setzberg (1706 m) hike](/hikes/2026-01-11-setzberg/) last week.

![The wooden bridge over Gießenbach](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-07.jpg)
![Anemone hepatica (liverwort/liverleaf/pennywort) on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-08.jpg)

After passing through the forested area and reaching the hills of
Oberaudorfer Alm (1120 m), we noticed a big cluster of hot air balloons in the
direction of Walchsee. It was a beautiful sight, and we managed to see them
for a while as we continued up the trail. For just 290 Euro you can
[take a ride](https://www.kaiserwinkl.com/en/event/kaiserwinkl-alpine-balloning.html)
in one of those balloons too! We also took a break at the Alm
for some snacks and drinks, enjoying the view of the surrounding mountains.

![Oberaudorfer Alm (1120 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-09.jpg)
![Hot air baloons near Walchsee](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-10.jpg)

As we reached the border between Landkreis Miesbach and Bayrischzell, the route
became significantly more challenging. There was a pair of footsteps following
the trail to the summit, but it eventually stopped, as the person changed their
mind and turned back. We climbed over a few fallen trees and walked over pine
needles and snow, so turning around wasn't on our menu.

![The signpost on the way to Trainsjoch (1707 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-11.jpg)
![Going up the "shortcut"](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-12.jpg)

Fueled by determination after seeing a signpost, we pushed on towards the peak.
As there was no marked trail ahead of us, we decided to take the straight way
up the slope, taking a "shortcut" through the bushes, trees, and snow. We spent
a lot more energy than expected, slipping and sliding on the snow, but we were
making good progress. The trees were also helping us keep our balance and avoid
falling. You can check the shortcut on the GPX track at the top.

![Reconnecting with the original route](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-13.jpg)
![View towards Trainsalm](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-14.jpg)

Eventually, we reached the original trail again, which was a relief. From
there, it was a straightforward climb to the summit of Trainsjoch (1707 m). I
was a bit bitter though when I saw the Trainsalm below our ridge, as that was
my original plan for the summer version of this hike, but my emotions passed
quickly as I was enjoying the climb.

![Border post #69, nice](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-15.jpg)
![View towards Trainsjoch (1707 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-16.jpg)

Following the ridge wasn't much of a challenge, just the usual physical effort
of hiking uphill through shallow snow. Since this section was on the southern
side, we were constantly exposed to the sun, warming us up nicely. We even saw
some people on the summit, although as we got closer we realized they were just
a bunch of signposts.

![View from Trainsjoch (1707 m) towards Nesselberg (1363 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-17.jpg)
![View from Trainsjoch (1707 m) towards Kaisergebirge](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-18.jpg)

Eventually, we reached the summit of Trainsjoch (1707 m) and saw real people
approaching from the other side. Stanislav brought a small drone to capture
the moment. As he was setting it up, my gaze wandered around the stunning views
of Kaisergebirge and [Vordere Kesselschneid (2002 m)](/hikes/2025-11-23-vordere_kesselschneid/),
which I had climbed in November. Although it brought back the memory of
fracturing my finger, I was still happy with the adventure overall. We even
got to see Brünnstein (1619 m), which [I climbed last summer](/hikes/2025-07-06-bruennstein/)
with David.

![Trainsjoch (1707 m) from above](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-24.mp4 "autoplay")

After relaxing for a bit and getting cold from the wind, we decided to start
heading down to Bayrischzell. The descent was much easier than the ascent, as
we could follow the trodden path with many footsteps down the slope. We also
met quite a few hiker groups heading up, which was nice to see.

![Looking back at Trainsjoch (1707 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-19.jpg)
![The view from Nesselberg (1363 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-20.jpg)

I was a bit underwhelmed by Nesselberg (1363 m) on the way down, as there was
no cross and the peak itself was just a small bump on the ridge. Still, a peak
is a peak, and I was happy to add it to my list.

![Jagdhütte along the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-21.jpg)

After passing a small Jagdhütte and following the snaky trail down to the
asphalt road, we reached the final stretch of the hike. While there was a bus
scheduled to arrive soon, we decided to walk on foot and never saw it passing
by. We could have taken the gravel road down to Bayrischzell, but we opted for
walking on the edge of the main road, avoiding the snow and ice on the side
paths.

![Almost at Bayrischzell with a view of Wendelstein (1838 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-22.jpg)

As we saw [Wendelstein (1838 m)](/hikes/2025-06-10-wendelstein/), which I
visited a few times before, we knew Bayrischzell was close. We considered
going to a cafe for some hot drinks, but the train was arriving soon, so we
decided to head straight to the station.

![A lion statue in Bayrischzell](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/trainsjoch/2026-01-18-23.jpg)

To sum up, this route is really great if you set yourself a goal of following
the ridge on the border between Germany and Austria. The views are great, as
you can see into both countries, and the hike itself is not too challenging,
especially if you avoid the "shortcut" we took. I would definitely recommend
some variation of this hike to anyone willing to hike in the area! Just try
to figure out the bus schedule in advance, as it saves you a long walk at the
end.
