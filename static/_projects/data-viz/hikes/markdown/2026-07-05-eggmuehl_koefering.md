On Thursday before the hike I had my other two wisdom teeth taken out. I was
feeling under the weather, and I was forbidden from doing active sports, so
taking a stroll across some fields was my only option.

![Corn field near the exit from Eggmühl](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-00.jpg)

I could either go to [Eggmühl](/hikes/2026-05-14-neufahrn_niederbayern_eggmuehl/)
or [Tutzing](/hikes/2025-02-23-herrsching_tutzing/)
to continue my [Hiking Web](/hikes/tag/Web/)
journey there, and I chose the former. Upon arrival, the dark cloudy sky
immediately caught my attention. I knew it was supposed to rain later in the
day, but I presumed I'd manage to avoid the worst of it by picking a location
up north from Munich.

Eggmühl was as small as I remembered it to be, the lion statue was still
majestic, and soon after I switched to walking across fields and forests on a
small trail. I stayed on the Via Nova pilgrimage route for a while, but it kept
going east, so I eventually left it.

![Walking through Rogging](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-01.jpg)
![The St. Johannes Evangelist church in Rogging](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-02.jpg)

Upon reaching Rogging, some locals met me and briefly talked about my route.
Despite the numerous hiking trails passing by, they were weirded out by me
being there. Oh well, no hard feelings. I then turned north towards Einhausen
and kept going.

![Poppies on the way from Rogging towards Einhausen](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-03.jpg)
![Wild Teasel (Dipsacus fullonum) near Einhausen](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-04.jpg)

The road there was very well kept. Asphalt for most of the way, no rubble or
mud on the track, locals enjoying strolls walking past me. Everything was just
too good to be true, so the clouds decided to drop some rain on us. Still, I
kept going and soon after passed the settlement.

![Road between Einhausen and Höhenberg](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-05.jpg)
![Wheat field near Höhenberg](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-06.jpg)

Next up was a federal road between Einhausen and Höhenberg. It was quite
nerve-wracking to walk on the side of it, with cars flying by at 80 km/h, with
no pedestrian train to be seen. As a positive, I saw some deer peeking out of a
nearby forest. I passed this section as quickly as possible and kept going.

![Forest road on the way to Untersanding](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-07.jpg)
![Wheat field near Untersanding](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-08.jpg)

Next up were a bunch of wheat fields leading me to Untersanding, with the
[Schloss Haus](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schloss_Haus_%28Neueglofsheim%29)
castle soon after. I deliberately took the longer route on the day
to visit all nearby castles, so I was happy to finally get close to one of my
main points of interest.

![Schloss Haus from afar](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-09.jpg)
![Wheat field near Schloss Haus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-10.jpg)

I barely saw anyone around when walking past the village, but the castle on the
hill was in clear view, giving me motivation to keep going. After crossing
another regional road and approaching it through a nice road with fruit trees
planted on both sides, I came across a big oak shortly before the castle. It
was already hollow inside, but the sheer size allowed it to keep living by
getting nutrients through the thick bark that remained of it. The locals
brought some children by car to the tree to play around and climb on it, and
the children were very excited about it. The [Saint-Wolfgang Oak](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt-Wolfgangs-Eiche)
is said to be 1250 years old based on the local legends. I looked into the name
and it's related to St. Wolfgang, one of the first bishops of Regensburg, but
the connection is not very substantiated and the name could have transitioned
from a different nearby oak.

![Saint-Wolfgang Oak near Schloss Haus](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-11.jpg)

As I approached the castle, it dawned on me that the whole area around it was
private, with no way of getting close. Security cameras and alarms deterred me
further from making any stupid moves. Since there was nothing else to do there,
I took a short break on a bench under the big oak and saw some locals from the
[Regensburger Diözesanfußwallfahrt nach Altötting](https://www.regensburger-fusswallfahrt.de/)
bringing two donkeys for a walk. Then I kept going towards Alteglofsheim.

![Regensburger Diözesanfußwallfahrt e.V. donkeys](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-12.jpg)

By this point, I've been walking for 10-12 km, and because of antibiotics and
general post-procedure pain I already started feeling lightheaded. My original
plan was to keep going another 16 km to Regensburg after reaching my
destination, but I wasn't feeling up for that. Still, I did my best to enjoy
the rest of this hike, no matter the circumstances.

![Sports equipment in the forest on the way to Alteglofsheim](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-13.jpg)
![A fairy house on the Waldlehrpfad trail in the forest near Alteglofsheim](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-14.jpg)

I think this was by far the most interesting section of the hike. The road
towards Alteglofsheim goes through another forest, filled with local bikers and
hikers. I met quite a few of them, some even training on the equipment
installed along the trails, like pull-up bars, ladders, etc.

![A group of Minions in the forest near Alteglofsheim](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-15.jpg)
![A bee on the Waldlehrpfad trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-16.jpg)

Soon after, I reached the Waldlehrpfad - a local trail with educational signs
and a smaller Wichtelwald section with tiny houses the locals built for magical
creatures. It was really nice to see the decorations, presumably made by local
kids and their parent. It's always nice to let children get closer to nature.

![A tiny magical building on the Waldlehrpfad trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-17.jpg)

After this nice section, the town of Alteglofsheim begins. While it isn't too
exciting by itself, it houses [Schloß Alteglofsheim](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schloss_Alteglofsheim),
a high medieval castle that was transferred to the third Bavarian music academy
in June 1999. With beautiful ornaments and stucco ceilings (even some by
[Giovanni Battista Carlone](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Carlone_(Stuckateur))),
it's a great sight to behold if you can get inside. The park surrounding it was
also renovated in 2002, but at this time of the year it didn't look too special
to me.

![Schloß Alteglofsheim](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-18.jpg)

When I left the castle and started walking towards Köfering, I saw a wall of
rain on the horizon. Judging by the wind, I had about 30 minutes to cover the
full distance to the train station, so I hurried there despite the dizziness
and headache I was having. The whole area near the station is currently dug up
to build some new housing as I understood, so the road was terrible, but I
pushed through. As soon as I reached the Köfering station, the thunderstorm
reached me, and the rest of the time until my train arrived, I was just hiding
in the corner to not get soaked.

![Metal Tiranosaurus in Köfering](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/eggmuehl_koefering/2026-07-05-19.jpg)

Although I typically don't recommend these hikes to other people, since it's
just me walking through some settlements, this one felt much better. The
castles on the way looked great, the shady and chill forests helped against the
heat, and the calm rural views were great to look at. While not for everyone,
I'm sure this route can offer some of you the much needed respite in-between
work in a hustling and bustling city.
