Having climbed [Zugspitze (2962 m) the week before](/hikes/2025-06-21-zugspitze_reintal/),
I was eager to downscale my ambitions a bit. As part of my
["Hiking Web" project](/hikes/tag/Web/), I wanted to connect my previous walks
in the Munich area with Starnberg. I hoped this would be a relaxing walk to the
lake with a swim at the end.

![Construction work in the downtown area](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-00.jpg)

The route started at Munich's main train station, where I headed south on foot
through the city. As usual, everything was under construction, making
sightseeing pretty pointless.

![Alte Pfarrkirche St. Margaret](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-01.jpg)

Soon, the dense residential area gave way to smaller buildings and more
greenery. I passed through Sendlinger Wald park, going past locals enjoying the
day. I was surprised to see the neighborhoods having unique features among them.
You could easily tell when you entered a different district.

![St. Thomas Morus church](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-02.jpg)

I knew that most of the hike would be forest paths and agricultural land, so I
was getting impatient to leave the city behind. After crossing the A95 highway,
I finally reached the outskirts of Munich. The path led me through a mix of
fields and small woods of Forstenrieder Park.

![Industrial building on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-03.jpg)

The forest walk was refreshing at first, with birds chirping and a cool breeze.
I was quickly traversing the area under the shade of tall trees. However, the
bugs were relentless. I found myself swatting away flies and mosquitoes every
few minutes. It was a constant battle to keep them at bay.

![A bench in the forest](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-04.jpg)

For some unknown reason, I decided that the best way to reach Starnberg was to
follow the A95 walking on the side of the road adjacent to the highway. This
was a terrible mistake. With a pretty high speed limit and no sidewalk, I was
constantly on edge, dodging cars and trucks whizzing past me. The noise and
exhaust fumes made the walk unpleasant. I regretted not taking a more scenic
route through the countryside, with a gravel road running in parallel through
the forest.

![A field near Unterschorn](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-05.jpg)

Thankfully, after what felt like an eternity, I finally reached Unterschorn,
a small village along the way. From there, a paved asphalt sidewalk led me
safely away from the highway. I even walked near the Munich International
School, which looked like a nice campus. We had a similar school when I was a
kid in my hometown, but there it was a mediocre place for privileged kids to
hang out. I wondered if the Munich International School was any different.
At least [the campus is 95% carbon-neutral](https://www.mis-munich.de/) nowadays.

![A field near Starnberg](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/muenchen_starnberg/2025-06-29-06.jpg)

The last stretch of the hike went by quickly. I passed by a lady looking for
her child on a bike that got lost along the way. Unfortunately, I didn't see
the kid, but I hoped they found each other soon.

When I arrived at the lakefront in Starnberg, I was relieved to be done with
the hike. I was very thirsty, so I got a drink and headed for a swim. I also
messaged David to come join me, which he promptly did, coming by bike in less
than an hour.

To sum up, I wouldn't recommend this as a hiking route. The urban start and
the highway stretch were not enjoyable. If you want to explore the forests and
fields south of Munich, there are definitely better paths to take. At the very
least, I got a refreshing swim in the lake to end the day and got to join this
route with my previous [trip between Starnberg and Tutzing](/404/).
