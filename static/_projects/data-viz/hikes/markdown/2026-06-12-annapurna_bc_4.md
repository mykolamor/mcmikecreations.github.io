Now that I reached [the Annapurna Base Camp](/hikes/2026-06-11-annapurna_bc_3/)
the day before and spent an evening there, I was ready to start the descent
back to the valley. [Parlad Dharel](https://www.instagram.com/parlad_dharel/),
my guide, suggested to go as far as we can, so we can take our time going
slower the next day.

He also mentioned that the road between Chhomrong and Samrung has a small hot
spring, so I could take a bath there and enjoy mylself, given enough time.
This sounded like the best opportunity, but it meant I'd have to walk a lot
today, making me a bit anxious.

We took our morning photos with the only other active European group of
British blokes. There were stil some hopes to see the clouds move away and
reveal the mountains, but it was not to be. We discussed some cool routes
that professionals take to reach the top of Annapurna and other peaks, but
it boiled down to "it's too dangerous even for them so nobody does it".
The southern slope of Annapurna is especially bad in that regard, so there
was a very small number of attempts.

![Dmitry Sobolev / Anatoli Boukreev Chorten memorial](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-02.jpg)
![Mykola ready to leave the Annapurna Base Camp](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-03.jpg)

On the way out of the base camp, Parlad asked me for a testimonial video for
his services and their company in general. He was very accommodating and
helpful throughout the trip, so I was happy to do it. I talked for around a
minute without any script, but he seemed happy with the take and said we
were done with the formalities.

![View towards Gandharba Chull (6248 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-04.jpg)
![Meconopsis Napaulensis near the Annapurna Base Camp](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-05.jpg)

The road back was much better that the ascent. The whole route is not that
steep, and the road is quite sturdy despite the moisture and fog in the air,
so we could walk at a good pace. Although the clouds were still covering the
peaks, the morning sun gave an amazing light to the valley and the mountains,
making an unforgettable view. Parlad offered to take some videos for me, which
I ended up using for my compilation clips at the end of each post and on social
media.

![British blokes following us on the way down to Samrung](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-06.jpg)
![Nettle-Leaf Coneflower at the Machhapuchhre Base Camp](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-07.jpg)

We walked past the lake and the blooming flowers quite fast and reached the
Machhapuchhre Base Camp in no time. The view from there was getting better as
the clouds were slowly moving upwards, so we took one of the best photos of the
trip there. The valley was still covered in clouds, but without the thick fog
the views and the river were showing through.

![The view from the Machhapuchhre Base Camp towards the valley](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-08.jpg)
![The glacier waterfall hole on the way down](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-09.jpg)

From the MBC we descended the slightly steeper section towards Deurali (3200 m)
and walked past the glaciers and waterfalls I've first seen on the way up. By
that point the clouds were pretty high up, so the whole valley was visible and
the lush forests were showing in full glory. Although in the previous days we
barely saw any other hikers except for a few Indian groups, we started seeing
more and more people on the way down. The rain also subsided and the forecast
was looking good for the whole day, so we enjoyed the warmth while we could.

![View from Deurali (3200 m) towards the Machhapuchhre Base Camp](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-10.jpg)
![View at Deurali (3200 m) from below](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-11.jpg)

Next up was the section towards Himalaya past the Hinku Cave shrine. This time
around we were walking so fast that we skipped the shrine completely, but it
was still cool to look at. Given the distance we had to cover, we chose to take
photos only when taking breaks. Besides, I already had a lot of photos from the
way up with roughly the same weather conditions, so I didn't feel like taking
more.

![A cool stone with trees on top near the Himalaya village](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-12.jpg)

We didn't take any breaks in the Himalaya village either, but we did stop for a
while at the Pojenhyam Baraha Than Temple. On the way up the heavy thunderstorm
caught us right when we were coming up to this shrine, getting me and my phone
completely wet. This time around the weather was much better, so we could
actually take our time, pose for photos, enjoy the waterfall across the creek
and in general calm ourselves down.

![The Pojenhyam Baraha Than Temple near the Himalaya village](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-13.jpg)

From there on we walked past Dovan (2500 m) and Bamboo villages until we reached
Kuldhigar and took a small break there on the way to Sinuwa (2340 m). The
original plan that the tour operator offers is to spend the night in Bamboo or
Sinuwa (2340 m) depending on the hiker's pace, but with me being comfortable in
the mountains and Parlad being a very experienced guide, we decided to push all
the way to our original hotel in Chhomrong.

![The views at the opposing mountains near Kuldhigar](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-14.jpg)
![Approaching Sinuwa (2340 m) from above](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-15.jpg)

The section between Kuldhigar and Sinuwa (2340 m) was the "resting" part of the
trek. The way there is quite flat, with occasional hills to go up and down, so
I had plenty of time to look around without being too careful about my steps.
The views towards Pokhara and the valley were starting to peek from behind the
remaining mountains, which was also cool to see. The air was still very moist,
layering mist over the hills, but you could still make out the summits and the
small villages near them.

![A hiker resting in Sinuwa (2340 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-16.jpg)
![View from Sinuwa (2340 m) towards Annapurna](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-17.jpg)

Sinuwa itself is split into multiple semi-separated villages connected by
sherpas and donkeys. The main village is the higher one that I passed at that
point. From it, you can already see towards Chhomrong and further, but the path
is still long and winding. The lower village, on the other hand, is a bit more
timid and accessible, so it is more popular with tourists.

![View from Sinuwa (2340 m) towards Chhomrong](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-18.jpg)
![Dogs between Lower Sinuwa and Tilche](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-19.jpg)

From there only one village remains before reaching Chhomrong - Tilche. It's a
tiny settlement without tourist facilities, but it's surrounded by rice fields
and has locals working the soil there. After Tilche, the path goes down to the
bridge over the river and then up some more rice fields to the main houses of
Chhomrong.

![Gumba place of worship in Chhomrong](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-21.jpg)

We walked past some small shrines and a bigger place of worship. Parlad
explained to me that the smaller shrines are usually for locals who were loved
by the community or contributed to it before passing away. Their relatives can
come to the shrines to pray and remember the dead. The bigger one is a normal
Buddhist stupa, which is a place of worship for the whole (living) community.

![View from Chhomrong towards Annapurna South (7219 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-22.jpg)
![Compilation of the fourth day of the Annapurna Base Camp hike](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/annapurna_bc_4/2026-06-12-23.mp4)

All in all, despite the quick pace and the long distance covered, it was one of
the most memorable days of the trip. I got to experience all the best portions
of the hike all over again in one big package, and enjoy the views I normally
had my back turned to on the way up. The weather was also much better than on
the previous days, with only a small shover near the end, so I could take my
photos in peace without worrying about drying my phone in such a humid weather.
