The hike started similar to how the last one ended, by going through Omiš and
turning onto a rocky path towards Naklice. This time, my mom joined for the
hike, similar to how she attended the [Old Fort hike](/hikes/2025-08-13-omis_fort/).
The evening weather was hot, but bearable, with 34 degrees in shade and 37 to
40 in the sun. I checked the trail on [LigMapper](/404/)
and assured my mom that she could do it, since this route was around the same
difficulty as the Old Fort.

![The view at Omiš from the cliffside](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-03.jpg)

We walked for a while until we saw the first bench and took a break. Meanwhile,
I was showing my mom the views and pointing to our hike from last year. Taking
such breaks from time to time, we kept going up, breathing in the dry afternoon
air and walking on dry rocks and grass.

![An alcove chapel inside a cliff hole](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-08.jpg)

Around half-way up, we walked past a wayside shrine, specifically an alcove
chapel. It was in a small hole in the cliff wall. Earlier in the day, we were
asking around about guided tours to the Croatian [Blue Cave](https://blue-cave-tour.com/)
and were told it's just a small hole in the cliffside. Now we had a live
example of such a hole and it indeed wasn't impressive.

![The view from Naklice towards Cetinaand the highway](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-04.jpg)

When we came up to Naklice and walked past the mural from my [Čeline (455 m)](/hikes/2026-08-08-omis_celine/)
hike, we took a small detour to the benches nearby. It was a nice view of
Sveti Jure (1319 m) and the rest of the ridge across the Cetina river and the
local highway.

![The view from the hills towards Sveti Jure (1319 m)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-05.jpg)

We kept going through the village and soon reached the trail going off from the
village and towards the Lisičina stream. Along the way we found an abandoned
fig tree and took a few ripe fruits for the road up.

![The climb towards the Church of Our Lady of the Snows](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-06.jpg)

The climb to the Church of Our Lady of the Snows was quite rough for my mom, as
it has the steepest incline of this route. We took frequent breaks and drank a
lot of water, but eventually we made it up.

![The cross at the Church of Our Lady of the Snows](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-07.jpg)

The view at the top was quite rewarding, and the other hikers I saw last time
were nowhere to be seen. We first took some photos at the crossroads, then
walked the remaining few meters towards the church. The Croatian flag was
waving in the wind as usual, and the area was still tidy.

![View from the top towards Dugi Rat](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-09.jpg)

Behind the church, one can find a few planted spruce trees and benches in the
shade which we gladly used. More photos followed, and after a relaxing break
we decided to go down through the other half of the official trail, towards the
Duće village.

![A look back at the Church of Our Lady of the Snows](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-10.jpg)
![The view from the trail towards Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-11.jpg)

The official trail was surprisingly nice. The rocks on the path were manageable
and not too smooth, the benches put evenly throughout the route helped us rest,
and the views of the Brač island were great.

![The arch along the trail](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-12.jpg)

At some point we walked over a cool arch with flowers around it, but it wasn't
wasn't marked on the map, so I have no clue what it was. Despite the nice
route, the trail here doesn't offer any shade, so the evening sun blasts you
at full force.

![A sign on the way](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-13.jpg)

Although the official way goes all the way to Dugi Rat, we decided to take a
shortcut and walk down an abandoned overgrows trail bike path. The signs for it
were still there, but the shrubs and thorny bushes were a clear indicator
nobody took this route recently.

![The detour on top of a trail bike path](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-14.jpg)

We walked and sometimes slid down the surprisingly steep bike path and ended up
on an asphalt road. That's what we wanted, a mountain road taking us straight
back to our town. While we were walking it, discussing some everyday topics and
glazing at the panorama of Brač, we noticed some smoke here and there. We've
been seeing it for quite a few days already, and the firefighters seemed to be
handling it well, so we didn't pay it any mind. Once we got down to the main
roads near the beach, we got some drinks at the supermarket and went home.

![Views onto Duće from the asphalt road towards Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-15.jpg)

To sum up this route, it's quite great and accessible to beginners. The time
you see on the top of the page is slightly over the officially marked time, so
if you're not too experienced, that's what you can expect. I would highly
suggest not taking the bike path down though. First, you don't know if it is
not in use when you're there. Second, it has uncomfortable steep sections
inaccessible to beginners. My mom managed to stick a spruce twig through her
shoe and into her foot drawing blood while (controllably) sliding down there,
which could be avoided by taking a normal route.

![An abandoned building down in Duće](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-16.jpg)

When we got home, washed up and prepared to go to sleep, we started smelling
smoke. Since we were staying in the middle of the old town, surrounded by
restaurants with fancy kitchens, ovens, and barbecue grills, we assumed it was
nothing important and went to sleep. Several hours later, we were woken up by
the surrounding ruckus.

![The raging fires near Omiš. Credit: [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9342wn2x27o)](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-00.jpg)

People were running, yelling, the smell of smoke got worse. I checked Google
Maps and saw wildfire warnings all around us. The local government made an
evacuation effort on Facebook, setting up refuge points away from the fires
near the coast. Still, the fire was spreading rapidly and there was nothing
the locals could do to stop it.

![The leftover smoke wall around Omiš](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-02.jpg)
![The fire brigade plane in action](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-17.jpg)

Since the old town itself didn't seem in danger, we tried going back to sleep.
When we woke up in  the morning, the fires down at the coast subsided, and
the rest were still burning up the forest near the Old Fort, on cliffs
overhanging Omiš. The smell of smoke was still present and the sky was dark,
smoke covering the sun and all, but at least the people were safe. Our
windowsills and clothes hanging outside (very smart, I know) were covered in
ashes, but intact.

![The fire brigade planes dropping and gathering water](/images/projects/data-viz/hikes/stories/omis_duce/2026-08-13-01.mp4)

The firefighters called for support from bigger brigades, including several
planes with water tanks, helicopters, numerous trucks, etc. They were battling
the leftover fires until noon or even later. The town previously brimming with
life was reduced to a village where only locals stayed, while tourists took
busses and private cars to Split and further en masse. We followed the story
further while going about our daily vacation lives, and eventually the rescuers
won and the fire was extinguished.
